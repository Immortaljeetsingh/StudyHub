Unit 1: Sales Force Management -- Role and Scope

📖 Topic Overview

Sales management is the planning, direction, and control of the personal selling activities of a business unit, including recruiting, selecting, training, equipping, assigning, routing, supervising, paying, and motivating the sales force. It is the revenue-generating arm of the organization and a critical link between the company and its customers.

▶
Sales Force Management
▶
Sales vs Marketing
💡 Indian Business Example

Hindustan Unilever (HUL): HUL has one of the largest direct sales forces in India with over 80,000 sales personnel covering 8 million retail outlets across 600,000 villages. Their sales force is organized hierarchically: National Sales Manager, Regional Managers, Area Managers, Territory Sales Officers, and Beat Salesmen. This massive reach is how Surf Excel, Lifebuoy, and Wheel reach every corner of India.

⚠ Exam Tip

For 5 marks: Define sales management per AMA and list its 3 levels (top, middle, operational). For 10 marks: Explain the evolution of sales management from pre-industrial era to modern consultative selling. Always contrast selling vs marketing.

🧠 Memory Aid

Sales Management = "REMPACT" -- Recruiting, Equipping, Motivating, Paying, Assigning, Controlling, Training. Think: "REMPACT manages the sales force."

Key Concepts

  • AMA Definition: "The planning, direction, and control of the personal selling activities of a business unit."
  • Three Levels: Top-level (strategic planning), Middle-level (tactical coordination), First-level (operational supervision).
  • Sales vs Marketing: Selling is product-focused and short-term; marketing is customer-focused and long-term. Sales is a subset of marketing promotion.
  • Evolution: Pre-industrial (single person doing everything) to industrial era (separate sales departments) to modern era (integrated, consultative, tech-enabled).
  • Modern Role: Sales managers now participate in strategic planning, forecasting, budgeting, territory design, CRM management, and cross-functional coordination.
  • Scope: Includes sales force organization, recruitment, training, compensation, territory design, forecasting, evaluation, and channel coordination.

PRE-ASSEMP: Key Functions of Sales Manager

  • Planning -- Setting sales objectives, strategies, and budgets
  • Recruiting -- Attracting qualified candidates for sales positions
  • Equipping -- Providing tools, technology, and product knowledge
  • Assigning -- Allocating territories, accounts, and targets
  • Supervising -- Monitoring day-to-day field activities
  • Selecting -- Choosing the best candidates from the applicant pool
  • Evaluating -- Measuring performance against quotas and KPIs
  • Motivating -- Inspiring through incentives, recognition, and coaching
  • Paying -- Designing fair compensation plans (salary, commission, bonus)
⚠ Exam Focus

Evolution of Sales Management: Pre-industrial era (single person doing everything) to Industrial era (separate sales departments with order-takers) to Modern era (consultative, tech-enabled, customer-centric value creators). The shift is from "pushing products" to "solving problems." Modern sales managers participate in strategic planning, CRM management, data analytics, and cross-functional coordination with marketing, finance, and supply chain.

  • Three management levels: strategic (top), tactical (middle), operational (first-line)

Unit 2: Recruitment and Selection of Sales Force

📖 Topic Overview

Recruitment is the process of attracting a pool of qualified applicants for sales positions. Selection is the process of choosing the most suitable candidates from that pool. Effective recruitment and selection reduce turnover costs (which can be 1.5-2x annual salary) and ensure the sales force has the right skills, motivation, and cultural fit.

▶
Recruitment and Selection
▶
Interview Techniques
💡 Indian Business Example

ICICI Bank: ICICI Bank recruits thousands of sales officers annually for its retail banking products (credit cards, loans, insurance). They use a multi-stage process: aptitude tests, group discussions, role-play selling scenarios, and panel interviews. ICICI specifically targets MBA freshers from Tier 2 cities who understand local customer behaviour. Their retention rate improved by 25% after introducing realistic job previews during recruitment.

⚠ Exam Tip

For 5 marks: List the 5 steps in the recruitment-selection process. For 10 marks: Explain each step with Indian examples and discuss internal vs external recruitment sources. Philip Kotler's qualities of a good salesperson are a frequent sub-question.

🧠 Memory Aid

Recruitment = "SJRTF" -- Source candidates, Job analysis, Review applications, Test and interview, Final selection. Think: "So Jolly Recruiters Test Fast."

Recruitment Sources

  • Internal Sources: Promotions, transfers, employee referrals. Infosys fills 30% of sales roles internally. Advantages: lower cost, faster onboarding, morale boost.
  • External Sources: Job portals (Naukri, LinkedIn), campus recruitment, placement agencies, newspaper ads. HUL recruits from top B-schools (IIMs, XLRI) for management trainees.
  • Online Recruitment: LinkedIn has become the primary channel for sales hiring in India. Naukri.com processes over 50,000 sales job postings monthly.

Selection Process

  1. Application Screening: Filter resumes based on qualifications, experience, and skills. Use ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) for large volumes.
  2. Preliminary Interview: Phone or video screening to assess communication skills, motivation, and basic fit.
  3. Formal Interview: Structured or unstructured interviews. Use behavioural questions ("Tell me about a time you closed a difficult sale").
  4. Testing: Aptitude tests, personality assessments (Big Five), sales simulation exercises. Godrej uses psychometric testing for all sales hires.
  5. Reference Check and Offer: Verify background, contact references, and extend the offer letter.

Job Analysis Chain

Effective recruitment starts with a clear understanding of the job. The chain flows as follows:

  • Job Analysis: Systematic study of what the sales job involves -- duties, responsibilities, working conditions, reporting relationships.
  • Job Description: Written statement derived from job analysis. Lists duties, responsibilities, territory size, travel requirements, and reporting structure.
  • Job Specification: Qualifications, skills, traits, and experience needed. Example: "MBA preferred, 2+ years FMCG sales experience, fluent in regional language, willing to travel 80%."
  • Recruitment: Using the job specification to attract matching candidates from appropriate sources.
💡 Indian Business Example

Godrej Consumer Products: Godrej's sales job analysis revealed that the top 3 success traits for rural sales officers were: (1) ability to build relationships with kirana owners, (2) physical fitness for extensive travel, and (3) comfort with ambiguity in unstructured markets. They redesigned their job specification and interview process to test these traits. Result: 40% improvement in new hire retention within the first year.

Key Recruitment Formulas

  • Attrition Rate = (People Leaving / Total Employees) x 100
  • Recruitment Pool Size = Hires Needed / (Selection Rate x Offer Acceptance Rate)
💡 Numerical Example

Problem: A company needs to hire 50 salespeople. Their selection rate is 20% (1 in 5 applicants is selected) and offer acceptance rate is 80% (80% of offers are accepted). How many applicants do they need?

Solution: Recruitment Pool = 50 / (0.20 x 0.80) = 50 / 0.16 = 312 applicants needed

Qualities of a Good Salesperson (Kotler)

  • Empathy: Ability to understand the customer's perspective and feelings
  • Self-drive: Internal motivation and ambition to achieve targets
  • Resilience: Ability to handle rejection without losing enthusiasm
  • Communication: Clear, persuasive verbal and non-verbal skills
  • Product Knowledge: Deep understanding of features, advantages, and competitive positioning
  • Problem-Solving: Creative ability to match customer needs with product solutions

Unit 2 Summary

  • Recruitment: attracting qualified candidates; Selection: choosing the best fit
  • Job Analysis chain: Analysis, Description, Specification, Recruitment
  • Internal sources (promotions, referrals) vs external (portals, campus, agencies)
  • 5-step selection: screen, preliminary interview, formal interview, test, reference check
  • Key formulas: Attrition Rate, Recruitment Pool Size
  • Sales turnover costs 1.5-2x annual salary; better recruitment reduces attrition
  • Kotler's qualities: empathy, self-drive, resilience, communication, product knowledge, problem-solving

Unit 3: Training the Sales Force

📖 Topic Overview

Sales training is the process of developing selling skills, product knowledge, and customer relationship abilities in sales personnel. It covers orientation training for new hires, ongoing skill development for experienced reps, and management training for those moving into supervisory roles. Well-trained sales forces outperform untrained ones by 20-30% in revenue generation.

▶
Sales Training Methods
▶
ADDIE Training Model
💡 Indian Business Example

Maruti Suzuki: Maruti runs a comprehensive training program at its Manesar and Gurgaon facilities. New sales executives undergo a 45-day induction covering product specifications, customer handling, objection resolution, and CRM software usage. They practice role-play with trained actors simulating different customer types. Maruti also uses VR (virtual reality) for training salespeople on the Nexa premium experience. This investment has helped Maruti maintain 40%+ market share in India.

⚠ Exam Tip

For 5 marks: Define sales training and list 4 training methods. For 10 marks: Explain the ADDIE training model (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) with Indian examples. Kirkpatrick's 4-level evaluation is a frequent sub-question.

🧠 Memory Aid

Training = "ADDIE" -- Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation. Think: "ADDIE always trains well."

Training Methods

  • On-the-Job Training: Shadowing experienced reps, field training, mentorship. Eureka Forbes uses "buddy system" where new reps accompany senior salespeople for 2 weeks.
  • Classroom Training: Lectures, case studies, group discussions. TCS and Infosys use classroom sessions for product knowledge training.
  • Role Playing: Simulating selling situations. Salespeople practice handling objections, closing techniques, and negotiation. Bajaj Auto uses this extensively.
  • Online/E-Learning: Webinars, video courses, mobile learning apps. Byju's trains its sales force using digital modules accessible on smartphones.
  • Simulation: VR-based training for complex products. Maruti's Nexa showrooms use VR for premium customer experience training.

Kirkpatrick's 4 Levels of Evaluation

  1. Reaction: Did participants find the training useful? (Feedback forms, satisfaction surveys)
  2. Learning: Did they acquire the knowledge/skills? (Pre/post tests, quizzes)
  3. Behaviour: Are they applying it on the job? (Field observation, manager assessments)
  4. Results: Did it improve business outcomes? (Sales volume, conversion rates, customer satisfaction)

Sales Training Content Areas

Training AreaContentBest Method

Selling TechniquesProspecting, presentations, closing, objection handlingRole-playing + case studies

Training Process Cycle

The systematic training process follows four stages:

  1. Needs Analysis: Identify the gap between current and desired performance. Use sales data, field observations, and manager feedback. Example: If closing rates are 15% vs target of 30%, the training need is closing skills.
  2. Design: Define learning objectives, content structure, methods, and timeline. Select trainers (internal experts vs external consultants).
  3. Delivery: Conduct training using selected methods. Mix classroom with field training for best results. Include practice and feedback loops.
  4. Evaluation: Measure effectiveness using Kirkpatrick's 4 levels. Compare pre and post-training performance data.
⚠ Exam Tip

For 10 marks: Explain the complete training process with Indian examples. Compare on-the-job vs classroom methods. The training process cycle (Needs Analysis to Evaluation) is different from the ADDIE model -- ADDIE is a design framework, while the training process is an operational cycle. Know both.

Unit 3 Summary

  • Sales training develops product knowledge, selling skills, and customer relationship abilities
  • Training content areas: product knowledge, selling techniques, policies, territory, CRM, relationships
  • Methods: on-the-job, classroom, role-play, e-learning, simulation (VR)
  • Training process: Needs Analysis, Design, Delivery, Evaluation
  • ADDIE model provides systematic training design framework
  • Kirkpatrick's 4 levels: Reaction, Learning, Behaviour, Results

Unit 4: Compensation Plans for Sales Force

📖 Topic Overview

Sales compensation is the total package of monetary and non-monetary rewards provided to sales personnel in exchange for their contributions. The compensation plan must balance three objectives: attracting talent (competitiveness), motivating performance (incentive), and controlling costs (affordability). Common structures include straight salary, straight commission, and combination plans.

▶
Sales Compensation Plans
▶
Commission Structure
💡 Indian Business Example

LIC of India: LIC agents earn primarily through commissions (no fixed salary). New agents get 25% first-year commission on regular premium policies, reducing to 5% renewal commission in subsequent years. This structure motivates agents to constantly acquire new customers while maintaining existing ones. Top LIC agents earn Rs 50-100 lakhs annually. The commission-only model attracted over 13 lakh agents across India, making LIC the largest sales force in the country.

⚠ Exam Tip

For 5 marks: List 3 types of compensation plans (straight salary, straight commission, combination) and one advantage of each. For 10 marks: Compare all three plans with Indian examples and discuss when each is appropriate. Designing a compensation plan for a given scenario is a common case study question.

🧠 Memory Aid

Compensation = "SCB" -- Straight salary, Commission only, Both (combination). Think: "SCB pays the bills."

Compensation Plan Types

  • Straight Salary: Fixed monthly pay regardless of sales. Used when: long sales cycles, team selling, new markets. Example: IT sales reps at TCS and Infosys get fixed salary plus annual bonus.
  • Straight Commission: Pay based entirely on sales results (percentage of revenue). Used when: clear sales attribution, independent selling, real estate. Example: LIC agents, real estate brokers, insurance advisors.
  • Combination (Salary + Commission): Base salary plus performance incentive. Most common in India. Used when: balance of control and motivation needed. Example: HUL sales officers get Rs 25,000-40,000 base + 10-15% incentive on targets.
  • Bonus: Lump-sum payment for achieving specific targets. Diwali bonus is common in Indian sales organizations.
  • Non-Monetary Incentives: Recognition awards, foreign trips, club memberships. Maruti Suzuki offers top dealers Thailand trips; Samsung offers Europe trips to top performers.

Designing Compensation Plans

  • Step 1: Define sales objectives (volume, market share, new accounts, profitability).
  • Step 2: Determine the role of non-selling activities (service calls, collections, reports).
  • Step 3: Choose the mix (60:40 salary-commission is most common in India).
  • Step 4: Set quotas based on territory potential, not equal targets for all.
  • Step 5: Review and adjust annually based on market conditions.

Unit 4 Summary

  • Three plans: straight salary (stability), straight commission (motivation), combination (balance)
  • Combination (salary + commission) is the most widely used plan in Indian sales organizations
  • Non-monetary incentives (recognition, travel, awards) are powerful motivators in Indian culture
  • Compensation must align with sales objectives, territory potential, and competitive market rates

Unit 5: Territory Management

📖 Topic Overview

A sales territory is a group of customers or a geographical area assigned to a single salesperson. Territory management involves designing, assigning, and optimizing these territories to maximize coverage, minimize travel costs, and ensure fair workload distribution. Effective territory design improves both customer satisfaction and salesperson morale.

▶
Territory Management
▶
Sales Route Planning
💡 Indian Business Example

Coca-Cola India: Coca-Cola divides India into 5 major regions, then sub-divides into states, districts, and beats. Each beat is assigned to a delivery salesperson who visits a fixed set of retailers on a predetermined route. The territory design considers population density, outlet count, sales potential, and travel distance. In Mumbai alone, Coca-Cola has over 500 beats covering 200,000+ outlets. This beat system ensures "no retailer left behind."

⚠ Exam Tip

For 5 marks: Define territory management and list 3 bases for territory design (geographical, sales potential, workload). For 10 marks: Explain the workload method for territory design with a numerical example. Mention the benefits of territory management for both the company and the salesperson.

🧠 Memory Aid

Territory Design = "GSW" -- Geographical, Sales potential, Workload basis. Think: "Good Sales Work."

Bases for Territory Design

  • Geographical: Assign territories based on physical boundaries (state, district, pin code). Simple to manage. Used by FMCG companies like HUL and ITC.
  • Sales Potential: Divide customer base by revenue potential. High-potential territories get top performers. Used by B2B companies like IBM and Siemens.
  • Workload Method: Calculate total workload (calls needed) and divide by individual capacity. Most scientific approach.
  • Servicing Requirement: Accounts requiring more attention get dedicated territories. Used for key account management at TCS and Wipro.

Workload Method (Numerical Example)

  • Step 1: Total customers = 10,000 retailers in a district.
  • Step 2: Average calls per customer per month = 4 visits.
  • Step 3: Total calls per month = 10,000 x 4 = 40,000.
  • Step 4: One salesperson can make 20 calls per day x 25 working days = 500 calls/month.
  • Step 5: Sales force needed = 40,000 / 500 = 80 salespersons.

Benefits of Territory Management

  • For Company: Better market coverage, reduced overlap, improved sales forecasting, lower travel costs.
  • For Salesperson: Clear accountability, fair workload, performance evaluation, career growth path.
  • For Customer: Consistent contact person, faster service, stronger relationship.

Unit 5 Summary

  • Sales territory = geographical area or customer group assigned to one salesperson
  • Three bases: geographical, sales potential, workload method
  • Workload method is most scientific; calculates calls needed divided by individual capacity
  • Benefits: better coverage, fair evaluation, lower costs, stronger customer relationships

Unit 6: Sales Forecasting

📖 Topic Overview

Sales forecasting is the process of estimating future sales revenue over a specific time period (monthly, quarterly, annually). It is the foundation for all business planning -- production, inventory, staffing, budgeting, and strategic decisions. Accurate forecasting helps companies avoid stockouts, reduce overproduction, and optimize resource allocation.

▶
Sales Forecasting Methods
▶
Demand Forecasting
💡 Indian Business Example

Amul: Amul forecasts demand for 50+ lakh litres of milk daily across 18,000+ villages. They use a combination of historical sales data, seasonal patterns (higher demand during festivals like Diwali and Holi), weather forecasts, and real-time data from village-level dairy cooperatives. During summer, demand for ice cream and buttermilk increases by 30-40%. Amul's forecasting accuracy enables them to collect, process, and distribute fresh milk within 24 hours -- a logistical marvel.

⚠ Exam Tip

For 5 marks: Define sales forecasting and list 3 qualitative methods (jury, Delphi, sales force composite). For 10 marks: Compare qualitative vs quantitative methods with Indian examples. Time series analysis and regression analysis are common numerical questions.

🧠 Memory Aid

Forecasting = "QQT" -- Qualitative (expert opinion), Quantitative (data analysis), Time series (pattern-based). Think: "Quality Quantitative Trends."

Qualitative Methods (Judgment-Based)

  • Jury of Executive Opinion: Senior executives pool their estimates. Used for new product launches where no historical data exists. Tata Motors uses this for new car model forecasts.
  • Delphi Method: Iterative surveys of experts until consensus is reached. Used for long-range strategic forecasting.
  • Sales Force Composite: Individual salespersons estimate demand in their territories; these are aggregated upward. Relies on frontline market knowledge. Used by HUL, ITC, and Maruti.
  • Customer Survey: Directly asking customers about future purchase intentions. Used in B2B selling by companies like L&T and BHEL.

Quantitative Methods (Data-Based)

  • Time Series Analysis: Uses historical data to identify patterns (trend, seasonality, cyclical, random). Maruti uses 10-year sales data to predict monthly car demand.
  • Moving Average: Smooths out short-term fluctuations. 3-month moving average is used by FMCG companies for weekly forecasting.
  • Exponential Smoothing: Gives more weight to recent data. Used by e-commerce companies like Flipkart for demand prediction.
  • Regression Analysis: Identifies relationship between sales and other variables (GDP, population, advertising spend). Used by real estate companies to forecast housing demand.

Unit 6 Summary

  • Sales forecasting estimates future revenue; foundation for all business planning
  • Qualitative: jury opinion, Delphi, sales force composite, customer surveys
  • Quantitative: time series, moving average, exponential smoothing, regression
  • Best practice: combine both qualitative and quantitative methods for accuracy

Unit 7: Salesforce Automation (SFA)

📖 Topic Overview

Salesforce Automation (SFA) refers to the use of software and digital tools to automate and streamline sales activities such as contact management, lead tracking, order processing, reporting, and performance analysis. SFA enables salespeople to spend more time selling and less time on administrative tasks. Modern SFA integrates with CRM (Customer Relationship Management) systems for a complete customer view.

▶
CRM and Sales Automation
▶
Salesforce CRM Overview
💡 Indian Business Example

Asian Paints: Asian Paints equipped its 12,000+ sales force with tablet-based SFA tools. Salespeople can check real-time inventory, place orders, track deliveries, and access customer history on their devices. The system auto-generates route plans for dealer visits. This reduced order processing time from 3 days to 4 hours and improved dealer satisfaction scores by 35%. Asian Paints is considered a pioneer in Indian sales force automation.

⚠ Exam Tip

For 5 marks: Define SFA and list 4 components (contact management, lead tracking, order management, reporting). For 10 marks: Explain the benefits and challenges of SFA implementation in Indian companies with examples. CRM vs SFA distinction is a common sub-question.

🧠 Memory Aid

SFA = "CLOR" -- Contact management, Lead tracking, Order processing, Reporting. Think: "SFA is the CLOR to clean up sales."

SFA Components

  • Contact Management: Centralized database of all customer interactions. Salesforce, Zoho CRM, and HubSpot are popular in India.
  • Lead Management: Track leads from generation to conversion. Score leads based on engagement level. Helps prioritize high-value prospects.
  • Order Management: Automate order entry, pricing, discount approval, and delivery tracking. Asian Paints and HUL use this extensively.
  • Reporting and Analytics: Real-time dashboards showing sales performance, pipeline, conversion rates, and territory-wise results.
  • Mobile SFA: Smartphone-based tools for field sales. GPS tracking, photo capture of displays, and digital signatures for orders.

Benefits and Challenges

  • Benefits: Increased selling time (from 30% to 50% of workday), better data quality, faster decision-making, improved customer service, reduced paperwork.
  • Challenges in India: Resistance from salespeople ("too much monitoring"), poor internet connectivity in rural areas, high initial cost, training requirements, data privacy concerns.
  • Popular SFA Tools in India: Salesforce (enterprise), Zoho CRM (SMB), Freshsales (startups), LeadSquared (insurance/NBFC), Kylas (Indian alternative).

Unit 7 Summary

  • SFA automates contact management, lead tracking, order processing, and reporting
  • Asian Paints is India's pioneer in sales force automation with tablet-based tools
  • Benefits: more selling time, better data, faster decisions; Challenges: resistance, connectivity, cost
  • Popular tools: Salesforce, Zoho CRM, Freshsales, LeadSquared

Unit 8: Personal Selling Process

📖 Topic Overview

Personal selling is direct, face-to-face communication between a salesperson and a prospect for the purpose of making a sale. It is the most flexible and effective form of promotion because it allows two-way communication, immediate feedback, and relationship building. The personal selling process follows a systematic approach from prospecting to follow-up.

▶
Personal Selling Process
▶
Consultative Selling
💡 Indian Business Example

Eureka Forbes: Eureka Forbes pioneered direct selling in India with its Aquaguard water purifiers. Their salespeople follow a rigorous 7-step process: (1) Prospecting through referrals, (2) Pre-approach via phone call, (3) Approach with a free water testing kit, (4) Presentation showing water impurities, (5) Handling objections about price ("Is it worth Rs 15,000?"), (6) Closing with EMI options, (7) Follow-up for installation and service. Their 15,000+ direct sales force sells Rs 3,000 crore worth of products annually.

⚠ Exam Tip

The 7-step selling process is a guaranteed question. For 10 marks: Explain each step with an Indian example. For 5 marks: List the 7 steps and explain the most important one (objection handling is most commonly asked). AIDA model (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) is a common companion question.

🧠 Memory Aid

Personal Selling = "PPAPHC-F" -- Prospecting, Pre-approach, Approach, Presentation, Handling objections, Closing, Follow-up. Think: "Please Please Always Present, Handle, Close, Follow."

The 7-Step Personal Selling Process

  1. Prospecting: Identifying potential customers (leads). Sources: referrals, cold calling, trade shows, social media, website inquiries. Byju's generates leads through free scholarship tests.
  2. Pre-Approach: Gathering information about the prospect before meeting. Research company, needs, decision-makers, budget. B2B salespeople at IBM spend 30% of time in pre-approach research.
  3. Approach: First contact with the prospect. Make a good impression. Eureka Forbes uses "free water testing" as an approach strategy -- it's a service, not a sales pitch.
  4. Presentation: Demonstrating the product's features, advantages, and benefits (FAB). Use visual aids, demonstrations, and customer testimonials. Tata Motors uses test drives for car presentations.
  5. Handling Objections: Addressing prospect's concerns (price, quality, timing, competition). Techniques: feel-felt-found method, direct denial, compensation. This is the most critical skill for Indian salespeople.
  6. Closing: Asking for the order. Techniques: assumptive close, alternative close, urgency close ("This Diwali offer ends tomorrow"). HDFC Bank salespeople use the assumptive close: "Shall I start the documentation?"
  7. Follow-Up: Post-sale activities to ensure satisfaction and build relationships. Includes delivery confirmation, installation, training, and periodic check-ins.

AIDAS Theory of Selling

The AIDAS model explains the psychological stages a buyer goes through during a sales interaction. It is one of the oldest and most widely tested selling theories in marketing.

🧠 Memory Aid

AIDAS = "AIDA + S" -- Attention (grab), Interest (build), Desire (create), Action (close), Satisfaction (retain). Think: "AIDA gets the sale, S keeps the customer."

Behavioral Equation Model

This mathematical model by Howard explains purchase behaviour as a function of four variables:

  • B = P x D x K x V
  • B (Behaviour): The actual purchase decision
  • P (Predisposition): The buyer's attitude toward the product (influenced by brand image, past experience)
  • D (Drive): Internal motivation level (urgency of need, financial capacity)
  • K (Incentive): The perceived value of the reward (discount, bonus, freebie)
  • V (Habit): Past buying behaviour and brand loyalty

Indian Application: A loyal Amul customer (high V) who needs milk daily (high D) with a positive brand perception (high P) and a festival cashback offer (high K) will almost certainly make the purchase (high B).

The FAB method structures the sales presentation to move from product attributes to customer value:

AIDA Model for Sales Communication

AIDA is used for structuring sales letters, advertisements, and persuasive communications. Unlike AIDAS (which is for face-to-face selling), AIDA applies to written and mass communication:

  • A -- Attention: Grab the reader's attention with a powerful headline. Example: "Your electricity bill can be ZERO -- here's how." (Tata Power Solar ad)
  • I -- Interest: Build interest with relevant facts and benefits. Example: "Solar panels pay for themselves in 3 years and last 25 years."
  • D -- Desire: Create desire through testimonials, limited offers, and emotional appeal. Example: "Join 50,000 Mumbai homes already saving Rs 3,000/month."
  • A -- Action: Clear call to action. Example: "Call 1800-XXX-XXXX for a FREE home survey. Offer valid till Diwali."
⚠ Exam Tip

Unit 13: Negotiation Skills in Sales

📖 Topic Overview

Negotiation is a discussion between two or more parties to reach a mutually acceptable agreement. In sales, negotiation occurs when the buyer and seller need to agree on price, delivery, terms, or service levels. Effective negotiators create win-win outcomes rather than zero-sum games. Strong negotiation skills can mean the difference between a profitable deal and a loss-making one.

▶
BATNA and Win-Win Negotiation
💡 Indian Business Example

Infosys Contract Negotiations: When Infosys negotiates large IT outsourcing contracts (worth Rs 100-500 crore), the negotiation process can take 3-6 months. The sales team prepares by understanding the client's pain points, benchmarking competitor pricing, and identifying non-price variables (SLA terms, transition support, innovation credits). Their BATNA is strong because they have a pipeline of other large deals, which gives them confidence to walk away from unfavourable terms.

⚠ Exam Tip

For 5 marks: Define BATNA and explain why it is important in negotiation. For 10 marks: Explain the 5 negotiation styles (Thomas-Kilmann model) with Indian business examples. Win-win negotiation principles are a favourite essay question.

🧠 Memory Aid

Negotiation Stages = "POB-CI" -- Preparation, Opening, Bargaining, Closing, Implementation. Think: "POB-CI negotiates well."

BATNA -- Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement

  • Definition: Your best fallback option if the current negotiation fails. It is your "walk-away" position.
  • Why it matters: A strong BATNA gives you confidence and leverage. If you have no alternative, you are desperate and will accept bad terms.
  • Example: A Maruti dealer negotiating with a fleet company has BATNA = selling those cars to individual customers at higher margins. If the fleet deal does not meet minimum margin, the dealer walks away.
  • Improving BATNA: Generate multiple alternatives before negotiating. Never negotiate from a position of weakness.

Negotiation Process (5 Stages)

  1. Preparation: Research the counterpart, set goals, know your BATNA, understand their needs. Infosys spends weeks preparing for large deals.
  2. Opening: Set the tone, establish rapport, state positions. Indian business culture values relationship-building before getting into numbers.
  3. Bargaining: Exchange offers and counter-offers. Make concessions strategically -- never give something without getting something back.
  4. Closing: Summarize agreed terms, confirm commitment, document the deal. Use the assumptive close: "Shall we have the contract drafted?"
  5. Implementation: Fulfill obligations, maintain the relationship. Post-negotiation follow-up builds trust for future deals.

Negotiation Styles (Thomas-Kilmann Model)

CollaboratingWin-win, creative problem-solvingLong-term partnerships, complex dealsTCS-client outsourcing contracts

Win-Win Negotiation Principles

  • Focus on interests, not positions: Understand WHY they want something, not just WHAT they want.
  • Separate people from problems: Attack the issue, not the person. Maintain respect.
  • Generate options for mutual gain: Brainstorm creative solutions. "What if we reduce price but extend the contract to 3 years?"
  • Use objective criteria: Market data, industry standards, and benchmarks reduce conflict.
  • Know your walk-away point: Never negotiate below your minimum acceptable terms.

Break-Even Analysis (Numerical)

Break-even point is the sales volume at which total revenue equals total costs (no profit, no loss).

  • Break-Even Point (units) = Fixed Costs / (Selling Price per unit - Variable Cost per unit)
  • Break-Even Point (Rs) = Fixed Costs / Contribution Margin Ratio
  • Contribution Margin = Selling Price - Variable Cost per unit
  • Contribution Margin Ratio = Contribution Margin / Selling Price
💡 Numerical Example

Problem: A sales territory has fixed costs of Rs 5,00,000. Product selling price is Rs 500/unit. Variable cost is Rs 300/unit. Calculate break-even point.

Solution:

  • Contribution Margin = Rs 500 - Rs 300 = Rs 200/unit
  • Break-Even = Rs 5,00,000 / Rs 200 = 2,500 units
  • At 2,500 units, revenue = 2,500 x Rs 500 = Rs 12,50,000 = Total Cost
  • Sales beyond 2,500 units = pure profit of Rs 200/unit
