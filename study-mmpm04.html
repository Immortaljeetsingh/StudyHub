Unit 1: Introduction to International HRM

📖 Core Concepts
▶
International Human Resource Management
▶
International HR Planning

IHRM = HRM activities carried out across national borders, dealing with employees from multiple countries. More complex than domestic HRM due to multiple countries, cultures, and legal systems.

Key Definitions

TermDefinition
MNCCorporation operating in multiple countries with centralized management
TNCProduces, trades, and funds worldwide with dispersed operations
PCNParent Country National — resident of MNC's home country working abroad
HCNHost Country National — national of host country working for MNC
TCNThird Country National — citizen of country other than home or host

Six Factors Distinguishing IHRM from Domestic HRM

1-3: Scope & Environment
  • Wider nationalities — multiple races, cultures, backgrounds
  • Broader relationships — families, visas, permits, relocation
  • International environment influence — political, economic, legal differences
4-6: Operations & Approach
  • Enduring complexities — tax, compensation, benefits across borders
  • Additional functions — immigration, logistics, language training
  • Different approaches — culturally adapted HR practices
💡 Real-World Example

TCS operates in 46 countries with PCN managers from India, HCN staff locally, and occasionally TCN specialists.

⚠ Exam Tip

Clearly distinguish PCN, HCN, TCN with examples. This is a frequent short-answer question.

🧠 Memory Aid

"MHT-PCT" — MNC, Home/Host, TNC → PCN, HCN, TCN

✅ Unit 1 Summary

  • IHRM = HRM across national borders, more complex than domestic HRM
  • Key terms: MNC, TNC, PCN, HCN, TCN
  • Six distinguishing factors: wider nationalities, broader relationships, international environment, enduring complexities, additional functions, different approaches

Unit 2: Social & Cultural Context of IHRM

📖 Core Concepts
▶
Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions
▶
Trompenaars' Cultural Dimensions

Culture = collective programming of the mind distinguishing one group from another (Hofstede). Culture deeply impacts recruitment, compensation, motivation, and leadership styles.

Hofstede's 6 Cultural Dimensions

DimensionDescriptionHigh Score ExampleLow Score Example
Power DistanceAcceptance of unequal powerMalaysia (100)Denmark (18)
IndividualismSelf vs group focusUSA (91)Guatemala (6)
MasculinityAchievement vs caringJapan (95)Sweden (5)
Uncertainty AvoidanceComfort with ambiguityGreece (112)Singapore (8)
Long-term OrientationFuture vs present focusChina (87)USA (26)
IndulgenceGratification controlMexico (97)Egypt (4)

Trompenaars' 7 Cultural Dimensions

Values & Relationships
  • Universalism vs Particularism (rules vs relationships)
  • Individualism vs Communitarianism
  • Specific vs Diffuse (compartmentalized vs holistic)
  • Neutral vs Affective (emotional restraint vs expressiveness)
Status, Time & Control
  • Achievement vs Ascription (earned vs given status)
  • Sequential vs Synchronic (linear vs flexible time)
  • Internal vs External control (dominate vs adapt to environment)
💡 Real-World Example

India scores high on power distance (77) — hierarchical organizations work well. USA scores high on individualism (91) — individual performance-based rewards.

⚠ Exam Tip

Know Hofstede's 6 dimensions with country scores. Compare with Trompenaars in a table.

🧠 Memory Aid

"PIMULI" — Power distance, Individualism, Masculinity, Uncertainty, Long-term, Indulgence

✅ Unit 2 Summary

  • Culture = collective programming of the mind (Hofstede)
  • Hofstede's 6 Dimensions: PDI, IDV, MAS, UAI, LTO, IVR
  • Trompenaars' 7 Dimensions for cross-cultural analysis
  • Both frameworks essential for understanding IHRM context

Unit 3: Cross-Cultural Diversity

📖 Core Concepts
▶
Managing Cultural Diversity
▶
Cross-Cultural Communication

Cross-cultural diversity = differences in values, norms, beliefs across cultures in the workplace.

Challenges of Cultural Diversity

Communication & Work
  • Communication barriers (language, non-verbal differences)
  • Different work ethics and expectations
  • Conflict resolution styles vary
  • Decision-making approaches differ
Key Barrier
  • Ethnocentrism = belief in superiority of one's own culture (major barrier)
  • Resistance to different approaches
  • Stereotyping and prejudice

Managing Diversity

  • Cultural sensitivity training programs
  • Cross-functional cross-cultural teams
  • Inclusive diversity policies
  • Inclusive leadership development
  • Mentoring across cultures

Benefits of Diversity

  • Broader perspectives and viewpoints
  • Better problem-solving and creativity
  • Enhanced global market understanding
  • Improved decision-making quality
  • Competitive advantage in global markets
💡 Real-World Example

Infosys manages diversity across 46 countries with cultural sensitivity training and global mobility programs.

⚠ Exam Tip

Discuss both challenges AND benefits of diversity. Examiners want balanced answers.

🧠 Memory Aid

"CDE" — Cultural diversity, Differences, Ethnocentrism

✅ Unit 3 Summary

  • Cross-cultural diversity = differences in values, norms, beliefs
  • Challenges: communication barriers, ethnocentrism, different work ethics
  • Benefits: broader perspectives, better problem-solving, competitive advantage
  • Managing: cultural training, inclusive policies, cross-cultural teams

Strategic IHRM = aligning HR practices with international business strategy. Staffing approach must match the company's internationalization stage.

Perlmutter's EPRG Model

PolycentricEach subsidiary by HCNDecentralizedUnilever in local markets

Factors Affecting Staffing Strategy

  • Stage of internationalization
  • Cultural distance between countries
  • Availability of skilled local talent

Toyota uses ethnocentric approach for quality control (Japanese managers oversee manufacturing globally), but polycentric for sales (local managers understand local markets).

Unit 5: Staffing for International Assignments

📖 Core Concepts
▶
Expatriate Management
▶
Culture Shock Stages

International Recruitment Sources: Internal transfers, external hiring, third-country nationals. Expatriate = employee working in a country other than their home country.

Selection Criteria for Expatriates

Professional Competencies
  • Technical competence
  • Cross-cultural adaptability
  • Language skills
  • Leadership ability
Personal Factors
  • Family situation and spousal support
  • Motivation for international assignment
  • Emotional stability and resilience

Culture Shock Stages (U-Curve Model — Oberg)

📊 U-Curve of Culture Shock
1. Honeymoon
Fascination, excitement
→
2. Crisis
Frustration, anxiety
→
3. Adjustment
Adapting, learning
→
4. Mastery
Integration, comfort

Expatriate Failure

  • Premature return due to inability to adjust
  • Primary reasons: Family issues, cultural inability, lack of organizational support, career concerns
  • Cost: 2-3x annual salary per failed assignment
⭐ Key Concept

Inpatriation = transferring HCNs to HQ for corporate culture absorption (reverse of expatriation).

💡 Real-World Example

IBM's expatriate failure rate dropped from 25% to 5% after implementing pre-departure cultural training.

🧠 Memory Aid

"HCAM" — Honeymoon, Crisis, Adjustment, Mastery

✅ Unit 5 Summary

  • Selection criteria: technical competence, cultural adaptability, family situation, language, motivation, emotional stability
  • Culture shock stages: Honeymoon → Crisis → Adjustment → Mastery
  • Expatriate failure cost: 2-3x annual salary
  • Inpatriation = reverse of expatriation (HCN to HQ)

Unit 7: International Performance Management

📖 Core Concepts
▶
Performance Management vs Appraisal
▶
Appraising International Managers

Performance appraisal across borders faces unique challenges due to cultural and contextual differences.

Challenges in International Performance Appraisal

  • Different performance standards across countries
  • Cultural bias in evaluation (halo effect, leniency)
  • Distance and communication barriers
  • Different goals for different subsidiaries
  • Varying environmental conditions

Performance Criteria

Objective Measures
  • Sales, profits, market share, ROI
Subjective Measures
  • Leadership, teamwork, adaptability, cultural sensitivity

Who Should Evaluate?

EvaluatorAdvantageDisadvantage
Host country managerLocal knowledge, daily observationMay not understand HQ perspective
Home country managerStrategic alignmentDistance, limited visibility
Self-evaluationSelf-awarenessBias, inflation
360-degree feedbackComprehensiveComplex, time-consuming
💡 Real-World Example

GE uses 360-degree feedback globally but adjusts weightings based on cultural context.

🧠 Memory Aid

"HSC-360" — Host, Self, Country manager → 360-degree feedback

✅ Unit 7 Summary

  • Challenges: different standards, cultural bias, distance, varying goals
  • Criteria: objective (sales, ROI) + subjective (leadership, adaptability)
  • Evaluators: host manager, home manager, self, 360-degree feedback
  • Best practice: multiple evaluators, separate technical/contextual measures, regular feedback

Unit 9: Internal Career Management

📖 Core Concepts
▶
International Career Management
▶
Expatriation vs Repatriation

International assignments must be managed as part of long-term career development, not isolated events.

International Career Paths

Mobility Paths
  • Expatriate assignments — parent to host country
  • Inpatriate assignments — host to parent country
  • Lateral transfers across regions
Development
  • Global leadership pipeline — systematic development of global leaders

Career Development Challenges

  • Repatriation = returning expatriate to home country
    • Reverse culture shock
    • Job reintegration difficulties
    • Loss of autonomy and status
    • "Out of sight, out of mind" problem
  • Limited visibility to HQ during assignment
  • Career plateau after return

Best Practices

  • Mentoring programs during international assignment
  • Repatriation planning from day one
  • Career tracking systems for expatriates
  • Clear career path communication before departure
  • Knowledge transfer mechanisms
💡 Real-World Example

Procter & Gamble assigns a "home sponsor" to every expatriate to maintain career visibility.

🧠 Memory Aid

"EIG" — Expatriate, Inpatriate, Global pipeline

✅ Unit 9 Summary

  • Career paths: expatriate, inpatriate, lateral transfers, global leadership pipeline
  • Repatriation challenges: reverse culture shock, job reintegration, loss of status
  • Best practices: mentoring from day one, career tracking, knowledge transfer

Unit 13: IHRM Trends & Challenges

📖 Core Concepts
▶
HR Trends 2024
▶
AI in HR

IHRM is rapidly evolving due to technology, globalization, and changing workforce expectations.

Current Trends

Workforce & Technology
  • Globalization — increased cross-border mobility
  • Remote work — virtual teams, digital nomads
  • Technology-driven HR — HRIS, AI in recruitment, people analytics
  • Gig economy — flexible work arrangements
People & Values
  • DEI — Diversity, Equity & Inclusion initiatives
  • Employee well-being — mental health, work-life balance
  • Sustainability & CSR in HR policies

Key Challenges

  • Managing global talent pipeline
  • Compliance with multiple legal frameworks (GDPR, labor laws)
  • Balancing global consistency with local adaptation
  • Data privacy across jurisdictions
  • Succession planning for global roles
  • Retaining talent in competitive global market

Ethical Issues

  • Fair wages across countries (living wage vs market rate)
  • Working conditions in developing countries
  • Discrimination and harassment policies
  • Whistleblower protection across jurisdictions
💡 Real-World Example

COVID-19 accelerated remote work globally, creating new IHRM challenges for managing distributed teams across time zones.

🧠 Memory Aid

"GDR-TS" — Globalization, Diversity, Remote work → Technology, Sustainability

✅ Unit 13 Summary

  • Trends: globalization, remote work, DEI, technology-driven HR, gig economy, well-being
  • Challenges: talent pipeline, legal compliance, global-local balance, data privacy
  • Ethical issues: fair wages, working conditions, discrimination, whistleblower protection
  • Future: AI in HR, employee experience, agile HR, sustainability
