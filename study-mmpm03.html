Unit 1: Product Concept & Product Mix Decisions

📖 Core Concept
▶
Product Concept & Levels
▶
Product Mix Decisions

A Product is anything offered to a market for attention, acquisition, use, or consumption. Theodore Levitt: "Products are almost always a combination of the tangible and the intangible." A product is a complex cluster of value satisfactions.

Kotler's 5 Product Levels

📊 The 5 Product Levels (Core to Potential)
Level 1: Core Benefit
→
Level 2: Basic Product
→
Level 3: Expected Product
→
Level 4: Augmented Product
→
Level 5: Potential Product

Product Classification

🛒 Consumer Goods
  • Convenience: Frequent, low effort (Parle-G, Colgate)
  • Shopping: Comparison on quality/price (Allen Solly, Godrej Furniture)
  • Specialty: Brand loyalty, unique (Rolex, BMW, Tanishq)
  • Unsought: Not actively sought (LIC, Amway)
🏭 Industrial Goods
  • Materials & Parts: Raw materials, components
  • Capital Items: Installations, equipment
  • Supplies & Services: Maintenance, operating supplies

Product Mix Dimensions

DimensionMeasuresExample (Amul)
WidthNumber of product linesButter, Cheese, Milk, Ice Cream, Chocolate
LengthTotal items across lines200+ ice cream SKUs
DepthVariants per productButter: 10g, 100g, 500g, 1kg
ConsistencyRelatedness of linesAll dairy-based = high consistency

Line Stretching

  • Downward: Premium to lower (Surf Excel → Wheel)
  • Upward: Entry to premium (Lifebuoy → premium liquid handwash)
  • Two-way: Both directions (LG: Rs 6,000 economy to Rs 1,55,000 premium)
🧠 Memory Aid

"CB-EPAP" — Core, Basic, Expected, Augmented, Potential (Kotler's 5 Levels). "WLDC" — Width, Length, Depth, Consistency.

💡 Indian Example

Tata Group: Wide product mix (cars, IT, steel, tea). Each Tata brand has dedicated product managers managing the product lifecycle end-to-end.

⚠ Exam Tip

Always draw Kotler's 5 levels as a concentric circle diagram. Explain each level with examples. Product Manager concept originated at P&G in 1931 (McElroy Memo).

✅ Unit 1 Summary

  • Kotler's 5 levels: Core → Basic → Expected → Augmented → Potential
  • Products classified as Consumer (CSSU) and Industrial (MCS) goods
  • Product Mix: Width, Length, Depth, Consistency
  • Line stretching: downward, upward, two-way

Unit 2: Product Line Decisions

📖 Core Concept
▶
Product Line Decisions
▶
Product Line Length

Product Line = group of closely related products. Product line length is optimal when adding an item increases total profit.

Product Line Strategies

⬇ Stretching
  • Upward: Entering premium (Toyota → Lexus)
  • Downward: Entering lower-end (Mercedes → A-Class)
  • Two-way: Both ends (Maruti: Alto + Ciaz)
⚙ Other Strategies
  • Filling: Adding items within current range
  • Modernization: Updating technology/features
  • Featuring: Highlighting specific items
  • Pruning: Removing weak items (HUL: 110+ → ~30 power brands)

Line Extension: Advantages vs Disadvantages

Advantages (7)Disadvantages (6)
Consumer variety-seekingWeaker line logic
Increased shelf spaceLower brand loyalty
Pricing breadthUnderexploited ideas
Short-term sales gainsStagnant category demand
Competitive defensePoorer trade relations
Capacity utilizationMore competitor opportunities
Full line offering—
🧠 Memory Aid

"S-F-M-F-P" — Stretching, Filling, Modernization, Featuring, Pruning.

💡 Indian Example

Samsung: Stretches phones upward (Galaxy S Ultra), mid-range (Galaxy A), budget (Galaxy M) simultaneously.

⚠ Exam Tip

Line extensions rarely expand total category demand — they mainly redistribute market share. Mention cannibalization risk.

✅ Unit 2 Summary

  • Strategies: Stretching, Filling, Modernization, Featuring, Pruning
  • Extensions have 7 advantages and 6 disadvantages
  • Successful pruning (HUL, Titan) keeps portfolios profitable

🧠 Memory Aid

"IGMD" — Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline. "RCCTO" — Relative advantage, Compatibility, Complexity, Trialability, Observability.

💡 Indian Example

Jio (2016-2023): Introduction → free data (penetration pricing). Growth → 300M+ subscribers. Maturity → ARPU stabilization, JioFiber, JioMart.

⚠ Exam Tip

PLC must be understood at 3 levels: Product Class (longest), Product Form (most predictable), Brand (shortest). Always mention limitations.

✅ Unit 3 Summary

  • PLC: Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline
  • Each stage requires different product, price, promotion, distribution strategies
  • Rogers' 5 adopter categories and 5 adoption factors
  • PLC is useful as planning tool but has limitations

Unit 4: Product Strategy & New Product Planning

📖 Core Concept
▶
Product Launch Strategy
▶
Test Marketing

Ansoff's Growth Matrix defines four growth strategies. ~80-90% of new products fail due to poor market research, overestimating demand, or wrong positioning.

Ansoff's Growth Matrix

📊 Ansoff Matrix (2x2)
Market Penetration
Existing Products + Existing Markets
Product Development
New Products + Existing Markets
Market Development
Existing Products + New Markets
Diversification
New Products + New Markets

New Product Categories

  • New-to-the-world: True innovations (iPhone 2007)
  • New product lines: Tata entering telecom
  • Additions to existing lines: New Oreo flavor
  • Repositioning: Old Spice rebranding
  • Cost reductions: Cheaper manufacturing versions
🧠 Memory Aid

"MMPD" — Market penetration, Market development, Product development, Diversification.

💡 Indian Example

Google Glass failed (poor timing, privacy concerns). Maggi succeeded through market penetration (affordable pricing).

⚠ Exam Tip

Draw Ansoff Matrix as 2x2 grid. Give Indian examples for each quadrant. Discuss first-mover vs fast-follower.

✅ Unit 4 Summary

  • Ansoff Matrix: Penetration, Product Dev, Market Dev, Diversification
  • 5 new product categories with examples
  • ~80-90% new products fail; key reasons identified

Unit 12: Brand Architecture & Brand Extension

📖 Core Concept
▶
Warranties & Guarantees
▶
Service Quality

Brand Architecture = hierarchy of brands within a company. Brand Extension = using existing brand name for new product category.

Brand Architecture Strategies

StrategyDescriptionExample
Branded HouseSingle master brandGoogle, Virgin
House of BrandsSeparate brandsP&G (Tide, Pampers)
Endorsed BrandsParent endorses sub-brandsMarriott Bonvoy
Sub-brandsVariant within master brandiPhone Pro Max

Brand Extension Types

  • Line Extension: Same category, new variant (Coca-Cola Zero)
  • Category Extension: New category (Maruti insurance)
  • Co-branding: Two brands together (Nike + Apple)
🧠 Memory Aid

"BH-HE-ES" — Branded House, House of Brands, Endorsed Brands, Sub-brands.

💡 Indian Example

Tata successfully extends across salt, tea, cars, IT. Virgin extends to airlines, music, mobile.

⚠ Exam Tip

Brand architecture has 4 strategies — draw a comparison table. Always discuss risks of brand extension: brand dilution, cannibalization. Co-branding examples (Nike+Apple, Tata+Starbucks) score extra marks.

✅ Unit 12 Summary

  • 4 architecture strategies: Branded House, House of Brands, Endorsed, Sub-brands
  • Line vs Category extension with success factors and risks
  • Co-branding examples and benefits

Brand equity measurement approaches: Financial (price premium, royalty relief), Consumer-based (awareness, associations, loyalty), and Tracking studies.

Measurement Approaches

ApproachMethodsProsCons
FinancialPrice premium, royalty relief, stock marketObjective, comparableIgnores consumer perception
Consumer-basedAwareness, associations, loyalty metricsCaptures perceptionSubjective, hard to monetize
CombinedInterbrand model (financial + brand role + strength)Holistic viewComplex methodology

Brand Equity Models

  • Aaker's Brand Equity Ten: 10 measures across 5 categories
  • Keller's CBBE Model: 6 dimensions from Salience to Resonance
  • Interbrand Model: Financial + brand role + brand strength
  • Brand Tracking Studies: Ongoing monitoring of brand health metrics
🧠 Memory Aid

"F-C-T" — Financial, Consumer-based, Tracking.

💡 Indian Example

Interbrand valued Apple at $400+ billion, making it the world's most valuable brand.

⚠ Exam Tip

Compare financial vs consumer-based measurement approaches. Always mention Interbrand model for higher marks.

✅ Unit 15 Summary

  • 3 approaches: Financial, Consumer-based, Combined
  • Key models: Aaker's Ten, Keller's CBBE, Interbrand
  • Brand tracking studies for ongoing monitoring
