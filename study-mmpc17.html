Block-01: Corporate Level Strategy

Unit 1: Corporate Management — An Overview

📖 Core Concepts
  • Corporate Management has three dimensions: Planning + Implementation + Evaluation & Control
  • It evolved through 5 paradigm shifts from Adhocism to integrated Corporate Management
  • Corporate Planning (Drucker): "A continuous process of making entrepreneurial decisions systematically with the best possible knowledge of their futurity"
  • Corporate Planning (Hussey): "Not a technique, it is a complete way of running a business"
Corporate Planning: Continuous process of making entrepreneurial decisions systematically with the best possible knowledge of their futurity (Drucker)
Corporate Management: Encompasses corporate planning + implementation + evaluation & control of plans
📋 5 Paradigm Shifts (A-P-E-C-C)
  • Adhocism (till 1930) — reactive, exigency-driven decisions
  • Planned Policy — post Great Depression, anticipating contingencies
  • Environment-Strategy Interface — strategy copes with environmental forces
  • Corporate Planning — environmental appraisal to strategic alternatives
  • Corporate Management — includes implementation & control
📊 Corporate Planning Process Steps
1. Formulation of Strategic Intent
↓
2. Environmental Appraisal
↓
3. Generation of Strategic Alternatives
↓
4. Evaluation of Alternatives
↓
5. Decisions (Strategy, Policies, Programmes)
📋 Implementation Aspects
  • Project — specific initiatives with timelines
  • Procedural — standard operating procedures
  • Resource Allocation — budgeting methods: Strategic, Zero-Base, PLC-Based, BCG
  • Structural — organizational design
  • Behavioral — leadership, culture, motivation
  • Functional — marketing, finance, operations integration

Reliance Industries under Mukesh Ambani transformed from adhoc decision-making to systematic corporate management — planning Jio's entry into telecom years in advance, implementing through massive infrastructure investment, and evaluating performance through subscriber growth metrics.

  • Corporate Management = Planning + Implementation + Evaluation & Control
  • 5 paradigm shifts from Adhocism to integrated Corporate Management
  • Corporate Planning process: 5 steps from intent to decisions
  • Implementation aspects: Project, Procedural, Resource, Structural, Behavioral, Functional

Unit 2: Corporate Policy

📖 Core Concepts
  • Corporate policy = guidelines for decision-making at corporate level
  • Types of policies: Growth, Stability, Retrenchment, Combination
  • Policy formulation involves: mission, objectives, environmental analysis, strategy selection
  • Corporate governance: board responsibilities, transparency, accountability
Corporate Policy: A broad guideline for decision-making that helps managers make consistent choices aligned with organizational objectives
Growth Policy: Expanding operations through new products, markets, or acquisitions (e.g., Amazon expanding from books to everything)
Retrenchment Policy: Downsizing or divesting to cut losses (e.g., Tata Steel selling UK operations)
📋 4 Policy Types (G-S-R-C)
  • Growth — expanding through new products/markets/acquisitions
  • Stability — maintaining current operations without major changes
  • Retrenchment — downsizing or divesting to cut losses
  • Combination — mix of growth, stability, and retrenchment simultaneously
📊 Policy Formulation Process
1. Define Mission & Objectives
↓
2. Environmental Appraisal (SWOT/PESTLE)
↓
3. Generate Strategic Alternatives
↓
4. Evaluate (Risk-Return Tradeoff)
↓
5. Select & Communicate Policy
📋 Corporate Governance Dimensions
  • Board composition (independent directors)
  • Transparency in reporting
  • Accountability to stakeholders
  • Ethical conduct and compliance

Tata Group's corporate policy emphasizes "nation-building" — policies guide decisions like entering telecom (Tata Docomo), exiting steel UK operations, and investing in technology (Tata Elxsi). Each decision aligns with their overarching growth + stability policy framework.

Unit 3: Intensive Growth Strategies

📖 Core Concepts
  • 3 levels of strategy: Corporate ("What business?"), Competitive ("How to compete?"), Functional ("Department contribution?")
  • 4 Generic Corporate Strategies: Stability, Growth, Retrenchment, Combination
Corporate Strategy: Determines what businesses the firm should be in (portfolio decisions)
Competitive Strategy: How to compete in each business unit (Porter's generic strategies)
Diversification: Entering new markets with new products, either related or unrelated to existing business

📋 Ansoff's Grid Explained
  • Market Penetration (Least Risky): Existing products in existing markets. Increase purchase frequency, attract competitors' customers
  • Market Development: Existing products in new markets (new geographies or segments)
  • Product Development: New products for existing markets (requires R&D)
  • Diversification (Most Risky): New products for new markets. Related vs unrelated

Unit 4: Integration & Diversification Growth Strategies

📖 Core Concepts
Vertical Integration: Expanding operations forward (toward customers) or backward (toward suppliers) along the value chain
Horizontal Integration: Acquiring or merging with competitors at the same level of the value chain
Related Diversification: Entering new businesses that share common capabilities with existing operations
Unrelated Diversification: Entering entirely new industries with no connection to current businesses
📋 Vertical Integration
  • Backward Integration: Moving toward suppliers (e.g., manufacturer buying raw material supplier)
  • Forward Integration: Moving toward customers (e.g., manufacturer opening retail stores)
📋 Horizontal Integration
  • Same level of value chain — acquiring competitors
  • Example: Facebook acquiring Instagram
📋 Diversification Types
  • Related: Leverages existing capabilities (e.g., Tata Group across industries)
  • Unrelated/Conglomerate: New industries entirely (e.g., Reliance entering telecom from petrochemicals)
📋 Reasons for Diversification
  • Economies of scale & scope
  • Risk reduction through portfolio spread
  • Market dominance
  • Surplus resource utilization
📊 Integration Types
Backward Integration (toward suppliers)
↓
Horizontal Integration (same level)
↓
Forward Integration (toward customers)

Reliance Industries exemplifies all strategies — backward integration (owning oil fields), forward integration (Reliance Retail), horizontal integration (acquiring Hamleys), related diversification (Jio telecom from petrochemicals), and unrelated diversification (Jio Financial Services). Amazon also practices vertical integration by owning warehouses (backward) and delivery fleet (forward).

✅ Unit Summary

  • Vertical integration: Backward (suppliers) and Forward (customers)
  • Horizontal integration: Acquiring competitors at same level
  • Related diversification leverages existing capabilities; Unrelated enters new industries
  • Reasons: economies of scale/scope, risk reduction, market dominance, surplus resources

Block-02: International Strategy

Unit 5: Strategic Alliances

📖 Core Concepts
  • Cooperative agreements between firms to achieve shared objectives while remaining independent
  • Types: Joint Ventures, Equity Strategic Alliances, Non-equity Strategic Alliances, Consortia
Strategic Alliance: A cooperative agreement between two or more firms to achieve shared objectives while remaining independent organizations
Joint Venture: A new entity created by two or more parent companies combining resources, with shared ownership and risk
Equity Alliance: One partner purchases equity in the other, creating a financial bond
Non-equity Alliance: Partners share resources through contracts without creating a new entity

Starbucks-Tata Alliance: Starbucks entered India through a JV with Tata Global Beverages. Tata provided local market knowledge, real estate, and tea/coffee sourcing. Starbucks brought global brand, operations expertise, and coffee culture. The alliance preserved both companies' independence while achieving what neither could alone.

Unit 6: Internationalization Process

📖 Core Concepts
  • Firms internationalize to access markets, resources, reduce costs, or follow customers
Internationalization: The process of increasing involvement in international operations across country borders
MNC: A company that owns or controls production of goods or services in at least one country other than its home country
Transnational: A firm that combines global efficiency with local responsiveness, operating as an integrated network worldwide
Ethnocentric: A management philosophy where home-country practices are considered superior and applied globally
📊 5 Stages of Internationalization (D-F-J-M-T)
Stage 1: DOMESTIC — Ethnocentric, home market only
↓
Stage 2: FOREIGN — Export to foreign markets
↓
Stage 3: JOINT VENTURE — Physical operations abroad
↓
Stage 4: MULTINATIONAL — Multiple production facilities worldwide
↓
Stage 5: TRANSNATIONAL — Global efficiency + local responsiveness
📋 Advantages of MNCs
  • Superior technical know-how
  • Economies of scale
  • Lower input costs (bulk buying)
  • Access to overseas raw materials
  • Brand image & goodwill
  • Low-cost financing
  • Financial flexibility (shift to low-tax locations)
  • Information advantages
  • Risk offset through diversification
📋 Disadvantages of MNCs
  • Business risks (forex fluctuations)
  • Regulations in host country
  • Different legal systems
  • Political risks
  • Operational difficulties
  • Cultural differences
📋 Models of International Trade
  • Product Life Cycle Model (Vernon, 1966): New → Mature → Standardized
  • Uppsala Model: Progressive internationalization through stages
  • Transaction Cost Model: Internationalize when foreign transaction cost < internal cost
  • Eclectic Model (Dunning): OLI framework (Ownership, Location, Internalization)
▶
Internationalization
▶
MNC vs Transnational
💡 Real-World Example

Infosys followed D-F-J-M-T perfectly: started as a domestic IT company (Stage 1), began exporting software to US clients (Stage 2), formed JV partnerships and subsidiaries abroad (Stage 3), became a multinational with offices in 50+ countries (Stage 4), and now operates as a transnational with global delivery centers adapting to local markets (Stage 5).

🧠 Memory Aid

D-F-J-M-T = Domestic → Foreign → Joint Venture → Multinational → Transnational

🎯 Exam Tip

D-F-J-M-T is the most tested mnemonic in this subject. Know all 5 stages with examples.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 stages: Domestic → Foreign → JV → MNC → Transnational
  • MNC advantages: know-how, scale, low cost, brand, financing, risk offset
  • MNC risks: forex, regulations, politics, culture
  • Models: Vernon (PLC), Uppsala, Transaction Cost, Dunning's OLI

Unit 7: Market Risk Assessment

📖 Key Definitions
Political Risk: The risk of loss due to political instability, government policy changes, or expropriation in a host country
Forex Risk: The risk of financial loss due to fluctuations in exchange rates between currencies
Country Risk: The overall risk of investing in a particular country, encompassing political, economic, and legal factors
📋 5 Types of International Risks (P-E-C-F-L)
  • Political Risk — Government instability, policy changes, expropriation
  • Economic Risk — Inflation, exchange rate fluctuations, recession
  • Cultural Risk — Language barriers, business customs, consumer behavior
  • Currency/Forex Risk — Exchange rate volatility affecting profits
  • Legal/Regulatory Risk — Different laws, compliance requirements
📋 Risk Assessment Frameworks
  • Qualitative analysis (expert judgment)
  • Quantitative analysis (scoring models)
  • Comparative analysis (cross-country comparison)
📋 Risk Mitigation Strategies
  • Political risk insurance (MIGA, OPIC)
  • Hedging for currency risk (forwards, futures, options)
  • Joint ventures with local partners (share political risk)
  • Diversification across multiple countries
▶
International Risk
▶
Country Risk Analysis
💡 Real-World Example

Vodafone entered India (2007) and faced massive tax liability from India's retrospective tax law (2012) — a classic political/legal risk. Uber exited China (2016) due to regulatory and competitive risks. Samsung diversified manufacturing across Vietnam, India, and Indonesia to mitigate country-specific risks.

🧠 Memory Aid

P-E-C-F-L = Political, Economic, Cultural, Forex, Legal — 5 types of international market risks

🎯 Exam Tip

Know the 5 types of risks and give examples for each. Risk assessment questions often ask for mitigation strategies too.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 risk types: Political, Economic, Cultural, Forex, Legal
  • Assessment frameworks: Qualitative, Quantitative, Comparative
  • Mitigation: Political risk insurance, hedging, JVs with locals, diversification

Unit 8: Entry into International Markets

📖 Key Definitions
Licensing: An arrangement where a licensor grants rights to intangible property to a foreign licensee for a royalty fee
Franchising: A form of licensing where the franchisor provides a complete business system plus ongoing support
Wholly Owned Subsidiary (WOS): A foreign operation completely owned and controlled by the parent company
FDI: Investment in a foreign country that gives the investor significant management control

IKEA entered India through a wholly owned subsidiary (high control, high investment) to maintain its global brand standards. Block-03: Strategic Enablers

Unit 9: IT and Strategy

📖 Core Concepts
  • IT is strategically important due to: ubiquitous application, dramatic cost decline, universal ownership, exponential growth
IT Architecture: The organized set of rules and standards that guide the design and implementation of information systems in an organization
E-Business: Conducting business processes using the internet and digital technologies
Digital Transformation: The integration of digital technology into all areas of a business, fundamentally changing how it operates and delivers value
📊 Steps in IT Architecture Design
Business Vision → Business Objectives → Business Processes → Business Architecture → IT Architecture → IT Infrastructure
📊 Porter's Five Forces + IT Impact
ForceIT Impact
Supplier PowerIT reduces supplier power through e-procurement
Buyer PowerIT increases buyer power through price comparison
Threat of New EntrantsIT can be a barrier (high tech costs) or enabler (low entry with cloud)
Threat of SubstitutesIT creates new substitutes (streaming vs DVD)
RivalryIT intensifies competition through transparency
📋 Competitive Strategies via IT
  • Cost leadership (automation, efficiency)
  • Differentiation (unique digital services)
  • Cost focus (niche market efficiency)
  • Focused differentiation (premium digital experience)
📋 E-Business Models
  • B2B (Business-to-Business) — Alibaba
  • B2C (Business-to-Consumer) — Amazon
  • E-Market Places — online exchanges

Unit 10: Technology and R&D

📖 Core Concepts
  • Technology = R&D + Development (translating research into marketable products)
  • Need-push: Research driven by identified need
  • Technology-push: Research driven by new knowledge seeking application
R&D: Systematic activities combining basic and applied research to discover new knowledge and develop new applications
Need-Push R&D: Research driven by an identified market need or customer demand
Technology-Push R&D: Research driven by new scientific knowledge seeking commercial application
Technology Package: The combination of product design, production technique, and management systems that together form a firm's technological capability
📋 Technology Package (3 elements)
  • Product Design — what the product looks like and how it functions
  • Production Technique — how the product is manufactured
  • Management Systems — how operations are organized and controlled
📋 R&D Strategy Development
  • Project identification & selection is the most critical R&D activity
  • 7 pre-requisites for effective R&D strategy include: belief in strategy, planning staff, commitment
📋 Project Selection Criteria
  • Technical feasibility
  • Market potential
  • Resource availability
  • Strategic fit
  • Risk-return tradeoff
▶
R&D Strategy
▶
Technology Management
💡 Real-World Example

Tesla's R&D strategy is technology-push — battery technology innovations (from SpaceX materials science) drive new EV models. In contrast, Maruti Suzuki's R&D is need-push — developing CNG cars in response to India's fuel price concerns and environmental regulations.

🧠 Memory Aid

N-T-P-M = Need-push, Technology-push, Project selection, Technology Package — 4 R&D strategy pillars

🎯 Exam Tip

Know the difference between need-push and technology-push R&D. Questions often ask about R&D project selection criteria.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Technology = R&D + Development
  • Need-push vs Technology-push R&D
  • Project selection criteria: feasibility, market potential, resources, strategic fit, risk-return
  • Technology package: Product design + Production technique + Management systems

Unit 11: Knowledge Management (KM)

📖 Core Concepts
  • Knowledge = information + experience + context
  • Two types: Tacit (personal, hard to formalize) vs Explicit (codified, documented)
Knowledge Management: The process of creating, capturing, storing, sharing, and applying organizational knowledge to improve performance
Tacit Knowledge: Personal, context-specific knowledge that is hard to formalize and transfer (e.g., skills, intuitions)
Explicit Knowledge: Codified, documented knowledge that can be easily transmitted (e.g., manuals, databases)
SECI Model: Nonaka's model describing four modes of knowledge conversion between tacit and explicit forms
📊 SECI Knowledge Conversion Model
Socialization
Tacit → Tacit
(Training, mentoring, observation)
Externalization
Tacit → Explicit
(Documenting best practices, writing manuals)
Internalization
Explicit → Tacit
(Learning by doing, practice)
Combination
Explicit → Explicit
(Combining databases, reports)
📋 KM Cycle

Create → Capture → Store → Share → Apply

📋 KM Framework (5 Levels)
  • 1. Creation of Strategy for KMS
  • 2. Technical Infrastructure & Knowledge Resources
  • 3. Grouping of Knowledge
  • 4. Delivery of Knowledge
  • 5. Corporate & Individual Performance
📋 Benefits & Challenges of KM

Benefits: Reduced rework, Faster decision-making, Improved innovation, Competitive advantage

Challenges: Cultural resistance, Knowledge hoarding, Technology barriers, Measurement difficulties

Unit 12: Innovation

📖 Core Concepts
  • Invention = idea converted to practice (technical feasibility demonstrated)
  • Innovation = commercialization of invention (market success)
Innovation: The process of translating an idea or invention into a good or service that creates value for which customers will pay
Radical Innovation: Breakthrough changes that create new markets or significantly alter existing ones
Incremental Innovation: Small, continuous improvements to existing products, services, or processes
Disruptive Innovation: Innovation that creates new market by displacing established competitors (Christensen)
📋 Types of Innovation
  • Product innovation — new/improved product
  • Process innovation — new production method
  • Business model innovation — new way of doing business
  • Social innovation — solving social problems
📋 Innovation vs Invention
  • Invention = idea converted to practice (technical feasibility demonstrated)
  • Innovation = commercialization of invention (market success)
  • Innovation = Invention + Commercialization

  • Entry modes: Export (low risk) → License → Franchise → JV → Subsidiary (high risk)
  • IT architecture: Vision → Objectives → Processes → Business Arch → IT Arch → Infrastructure
  • R&D: Need-push vs Technology-push
  • Tacit knowledge = personal, hard to formalize
  • Explicit knowledge = codified, documented
  • SECI: Socialization (T→T), Externalization (T→E), Combination (E→E), Internalization (E→T)
  • Innovation = Invention + Commercialization
