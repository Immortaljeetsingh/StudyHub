Q1. Define sales management. Explain the evolution and modern scope of a sales manager. (10 marks)

Answer: According to the American Marketing Association, sales management is "the planning, direction, and control of the personal selling activities of a business unit." It includes recruiting, selecting, training, equipping, assigning, routing, supervising, paying, and motivating the sales force - summarized by the PRE-ASSEMP framework (Planning, Recruiting, Equipping, Assigning, Supervising, Selecting, Evaluating, Motivating, Paying). Sales management operates at three levels: top-level strategic planning, middle-level tactical coordination, and first-level operational supervision. Selling has evolved from the pre-industrial era, when a single person performed every function, through the industrial era of separate sales departments and order-takers, to the modern era of consultative, technology-enabled selling focused on solving customer problems. Today's sales manager is not merely a supervisor: the role covers strategic planning, forecasting, budgeting, territory design, CRM management, and coordination with marketing, finance, and supply chain. Selling is product-focused and short-term, while marketing is customer-focused and long-term; sales is the revenue-generating arm that links the company to its customers. A good example is HUL, whose 80,000-plus sales personnel reach 8 million retail outlets across 600,000 villages.

  • A good example is HUL, whose 80,000-plus sales personnel reach 8 million retail outlets across 600,000 villages.

Q2. Explain the seven steps of the personal selling process with examples. (10 marks)

Answer: Personal selling is direct, face-to-face communication with a prospect to make a sale, and it follows seven systematic steps. (1) Prospecting - identifying potential customers through referrals, cold calling, or trade shows; Byju's generates leads through free scholarship tests. (2) Pre-approach - researching the prospect before meeting, including company, needs, decision-makers, and budget. (3) Approach - making the first contact and a good impression; Eureka Forbes offers a free water-testing kit rather than a sales pitch. (4) Presentation - demonstrating features, advantages, and benefits (FAB); Tata Motors uses test drives. (5) Handling objections - addressing concerns about price, quality, or timing using techniques like feel-felt-found; this is the most critical skill for Indian salespeople. (6) Closing - asking for the order through assumptive, alternative, or urgency closes; HDFC Bank executives ask "Shall I start the documentation?" (7) Follow-up - ensuring satisfaction through delivery confirmation, installation, and periodic check-ins. The AIDAS model - Attention, Interest, Desire, Action, Satisfaction - describes the buyer's psychological journey through these stages, and Eureka Forbes's 15,000-plus direct sellers, selling Rs 3,000 crore worth of products annually, exemplify the complete process in India.

Key points:

  • Personal selling is direct, face-to-face communication with a prospect to make a sale, and it follows seven systematic steps.
  • (4) Presentation - demonstrating features, advantages, and benefits (FAB); Tata Motors uses test drives.
  • (1) Prospecting - identifying potential customers through referrals, cold calling, or trade shows; Byju's generates leads through free scholarship tests.

Q3. What is BATNA? Describe the stages of negotiation and the five negotiation styles. (10 marks)

Answer: BATNA stands for Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement - the negotiator's best fallback option if the current negotiation fails. A strong BATNA provides confidence and leverage, while a weak one forces acceptance of unfavourable terms. For example, a Maruti dealer negotiating with a fleet buyer has a BATNA of selling those cars to individual customers at higher margins. The negotiation process has five stages: preparation (research, goals, and BATNA), opening (rapport and positions), bargaining (offers and counter-offers with strategic concessions), closing (summarizing terms and documentation), and implementation (fulfilling obligations and maintaining the relationship). The Thomas-Kilmann model identifies five styles: competing (win-lose, used in government L1 tenders), collaborating (win-win problem-solving, used by TCS in long-term outsourcing contracts), compromising (splitting the difference, common in distributor margin talks), avoiding (postponing, useful in emotional situations), and accommodating (yielding to preserve relationships). Win-win negotiation focuses on interests rather than positions, separates people from problems, and uses objective criteria. Infosys, which prepares for three to six months on large contracts, demonstrates collaborative negotiation built on a strong BATNA and long-term partnership value.

Key points:

  • BATNA stands for Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement - the negotiator's best fallback option if the current negotiation fails.
  • For example, a Maruti dealer negotiating with a fleet buyer has a BATNA of selling those cars to individual customers at higher margins.
  • A strong BATNA provides confidence and leverage, while a weak one forces acceptance of unfavourable terms.

Q4. Compare qualitative and quantitative methods of sales forecasting with examples. (10 marks)

Answer: Sales forecasting estimates future revenue and is the foundation of production, inventory, staffing, and budgeting decisions. Qualitative (judgment-based) methods are used when historical data is limited, such as for new products: the jury of executive opinion pools senior managers' estimates, as Tata Motors does for new car models; the Delphi method uses iterative expert surveys until consensus; the sales force composite aggregates territory-level estimates from frontline salespeople, used by HUL, ITC, and Maruti; and customer surveys directly ask buyers about purchase intentions, common in B2B selling at L&T and BHEL. Quantitative (data-based) methods use historical data: time series analysis identifies trend, seasonal, cyclical, and random patterns - Maruti uses ten years of sales data to predict monthly demand; the moving average smooths short-term fluctuations; exponential smoothing weights recent data more heavily, as Flipkart does for demand prediction; and regression analysis relates sales to variables such as GDP or advertising spend. Best practice combines both approaches: Amul forecasts demand for 50 lakh litres of milk daily using historical data, festival seasonality (30-40 per cent higher ice-cream demand in summer), and real-time village cooperative data, enabling collection and distribution within 24 hours.
