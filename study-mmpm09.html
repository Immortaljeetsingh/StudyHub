Unit 1: An Overview of Retail Sector

📖 Definition

Retailing = sale of goods/services to end customer for personal consumption. Retailers break bulk from manufacturers into small quantities. India's retail = 8-10% of GDP, 2nd largest employer after agriculture. Unorganized sector = 88% (12 million+ small retailers). Brick and Click = combining physical stores with online presence for omnichannel.

📊 Indian Retail Market Facts
Market Size~$1.2 trillion
Unorganized Share88%
GDP Contribution8-10%
Employment Rank2nd after agriculture
Average Consumer Age27 years
💡 Indian Example

Modern players: Reliance Retail, DMart, Shoppers Stop, Trent (Tata). Foreign: IKEA, H&M, Zara. Growth drivers: Young demographics (avg age 27), credit friendliness, working women, low organized penetration, technology adoption.

🧠 Memory Aid

YCWO-TECT: Young, Credit, Working women, Organized penetration, Tech, Entry of corporates, Consumerism, Tier 2/3 expansion

✅ Unit Summary

  • Retailing = final leg of distribution to end consumer
  • 88% unorganized, $1.2T market, 8-10% of GDP
  • DMart success = EDLP + owned real estate + efficient supply chain

Unit 2: Concepts of Retailing

📖 Definition

Wheel of Retailing: New formats enter as low-price, low-margin, low-status → upgrade over time → become vulnerable to new low-price entrants. Merchandising = product assortment planning + visual displays. Planograms = shelf space allocation diagrams.

📊 Store Layout Types — GFLH
GridParallel aisles, structured flow → Supermarkets
Free-flowNo fixed aisles, open space → Boutiques
Loop (Racetrack)Guided path through store → Malls
HerringboneMain aisle with angled sides → Electronics
💡 Indian Example

IKEA's loop layout guides customers through entire store, maximizing exposure. Private labels: DMart, Reliance, BigBasket — higher margins (25-30% vs 15-20% for national brands).

🧠 Memory Aid

GFLH: Grid, Free-flow, Loop, Herringbone — 4 store layout types

✅ Unit Summary

  • Wheel of Retailing: low entry → upgrade → vulnerable
  • Store layouts: GFLH (Grid, Free-flow, Loop, Herringbone)
  • Supply chain tools: ECR, QR, VMI, Cross-docking

Unit 3: Retail Environment

📖 Definition

Shrinkage = inventory loss from theft, damage, or errors. India's rate: 2.38% (~INR 3,470 crores). Shoplifting = 47.6%, Staff theft = 25.5%. Greentailing = environmentally friendly retailing.

📊 Shrinkage Prevention — STAFF
Surveillance (CCTV)
↓
Technology (RFID, EAS)
↓
Audits + Fingerprinting + Friendly staff
💡 Indian Example

Security types — GPBC: General liability, Property, Business interruption, Cyber liability. Big Bazaar's "Green Day" promotes reusable bags.

🧠 Memory Aid

STAFF: Surveillance, Technology, Audits, Fingerprinting, Friendly staff

✅ Unit Summary

  • Shrinkage: 2.38% in India, shoplifting = 47.6%
  • Security types: GPBC (General, Property, Business, Cyber)
  • Greentailing: eco-friendly practices as competitive advantage

Unit 4: Strategic Retail Planning Process

📖 Definition

5 steps: (1) Define business mission, (2) Conduct SWOT, (3) Set objectives, (4) Develop strategy, (5) Implement and control. Indian retail evolved: Pre-1990s kirana → 1990s organized → 2010s e-commerce → 2020s omnichannel.

💡 Indian Example

Reliance Retail's strategy: enter with Jio ecosystem → acquire brands (Hamleys, Netmeds) → build omnichannel with JioMart.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 planning steps: Mission → SWOT → Objectives → Strategy → Implement
  • Indian evolution: kirana → organized → e-commerce → omnichannel

Unit 5: Models of Retailing

📖 Definition

7 models: Department Store (Shoppers Stop), Supermarket (DMart), Hypermarket (Reliance Smart), Specialty (Tanishq), Convenience (24Seven), Discount (DMart), Warehouse Club (Costco).

💡 Indian Example

DMart = Discount Store model with EDLP + owned properties = cost advantage.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Compare on 4 criteria: size, assortment, pricing, target customer
  • DMart = discount model, Shoppers Stop = department, Costco = warehouse

Unit 6: Based on Ownership

📖 Definition

5 formats: Independent (kirana), Chain (DMart), Franchise (McDonald's), Leased Department, Cooperative (Amul). Franchise types: Product/Trade Name, Business Format.

💡 Indian Example

Amul cooperative: 3.6M milk producers → village cooperatives → district unions → GCMMF → national distribution.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Franchise vs chain = shared control+high scalability vs full control+high investment
  • Amul = world's largest cooperative model

Unit 8: Retail Location Strategy

📖 Definition

Location = MOST important retail decision. Types: Free-standing, Shopping Center, CBD, Neighborhood. Trading areas: Primary (60-70%), Secondary (20-30%), Tertiary (remaining).

📊 Location Factors — TACTICAL
Traffic + Accessibility + Competition
↓
Trade area + Investment + Cost
↓
Appearance + Legal
💡 Indian Example

Starbucks selects locations using data analytics — foot traffic, office density, income levels. Huff's Gravity Model: Attraction = Floor Space / Distance².

🧠 Memory Aid

TACTICAL: Traffic, Access, Competition, Trade area, Investment, Cost, Appearance, Legal

✅ Unit Summary

  • Location is highest cost, lowest flexibility decision
  • Huff's Model: Attraction = Floor Space / Distance²
  • Trading areas: Primary 60-70%, Secondary 20-30%, Tertiary rest

Unit 9: Product Mix and Merchandise Strategy

📖 Definition

Width = number of product lines. Depth = varieties within each line. Private labels = store brands with higher margins (25-30%). OTB = Planned Sales + Markdowns + End Inventory − Begin Inventory.

💡 Indian Example

DMart's private label "D Mart" products are 10-20% cheaper than national brands with comparable quality.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Width vs depth: number of lines vs varieties per line
  • Private labels growing in India: DMart, Reliance, BigBasket

Unit 10: Retail Pricing Strategy

📖 Definition

7 strategies: EDLP, High-Low, Psychological (₹999), Bundle, Penetration, Skimming, Loss Leader. EDLP = consistently low (DMart). HLP = regular high, promotional low (department stores).

📊 EDLP vs HLP
EDLPStable prices, builds trust, lower marketing cost, Walmart/DMart
HLPFrequent promotions, creates excitement, higher cost, Shoppers Stop
💡 Indian Example

DMart's EDLP: no sales, no coupons — just consistently low prices. Reduces marketing costs and builds customer trust.

✅ Unit Summary

  • EDLP vs HLP is a guaranteed exam question
  • Give pros/cons of each with Indian examples

Unit 13: Managing Store Operations

📖 Definition

7 components: Opening/Closing, Cash, Inventory, Customer service, Housekeeping, Staff scheduling, Loss prevention. Decompression zone = first 5-15 feet where customers adjust.

📊 Key Formula
GMROI = Gross Margin / Avg Inventory Cost
↓
Profit generated per rupee invested in inventory
💡 Indian Example

Costco places essentials (milk, eggs) at the back — forcing customers to walk past high-margin items.

✅ Unit Summary

  • GMROI formula is a common numerical question
  • Store design: decompression zone, power wall, speed bumps, destination categories

Unit 14: Sourcing and Inventory Management

📖 Definition

EOQ = √(2DS/H) where D=demand, S=ordering cost, H=holding cost. Inventory Turnover = COGS/Avg Inventory. Days of Inventory = 365/Turnover. JIT = minimal inventory, frequent replenishment.

📊 Inventory Methods
JITMinimal inventory, frequent replenishment
EOQOptimal order size balancing costs
ABC AnalysisA=high value, B=medium, C=low
Safety StockBuffer against demand uncertainty
💡 Indian Example

Zara's fast fashion = JIT + vertical integration = new designs every 2 weeks with minimal unsold inventory.

✅ Unit Summary

  • EOQ = √(2DS/H), Inventory Turnover = COGS/Avg Inventory
  • Zara = JIT + vertical integration = fast fashion leader

Unit 15: Managing People and Processes

📖 Definition

Retail has 60-70% annual employee turnover. SOPs ensure consistency. Employee cycle: Recruit → Train → Evaluate → Compensate → Develop careers.

💡 Indian Example

McDonald's Hamburger University trains franchise operators on every aspect of store operations — ensuring global consistency.

✅ Unit Summary

  • High turnover (60-70%) is major retail challenge
  • SOPs: opening/closing, customer handling, inventory, cash, emergency

Unit 16: Customer Relationship Management

📖 Definition

CLV = Avg Purchase × Frequency × Lifespan. NPS = % Promoters − % Detractors. CRM components — CDPLS: Collect, Data analysis, Personalize, Loyalty, Service recovery.

📊 NPS Scale
Promoters (9-10): Loyal enthusiasts
↓
Passives (7-8): Satisfied but vulnerable
↓
Detractors (0-6): Unhappy, damage brand
💡 Indian Example

Amazon Prime: subscription loyalty that increases purchase frequency by 4x and doubles CLV vs non-Prime members.

🧠 Memory Aid

CDPLS: Collect, Data analysis, Personalize, Loyalty, Service recovery

✅ Unit Summary

  • CLV = Avg Purchase × Frequency × Lifespan
  • NPS = % Promoters − % Detractors
  • Retention is 5-7x cheaper than acquisition
