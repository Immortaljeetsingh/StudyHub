Unit 1: Marketing — An Overview

📖 Definition

Marketing is the process of planning and executing the conception, pricing, promotion, and distribution of ideas, goods, and services to create exchanges that satisfy individual and organizational objectives. — American Marketing Association

According to Philip Kotler, marketing is "the art and science of choosing target markets and getting, keeping, and growing customers through creating, delivering, and communicating superior customer value."

Core Marketing Concepts

🔄 Exchange Process

Marketing involves giving something of value in return for something else. Customers obtain a firm's product/service to satisfy needs in exchange for money.

⭐ Superior Customer Value

Difference between total benefits (tangible + intangible) and total costs. Value increases with quality and service; decreases with price, time, and effort.

Needs, Wants, and Demands

🎯 Key Distinction
  • Needs: State of felt deprivation — physiological (food, shelter), social (belonging), individual (knowledge, self-expression)
  • Wants: Form needs take when shaped by culture and personality. A person in India needs food but wants dal-chawal or biryani
  • Demands: Wants backed by purchasing power. An MBA aspirant wants IIM but may only be able to afford a regional B-school
🧠 Mnemonic: 5 Consumer Need Categories

S-R-U-D-S — "Smart Readers Understand Deep Subjects"

  • Stated needs — explicitly told ("I want a phone under ₹15,000")
  • Real needs — specific requirements (good camera, long battery)
  • Unstated needs — expected but not voiced (warranty, after-sales)
  • D
  • Secret needs — reluctant to admit (status symbol, peer approval)

The 4As Framework (India-Relevant)

Developed by Jagdish Sheth, more relevant for emerging markets like India:

Attraction

Product → What attracts the customer. Hindustan Unilever's Surf Excel: "Daag Acche Hain" attracts mothers emotionally.

Affordability

Price → What is affordable. Amul's pricing strategy: quality butter at ₹50/100g vs imported brands at ₹200+.

Accessibility

Place → Where it's available. Coca-Cola: available in 25 lakh+ retail outlets across India, even in remote villages.

Awareness

Promotion → How customer learns about it. Jio's ₹0 tariff announcement created mass awareness through word-of-mouth.

Hindustan Unilever (HUL): HUL reaches 8 million+ retail outlets across India. Its "Project Shakti" empowered 136,000+ rural women as micro-entrepreneurs, combining social impact with market penetration — a textbook marketing success combining all 4Ps and 4As simultaneously.

💡 Indian Example

Amul's Marketing: The "Taste of India" campaign is one of the longest-running brand campaigns globally. Amul uses topical advertising (current events in cartoon form), affordable pricing for mass markets, cooperative distribution through 75+ cold chains, and promotion through social media — reaching 1 billion+ impressions annually.

Case Study: Patanjali's Marketing Disruption

Patanjali disrupted Indian FMCG by combining Product (Ayurvedic/herbal positioning), Price (10-30% cheaper than HUL), Place (5,000+ Swadeshi Kendras + general trade), and Promotion (Baba Ramdev's personal brand + nationalism narrative). Within 5 years, it reached ₹30,000 crore revenue — proving that a fundamentally different approach to the 4Ps can disrupt even entrenched multinationals.

Case Study: Swiggy vs Zomato — Marketing Mix Comparison

Product: Both offer food delivery, but Swiggy expanded to groceries (Instamart) and pick-up/delivery (Swiggy Genie). Zomato focused on dining out and Gold membership. Price: Swiggy uses dynamic delivery fees; Zomato offers flat-rate Zomato Gold. Place: Both cover 500+ cities, but Swiggy leads in Tier-2 penetration. Promotion: Zomato's witty Twitter presence vs Swiggy's performance marketing approach. Both spend ₹1,000+ crore annually on marketing.

Exam Tip

In exams, always use Indian examples for each concept. Examiners reward students who connect theory to practice. For the 4Ps, prepare at least 2 Indian company examples per P. For the 4As framework, use emerging market examples — this is what distinguishes a top answer.

Mnemonic: Marketing Orientation Evolution

Production → Selling → Societal → Marketing concepts in chronological order. Modern marketing evolved from "make what we can" to "understand what customers need."

Unit 2: Marketing Environment Analysis

📖 Definition

The marketing environment consists of all actors and forces outside marketing that affect the firm's ability to build and maintain successful customer relationships. It comprises the micro environment (close to the firm) and the macro environment (broader forces).

Micro Environment — Forces Close to the Company

🏢 Internal Environment
  • Company's resources, culture, structure
  • Top management vision and past decisions
  • Example: Tata Group's culture of trust shapes all marketing decisions
🤝 Marketing Intermediaries
  • Wholesalers, retailers, distributors
  • Transport and warehousing firms
  • Example: BigBasket, JioMart act as modern intermediaries
⚔️ Competitors
  • Direct (same product), indirect (substitute)
  • Budget, premium, and local competitors
  • Example: Patanjali vs HUL in FMCG
👥 Customers
  • Consumer, business, reseller, government, international
  • Current vs potential customers
  • Example: Maruti targets first-time car buyers in Tier-2/3 cities

Macro Environment — PESTEL Analysis

Environmental Scanning & SWOT

Systematic monitoring of the marketing environment using:

  • Competitive Intelligence: Tracking competitor moves — e.g., Reliance Jio monitoring Airtel's pricing
  • Market Research: Customer data and preferences through surveys, focus groups
  • Trend Analysis: Identifying emerging patterns — e.g., shift to organic food
  • SWOT Analysis: Internal Strengths/Weaknesses + External Opportunities/Threats

ITC Limited: ITC's environmental scanning revealed rural India's growing purchasing power. It launched "Aashirvaad Atta" targeted at health-conscious homemakers, leveraging its existing distribution network of FMCG products. Today, Aashirvaad is India's No.1 packaged atta brand with ₹6,000+ crore revenue — demonstrating how environmental analysis drives marketing success.

💡 Indian Example

Amul's PESTEL Response: When the Indian government promoted "Make in India" (Political), Amul expanded capacity. When health consciousness grew (Social), it launched low-fat variants. When digital payments surged (Technological), it enabled UPI at its parlours. This multi-factor response shows mastery of environmental analysis.

Key Takeaway: The marketing environment is dynamic. Indian firms that scan both micro and macro environments systematically — like ITC, HUL, and Tata — consistently outperform competitors. PESTEL analysis is the backbone of strategic marketing planning.

Case Study: Jio — Reading the Macro Environment

Reliance Jio's 2016 launch is a masterclass in environmental analysis. Political: Digital India push created favorable policy environment. Economic: Rising smartphone penetration but high data costs (₹250/GB). Social: Young population hungry for digital content. Technological: 4G infrastructure ready but underutilized. Environmental: Digital reduces paper/transport needs. Legal: TRAI's pro-consumer regulations enabled new entrants. Jio read ALL six factors correctly and launched with free data — gaining 100M subscribers in 170 days.

Case Study: Amul's PESTEL Mastery (Extended)

Political: Cooperative model aligned with govt's rural development agenda — received policy support. Economic: Affordable pricing during inflation kept demand stable. Social: Health-conscious consumers → launched low-fat milk, probiotic curd. Technological: AI-driven cattle feed optimization, blockchain for supply chain. Environmental: Solar-powered dairy plants, biogas from cow dung. Legal: FSSAI compliance turned into marketing advantage ("FSSAI certified").

Exam Tip

When asked about PESTEL analysis, always provide specific Indian examples for each factor. Generic answers score average marks. Show the interconnection between factors — e.g., how Political (Make in India) + Economic (rising middle class) + Technological (UPI) together created the conditions for digital payment marketing.

Mnemonic: SWOT Internal vs External

S-W = Internal (what we control) | O-T = External (what happens to us)

"Strengths and Weaknesses are Within us; Opportunities and Threats are Outside."

Bases of Market Segmentation

🌍 Geographic
  • Country, region, city size, climate, urban/rural
  • Example: Maruti Suzuki: Alto for rural India, Baleno for urban
👤 Demographic
  • Age, gender, income, education, family size, occupation
  • Example: L'Oréal targets different income segments with Garnier (mass) vs Lancôme (premium)
🧠 Psychographic
  • Lifestyle, personality, values, social class, attitudes
  • Example: Royal Enfield targets adventure-seeking, individualistic riders
⚡ Behavioral
  • Usage rate, brand loyalty, benefits sought, user status
  • Example: Amazon Prime targets heavy online shoppers with loyalty benefits

Criteria for Effective Segmentation (M-A-S-A)

🧠 Mnemonic: MASA

M-A-S-A — "My Aunt Sells Apples"

  • Measurable — Size and purchasing power can be quantified
  • Accessible — Can be reached through distribution and promotion
  • Substantial — Large and profitable enough to serve
  • Actionable — Effective programs can be designed for it

Nestlé India: Nestlé uses STP brilliantly. Maggi is segmented by occasion (students, working professionals, kids) and positioned as "2-minute" convenience food. Nescafé targets urban millennials with "It All Starts With a Nescafé." Cerelac targets young mothers with nutrition messaging. Each brand occupies a distinct position in consumers' minds.

Key Takeaway: STP is the foundation of all marketing strategy. Successful Indian companies like Nestlé, HUL, and Maruti segment precisely, target selectively, and position distinctly. The position must be clear, competitive, and communicable.

Case Study: Maruti Suzuki's STP in India

Segmentation: Geographic (urban vs rural), Demographic (income-based: ₹3-5L, ₹5-10L, ₹10L+), Psychographic (first-time buyer vs upgrader), Behavioral (mileage-seeker vs performance-seeker). Targeting: Differentiated strategy — Alto for budget segment, Swift for youth, Baleno for premium hatchback, Brezza for SUV seekers. Positioning: Alto = "Kitna Deti Hai?" (fuel efficiency), Swift = "You're the one" (youth), Baleno = "The New Face of Premium." Each car occupies a distinct mental slot.

Case Study: PhonePe's Segmentation Success

PhonePe segmented India's digital payment market by digital literacy (not just income). It recognized that 70% of India was new to digital payments. So it designed for simplicity — vernacular languages, visual cues, minimal text. It targeted tier-2/3 cities where Paytm was less focused. Positioning: "Safe, simple, in your language." Result: 450M+ registered users, 40%+ UPI market share.

Exam Tip

For STP questions, always follow the logical sequence: Segment first (with bases), then Target (with strategy type), then Position (with positioning statement format). Use the format: "For [target segment] who [need], [brand] is the [category] that [benefit] because [reason to believe]."

💡 Example: Buying a Car

Influences on Consumer Behavior

🌍 Cultural Factors
  • Culture: Most fundamental — Indian vegetarianism affects food marketing
  • Subculture: Regional, linguistic, religious groups
  • Social Class: Income, education, occupation influence buying
👥 Social Factors
  • Reference Groups: Aspirational groups influence brand choice
  • Family: Joint family vs nuclear family decisions
  • Roles & Status: Buying to match social position
👤 Personal Factors
  • Age & Life Cycle: Student → professional → retiree needs differ
  • Occupation: IT professional vs farmer
  • Lifestyle: AIO (Activities, Interests, Opinions)
🧠 Psychological Factors
  • Motivation: Maslow's hierarchy drives needs
  • Perception: Selective attention, distortion, retention
  • Learning: Experience changes behavior
  • Beliefs & Attitudes: "Indian brands are trustworthy"

Models of Consumer Behavior

Rational, utility-maximizing. Limited by incomplete information.

Unconscious motivations. Luxury goods = status anxiety.

Social influences and group membership shape behavior.

Britannia Industries: Britannia understood that Indian mothers (primary decision-makers) prioritize nutrition for children. It repositioned from "biscuit company" to "good food company," launching NutriChoice, Little Hearts, and cheese products. By mapping the consumer decision journey — from recognizing children's nutrition needs to post-purchase trust — Britannia grew to ₹14,000+ crore revenue.

🧠 Mnemonic: Cultural-Social-Personal-Psychological

C-S-P-P — "Customers Seek Personal Pleasure"

Culture → Social → Personal → Psychological factors, from broadest to most individual influence.

Key Takeaway: Consumer behavior is influenced by cultural, social, personal, and psychological factors. Indian marketers must understand the joint family system, regional diversity, and value-consciousness that make Indian consumers unique.

Case Study: Flipkart's Understanding of Indian Consumer Behavior

Flipkart discovered that Indian consumers had high perceived risk in online shopping (psychological factor). Solution: Cash on Delivery — 60% of early orders were COD. It addressed the cultural factor of "touch and feel" before buying by offering 30-day returns. The social factor of word-of-mouth was leveraged through referral bonuses. Personal factor: EMI options addressed income constraints. Understanding these Indian-specific behavioral nuances helped Flipkart build trust and reach $35B valuation.

Case Study: Mamaearth — Targeting the Conscious Indian Consumer

Mamaearth identified a psychographic shift: young Indian mothers (25-35) increasingly preferred toxin-free, natural products. The cultural factor of "ayurvedic/natural" trust was combined with modern social proof (influencer marketing). Personal factor: new mothers are highly involved in baby care purchases (extensive problem solving). Psychological factor: fear of harmful chemicals on babies drove the decision. Result: ₹1,500+ crore brand built purely on understanding consumer behavior.

Exam Tip

Consumer behavior questions often ask to "analyze the factors influencing purchase decision." Structure your answer using C-S-P-P (Cultural-Social-Personal-Psychological) with specific examples for each. Then connect to the decision-making process (5 stages). This comprehensive approach scores top marks.

Product Mix Decisions

Width

Number of product lines. HUL: Personal Care, Home Care, Foods, Refreshments — 4 lines wide.

Length

Total items across all lines. HUL has 50+ brands across categories.

Depth

Variations per product. Surf: Surf Excel, Surf Excel Quick Wash, Surf Excel Matic — 3 variants deep.

Consistency

How related the lines are. HUL: all FMCG — high consistency. Tata: salt to software — low consistency.

Dabur India: Dabur exemplifies product mix mastery. Its product width spans Healthcare (Dabur Honey, Chyawanprash), Personal Care (Vatika, Gulabari), and Foods (Real juice). The depth includes Dabur Honey in 250g, 500g, 1kg sizes. This mix strategy helped Dabur grow from a small Ayurvedic shop to a ₹11,000+ crore FMCG giant.

🧠 Mnemonic: PLC Stages

I-G-M-D — "I Grow Mature and Decline"

Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline. At each stage, sales, profit, competition, and strategy change predictably.

Key Takeaway: Product decisions span from core benefit identification to managing entire product portfolios. Understanding PLC helps marketers adapt strategy — India's electric vehicle market is currently transitioning from Introduction to Growth, requiring different approaches than mature markets like smartphones.

Case Study: Tata Motors — Product Mix Mastery

Tata Motors demonstrates all four product mix dimensions. Width: Passenger vehicles (Nexon, Harrier), Commercial vehicles (trucks, buses), Electric vehicles (Nexon EV, Tiago EV). Length: 15+ models across categories. Depth: Nexon comes in XE, XM, XZ, XZ+ variants, each in petrol/diesel/EV. Consistency: All under "Tata" brand, sharing platform engineering. The Nexon EV is currently in the Growth stage of PLC — sales doubling annually, competition growing (MG Comet, Mahindra XUV400).

Case Study: Paper Boat — Redefining Product Levels

Paper Boat (Hector Beverages) reimagined all five product levels for Indian traditional drinks. Core: Refreshment and nostalgia. Generic: Packaged fruit drinks. Expected: Hygienic, convenient, no preservatives. Augmented: Traditional recipes (Aam Panna, Jaljeera), eco-friendly packaging, emotional storytelling. Potential: Health supplements, regional recipe expansion. By focusing on the augmented product level (nostalgia + tradition), Paper Boat created a ₹500+ crore brand in a crowded beverage market.

Exam Tip

For PLC questions, always mention the strategic implications at each stage — not just the characteristics. For Introduction: heavy promotion, skimming/penetration pricing. For Growth: maximize share, improve quality. For Maturity: differentiate, defend share. For Decline: harvest, divest, or rejuvenate. Use Indian examples at each stage.

Brand Building: "Rivaah" line for wedding jewellery, "Mia" for working women. Today: ₹30,000+ crore revenue, India's largest jewellery brand.

Unit 7: Pricing Decisions

📖 Definition

Price is the amount of money charged for a product or service, or the sum of values consumers exchange for the benefits of having or using the product. It is the only marketing mix element that generates revenue — all others are costs.

Pricing Objectives

💰 Profit-Oriented

Maximize current profit or target return on investment. Example: Apple targets 40%+ margins on iPhones.

📦 Volume-Oriented

Maximize market share through low pricing. Example: Jio's free initial tariffs to capture 400M+ subscribers.

⚖️ Status Quo

Maintain existing prices to avoid price wars. Example: Coca-Cola and Pepsi maintain ₹10/200ml for years.

👑 Prestige Pricing

High prices to signal quality/luxury. Example: BMW never discounts to preserve brand image.

Pricing Approaches

ApproachMethodFormulaIndian Example
Cost-BasedMarkup on costPrice = Cost + Markup%Grocery stores: 15-30% markup on MRP
Demand-BasedValue perceived by customerPrice = Perceived ValueOla/Uber surge pricing during peak hours
Competition-BasedMatch or beat competitorsPrice = Competitor ± X%Airtel matches Jio's data plans

Pricing Strategies

🚀 Skimming

High initial price, gradual reduction. For innovative/premium products.

Example: Samsung Galaxy S series launches at ₹70,000+; drops to ₹45,000 after 6 months.

💥 Penetration

Low initial price to gain market share quickly.

Example: Jio launched with free voice/data — gained 100M subscribers in 170 days.

🧠 Psychological

Pricing that creates perception of value.

Example: ₹999 instead of ₹1,000. Paytm "cashback" creates perceived savings.

🎁 Promotional

Temporary price cuts to drive trial or volume.

Example: Big Billion Days (Flipkart), Prime Day (Amazon) — 60-80% discounts.

Other Strategies

  • Dynamic Pricing: Prices change by demand — Ola/Uber, airline tickets, hotel rooms
  • Freemium: Basic free, premium paid — Spotify, LinkedIn, Canva
  • Bundle Pricing: Multiple products at combined discount — McDonald's Happy Meal
  • Geographic Pricing: Different prices by region — milk costs more in Mumbai than in rural Maharashtra
  • Loss Leader: Sell below cost to attract customers — Amazon's Kindle sold at cost
🧠 Mnemonic: Pricing Strategies

S-P-P-P — "Some People Prefer Peanuts"

Skimming → Penetration → Psychological → Promotional — the four primary strategies.

Colgate-Palmolive India: Colgate uses a tiered pricing strategy. Colgate Dental Cream at ₹50 for mass market, Colgate Total at ₹150 for premium, and Colgate Vedshakti for herbal seekers at ₹80. This covers every income segment. During competitive threats from Patanjali Dant Kanti, Colgate launched Colgate Swarna Vedshakti at ₹10, creating a price umbrella that protected market share across all segments.

Key Takeaway: Price is the most flexible marketing mix element. Indian companies must balance value-consciousness (penetration for mass markets) with aspirational pricing (skimming for premium). The Jio case study remains the most dramatic example of penetration pricing globally.

Case Study: D-Mart — Everyday Low Pricing (EDLP)

D-Mart (Avenue Supermart) uses cost-based EDLP — no sales, no discounts, no loyalty cards. Instead: (1) Owns most stores (no rent = lower costs), (2) Buys directly from manufacturers in bulk, (3) Pays suppliers in 3-7 days (vs industry 30-60 days) for better rates, (4) Minimal store décor. Result: 15-20% cheaper than competitors. D-Mart's ₹2,00,000+ crore market cap proves that consistent low pricing beats promotional pricing for value-conscious Indian consumers.

Case Study: Starbucks India — Prestige Pricing Challenge

Starbucks entered India in 2012 with Tata at ₹200-400 per coffee. But India's ₹10-20 chai culture made this challenging. Strategy: (1) Value-based positioning — not selling coffee, but "third place" experience (work, socialize), (2) Location premium — only in malls, airports, business districts, (3) Menu adaptation — Tandoori Paneer sandwich, masala chai. Pricing remained premium but justified through experience. 300+ stores today, profitable since 2023.

Exam Tip

Pricing questions often ask to "compare strategies." Use the S-P-P-P mnemonic and provide ONE Indian example for each strategy. Always mention factors affecting pricing: costs, demand, competition, government regulation, brand positioning. The Jio example is mandatory for any penetration pricing answer.

Unit 8: Distribution Decisions

📖 Definition

Distribution channels are sets of interdependent organizations involved in making a product or service available for use or consumption. The channel bridges the gap between producers and consumers in terms of time, place, and possession.

Channel Members

🏭 Agents & Brokers

Facilitate transactions without taking ownership. Commission-based. Example: Real estate agents, insurance agents.

📦 Wholesalers

Buy in bulk from producers, sell to retailers. Example: FMCG distributors in India's traditional trade.

🏪 Retailers

Final link to consumers. Example: Reliance Fresh, D-Mart, local kirana stores.

🤝 Franchisees

Licensed to operate under brand name. Example: McDonald's, Subway, Lenskart franchisees.

Distribution Strategies

StrategyDescriptionControlCostIndian Example
IntensiveMaximum outlets possibleLowHighCoca-Cola: 25 lakh+ outlets in India
SelectiveSelected outlets in each areaMediumMediumSamsung phones: authorized dealers only
ExclusiveLimited, exclusive outletsHighLowApple: Apple Premium Resellers only

Channel Design Decisions

Channel Length:

  • Zero-level (Direct): Producer → Consumer. Example: Amul parlours, D2C brands (Mamaearth)
  • One-level: Producer → Retailer → Consumer. Example: BigBasket buys from farmers
  • Two-level: Producer → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer. Traditional Indian FMCG trade
  • Three-level: Producer → Agent → Wholesaler → Retailer → Consumer. Complex B2B

Logistics & Supply Chain Management

Key decisions: warehousing, inventory management, transportation, order processing

  • Warehousing: Centralized vs decentralized. Amazon has 60+ fulfillment centers in India
  • Transportation: Rail, road, air, water — India's road freight carries 65% of goods
  • Inventory: JIT (Just-In-Time) vs safety stock. Maruti pioneered JIT with local suppliers

Asian Paints: Asian Paints revolutionized distribution in India's paint industry. With 70,000+ dealers across 35,000+ locations, it reaches the remotest villages. Its color tinting machines at dealer points allow instant customization. The supply chain uses 25+ manufacturing plants and AI-driven demand forecasting to ensure dealers never run out of popular shades — achieving 99%+ order fulfillment rates.

💡 Indian Distribution Innovation

HUL's Project Shakti: To reach 600,000+ Indian villages, HUL created a network of 136,000+ Shakti Ammas — rural women who sell HUL products door-to-door. This combines social empowerment with distribution innovation, reaching consumers that traditional channels cannot.

Key Takeaway: Distribution is the "place" in the marketing mix. India's challenge is its vast geography and 65% rural population. Companies like HUL, Asian Paints, and Amul have innovated distribution models that reach every corner of India, creating competitive advantage through availability.

Case Study: Lenskart — Omnichannel Distribution

Lenskart disrupted India's eyewear market with a hybrid distribution model. Online: Website + app with virtual try-on (AR technology) — 50M+ website visits/month. Offline: 2,000+ stores across 250 cities with free eye check-ups. Home: "Lenskart at Home" — eye test + frame selection at your doorstep. B2B: Supplies to 10,000+ opticians. This zero-level to two-level channel mix ensures every customer touchpoint is covered. Revenue: ₹5,000+ crore.

Case Study: Meesho — Reimagining Distribution for Bharat

Meesho created a social commerce distribution model for India's 500M+ WhatsApp users. Instead of traditional channels: (1) Resellers (housewives, students) share product catalogs on WhatsApp/Facebook, (2) They earn commission on each sale, (3) Meesho handles logistics/delivery. This zero-inventory, zero-investment model reached 15M+ resellers and ₹500+ crore monthly GMV. It's the digital equivalent of HUL's Project Shakti — using social networks as distribution channels.

Exam Tip

Distribution questions should cover: (1) Channel strategy (intensive/selective/exclusive), (2) Channel length (zero to three-level), (3) Logistics (warehousing, transport, inventory). Always mention India-specific challenges: vast geography, rural penetration, kirana store dominance (85% of FMCG sales).

Unit 9: Promotion Decisions

📖 Definition

Promotion mix (marketing communications mix) is the specific blend of advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, public relations, and direct marketing tools the firm uses to persuasively communicate customer value and build customer relationships.

Promotion Mix Elements

📺 Advertising

Paid, non-personal communication through mass media. TV, print, radio, outdoor, digital.

Example: Surf Excel "Daag Acche Hain" — ₹500+ crore annual ad spend

🤝 Personal Selling

Face-to-face interaction with buyers. Most effective for complex/expensive products.

Example: Bajaj Allianz insurance agents, real estate sales teams

🎁 Sales Promotion

Short-term incentives to encourage purchase. Discounts, coupons, contests.

Example: "Buy 1 Get 1 Free" at McDonald's, scratch cards in Lay's packets

📰 Public Relations

Building good relations with stakeholders through favorable publicity, events.

Example: Tata's PR through CSR activities, ITC's e-Choupal initiative

Sales Promotion Tools

  • Consumer Promotions: Samples, coupons, cashback, contests. Example: Paytm cashback offers
  • Trade Promotions: Discounts to retailers, display allowances. Example: HUL gives retailer incentives
  • Business Promotions: Trade shows, conventions, sales contests. Example: Auto Expo

Push vs Pull Strategy

Push (Trade → Consumer)

Manufacturer pushes product through channel using trade promotions and personal selling.

Example: Patanjali used Ayurvedic doctors and Swadeshi Kendras to push products

Pull (Consumer ← Brand)

Manufacturer creates consumer demand through advertising, making consumers ask retailers.

Example: Coca-Cola spends heavily on TV/digital so consumers demand it at stores

Asian Paints: Asian Paints uses a balanced push-pull strategy. Push: training painters and dealers as brand ambassadors, offering display incentives. Pull: memorable TV campaigns like "Har Ghar Kuch Kehta Hai" and digital campaigns on social media. This combination made Asian Paints India's largest paint company with 50%+ market share in decorative paints.

🧠 Mnemonic: Promotion Mix

A-P-S-P-D — "All People Should Promote Diligently"

Advertising → Personal Selling → Sales Promotion → Public Relations → Direct Marketing

Key Takeaway: The promotion mix must be tailored to the product, market, and budget. Indian companies increasingly use integrated marketing communications (IMC) — combining TV, digital, PR, and sales promotion for consistent messaging. Digital marketing is now the fastest-growing promotion channel in India.

Case Study: boAt — Digital-First Promotion Strategy

boAt (audio electronics) built a ₹10,000+ crore brand with zero TV advertising. Its promotion mix: (1) Influencer Marketing: 500+ micro-influencers on Instagram/YouTube — "boAtheads" community. (2) Content Marketing: Lifestyle content (music, fitness, gaming) — not product specs. (3) Social Media: 3M+ Instagram followers, meme-driven engagement. (4) Event Sponsorship: IPL teams, music festivals. (5) Amazon/Flipkart Sales: Big Billion Days exclusives. Cost per acquisition: ₹200 (vs ₹2,000+ for traditional FMCG). Proves digital-first promotion works in India.

Case Study: Fevicol's Legendary Advertising

Fevicol (Pidilite) has the longest-running Indian ad campaign — 60+ years of "Fevicol ka jod." Key promotion lessons: (1) Consistency: Same core message across decades — "unbreakable bond." (2) Creativity: Humorous, memorable ads (bus ad, egg ad, fish ad). (3) Media Mix: TV (mass reach) + outdoor (billboards at construction sites) + digital (viral social content). (4) PR: Fevicol ads become news — earned media worth ₹100+ crore annually. (5) Sales Promotion: Fevicol workshops for carpenters — personal selling + loyalty. Result: 70%+ market share in adhesives.

Exam Tip

Promotion questions often ask about Push vs Pull strategy. Push = trade promotions + personal selling (push through channel). Pull = advertising + consumer demand (pull through channel). Use A-P-S-P-D mnemonic for promotion mix elements. Always mention IMC (Integrated Marketing Communications) — combining all tools for consistent messaging.

Service Characteristics — I.I.V.P.

🧠 Mnemonic: IIVP

I-I-V-P — "Intelligent Individuals Value Patience"

  • Inseparability — Produced and consumed simultaneously. Haircut, live concert.
  • Variability — Quality varies by provider. Two cab rides can be very different.

Zomato: Zomato transformed India's food service marketing. It addressed intangibility through user reviews and food photos. Inseparability was tackled via real-time order tracking. Variability was managed through restaurant ratings and quality checks. Perishability (empty restaurant tables) was solved by offering discounts during off-peak hours. Zomato's IPO at ₹1 lakh crore valuation proves the power of service marketing.

Key Takeaway: India's services sector contributes 55%+ of GDP. Understanding service characteristics (IIVP) and quality dimensions (RATER) is critical. Indian companies like Zomato, Ola, and Practo have innovated solutions to inherent service marketing challenges.

Case Study: Ola — Solving Service Marketing Challenges

Ola tackled all four IIVP challenges in Indian ride-hailing. Intangibility: Driver ratings, car photos, fare estimates BEFORE booking. Inseparability: Real-time GPS tracking, in-ride safety features. Variability: Driver training programs, "Ola Select" for premium rides with verified drivers. Perishability: Dynamic pricing (surge) to balance supply-demand; "Ride Later" for advance booking. Additionally, Ola introduced Ola Money (fintech) and Ola Electric — extending service ecosystem. IPO at $7B+ valuation.

Case Study: Practo — Healthcare Service Marketing

Practo solved healthcare's unique service challenges. Intangibility: Doctor profiles with qualifications, experience, patient reviews, consultation fees — all before booking. Inseparability: Telemedicine solved the "patient must be present" constraint. Variability: Verified doctor credentials, standardized consultation process. Perishability: Online booking reduces empty appointment slots. RATER dimensions: Reliability (accurate doctor info), Assurance (verified credentials), Tangibles (clean clinic photos), Empathy (patient reviews), Responsiveness (instant booking confirmation). 25M+ monthly users.

Exam Tip

Service marketing questions MUST cover IIVP (4 characteristics) with examples. Use RATER for service quality questions. Compare retail formats table is frequently asked — memorize at least 5 formats with Indian examples. For B2B vs B2C, focus on the differences table — it's a guaranteed 5-mark question.

Unit 11: B2B & International Marketing

📖 Definitions

B2B marketing involves selling products/services to other businesses for use in their production processes or for resale. International marketing involves operating across national boundaries, adapting to different cultures, legal systems, and economic conditions.

B2B vs B2C Marketing

ExampleTata Steel selling to auto companiesAmul selling butter to households

B2B Buying Situations

  • Straight Rebuy: Routine reorder. Example: Auto manufacturer reordering standard steel
  • Modified Rebuy: Changing specifications. Example: Switching steel grade for new model
  • New Task: First-time purchase. Example: New EV battery sourcing

International Marketing Strategies

🌍 Global Strategy

Standardized product worldwide. High efficiency. Example: Coca-Cola tastes the same globally.

🏠 Multi-Domestic

Adapted to each country. High responsiveness. Example: McDonald's McAloo Tikki in India.

🌐 International

Export existing products. Low investment. Example: Indian IT services (TCS, Infosys).

🔄 Transnational

Global efficiency + local responsiveness. Example: Unilever adapts brands locally.

MRF Tyres: MRF demonstrates both B2B and international marketing excellence. In B2B, it supplies tyres to Tata Motors, Mahindra, and Ashok Leyland under OEM contracts. Internationally, MRF exports to 65+ countries, using different strategies — exporting to Africa, joint ventures in Sri Lanka, and subsidiaries in Australia. Its Chennai plant is one of the world's largest tyre manufacturing facilities.

Key Takeaway: B2B marketing requires relationship-building and rational value propositions. International marketing requires cultural sensitivity and strategic entry mode selection. Indian companies like MRF, Tata, and Infosys have built global footprints through careful strategy adaptation.

Case Study: Infosys — International Marketing Excellence

Infosys is India's most successful international marketing story. Entry Strategy: Started with exporting (low risk) → Joint ventures → Subsidiaries (Infosys BPO, EdgeVerve). Market Selection: US first (largest IT market), then Europe, then Asia-Pacific. Adaptation: Hired local talent in each market (not just Indian expats). Positioning: "Powered by Intellect, Driven by Values" — positioned as premium, not cheap outsourcing. B2B Marketing: Thought leadership (Infosys Knowledge Institute), CEO-level relationships, case studies. Revenue: $18B+, 200,000+ employees across 56 countries.

Case Study: Tata's Global Marketing Strategy

Tata Group uses different strategies for different businesses. Tata Motors: Acquired Jaguar Land Rover (JLR) for instant global premium presence — subsidiary strategy. Tata Steel: Acquired Corus (UK) → joint ventures in Southeast Asia. TCS: Organic growth through subsidiaries in 46 countries. Tata Tea: Acquired Tetley (UK) → branded Tata Global Beverages. Key lesson: There's no single "best" entry mode — the choice depends on industry, resources, and strategic goals. Tata's revenue: $150B+ across 100+ countries.

Exam Tip

B2B vs B2C comparison table is a guaranteed question. Memorize the 6 differences: Buyers, Decision Process, Sales Cycle, Relationship, Marketing, Example. For international marketing, know the 4 strategies (Global/Multi-domestic/International/Transnational) and 5 entry methods (Export → License → Franchise → JV → Subsidiary).

Unit 12: Digital Marketing & Emerging Issues

📖 Definition

Digital marketing is the use of digital channels — search engines, social media, email, websites, mobile apps — to promote products and services. It enables precise targeting, real-time measurement, and two-way customer communication at scale.

Digital Marketing Channels

🔍 Search Engine Marketing (SEM)

SEO (organic) + PPC (paid ads). Google processes 8.5 billion searches/day in India alone.

Example: Zomato bids on "food delivery near me" to appear at top of search results.

📱 Social Media Marketing

Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, LinkedIn, YouTube for brand building and engagement.

Example: Dunzo's viral Twitter presence drives brand awareness without TV ads.

📧 Email Marketing

Personalized email campaigns for retention and conversion.

Example: Amazon's "Recommended for you" emails drive 35% of its revenue.

📝 Content Marketing

Blog posts, videos, podcasts, infographics to attract and educate customers.

Example: Zerodha's Varsity module educates investors and builds trust.

Omnichannel Marketing

Seamless customer experience across all touchpoints — online, offline, mobile, social.

  • Click & Collect: Order online, pick up in-store (Reliance's JioMart + Reliance Retail)
  • Showrooming: Customers check in-store, buy online. Brands must integrate both
  • Webrooming: Research online, buy in-store. 78% of Indian consumers do this

Sustainable Marketing

🎯 Emerging Imperative
  • Green Marketing: Eco-friendly products and packaging. Example: Paper Boat's sustainable packaging
  • Cause Marketing: Supporting social causes. Example: Tata Tea's "Jaago Re" campaign
  • Ethical Marketing: Honest claims, fair pricing. Consumer Protection Act 2019 mandates transparency
  • Corporate Social Responsibility: ITC's e-Choupal, Tata's CSR initiatives

Nykaa: Nykaa exemplifies digital marketing excellence in India. It uses content marketing (Nykaa TV tutorials), influencer partnerships (beauty bloggers), social media engagement (Instagram with 5M+ followers), and data-driven personalization. Its omnichannel strategy includes 100+ physical stores alongside the app. Nykaa's ₹1 lakh crore+ IPO valuation was built entirely on digital-first marketing.

🧠 Mnemonic: Digital Marketing Channels

S-S-E-C-S — "Smart Students Excel in Computer Science"

Search Engine Marketing → Social Media Marketing → Email Marketing → Content Marketing → Mobile/Smartphone Marketing

Key Takeaway: Digital marketing is no longer optional — it's essential. India has 800M+ internet users and is the world's largest digital market by volume. Companies like Nykaa, Zomato, and PhonePe have billion-dollar businesses through digital-first strategies. The future belongs to marketers who master AI, data analytics, and omnichannel experiences.

Case Study: CRED — Digital Marketing Innovation

CRED (credit card payment platform) built a ₹50,000+ crore brand through unconventional digital marketing. (1) Exclusivity Positioning: Only credit score 750+ can join — created FOMO and aspiration. (2) Content Marketing: CRED Ads (IPL) — Rahul Dravid "Indiranagar ka gunda" went viral (100M+ views). (3) Gamification: CRED coins, rewards, jackpots — engagement beyond transactions. (4) Community: CRED members feel part of an exclusive club. (5) Brand Building: Premium aesthetics, quirky copy, celebrity endorsements. Zero traditional advertising spend — 100% digital and word-of-mouth.

Case Study: Zepto — Speed as Marketing

Zepto (quick commerce) made delivery speed its entire marketing strategy. Digital Channels: Instagram reels showing 10-minute deliveries, meme marketing, influencer unboxings. Positioning: "Groceries in 10 minutes" — the speed IS the message. Technology: Dark stores (micro-fulfillment centers) within 2km of dense areas. Data-Driven: AI predicts demand by area, time, weather. Customer Acquisition: First order free + referral bonuses. Result: $1.4B valuation in 2 years, 3M+ orders/day. Proves that in digital marketing, the product experience IS the promotion.
