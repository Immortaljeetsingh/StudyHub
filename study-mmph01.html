Block-01: Understanding Organisations

Unit 1: Approaches to Understanding Organisations

Block 1Unit 1
📖 Core Concepts

Organisation: A social unit with specific purposes; derived from Greek "organon" (tool/instrument). Chester I. Bernard defined it as "a system of consciously coordinated activities of two or more persons."

Weber's Ideal Type Features: Transparent boundaries, central coordination, internal differentiation, legitimate order, flexibility, part of societal transformation.

Levitt's 4 Components (1965): Task, People, Technology, Structure.

📖 Open vs Closed Systems

Open System: Constantly interacts with environment (all real organizations). 7 features: Subsystems, Synergy, Input-Output Model, Goal-Seeking, Entropy, Steady State, Feedback.

Closed System: Self-contained, isolated from environment (theoretical only).

📖 McKinsey 7S Model

Hard Ss: Strategy, Structure, Systems (tangible). Soft Ss: Style, Staff, Shared Values, Skills (intangible).

📖 Key Definitions

Organisation: A social unit with specific purposes, consciously coordinated, with defined boundaries and internal differentiation.

Synergy: The concept that the whole is greater than the sum of its individual parts.

Entropy: The natural tendency of systems toward disorder without energy infusion.

💡 Real-World Example

A manufacturing company (open system) receives raw materials (inputs), transforms them into products (throughput), sells to customers (output), and uses customer feedback to improve (feedback loop).

Unit 2: Theoretical Frameworks

Block 1Unit 2
📖 Core Concepts

Classical Theory: Scientific Management (Taylor) — time-and-motion studies, standardization. Administrative Theory (Fayol) — 14 principles. Bureaucracy (Weber) — hierarchy, rules, impersonality.

Neo-Classical Theory: Human Relations Movement (Mayo) — Hawthorne Experiments showed social factors matter more than physical conditions.

Systems Theory: Organisation as open system with inputs → transformation → outputs. Katz and Kahn (1966) first applied open systems theory.

Contingency Theory: No one best way to organize. Design depends on environment, technology, size.

📖 Key Definitions

Classical Theory: Early management theories focusing on efficiency, structure, and principles (Taylor, Fayol, Weber).

Contingency Theory: The view that the best organizational design depends on the specific circumstances.

Hawthorne Effect: Workers' productivity improves when they know they're being observed.

Unit 3: Organisational Effectiveness

Block 1Unit 3
📖 Core Concepts

Effectiveness: Achieving organizational goals — doing the right things. Efficiency: Using minimum resources — doing things right.

4 Approaches to Measuring Effectiveness:

  • Goal Approach: Achievement of stated goals
  • Systems Approach: Resource acquisition, adaptability, efficiency
  • Strategic Constituencies Approach: Satisfying key stakeholders
  • Competing Values Approach: Balancing contradictory demands (flexibility vs control, internal vs external)

Block-02: Basics of Organisational Structure

Unit 4: Fundamentals of Organisational Structure

Block 2Unit 4
📖 Core Concepts

Organisation Design: The total pattern of structural elements used to manage the overall organisation (Burton & Thakur, 1995).

Allen's 7-Step Design Process: Identify objectives → Organise top-down → Organise bottom-up → Decide management positions → Identify groupings → Seek balance → Check spans of control.

Organisation Life Cycle: Birth (centralised, informal) → Youth (growing, some formalization) → Middle (formal bureaucracy, decentralised) → Maturity (mechanistic, rigid).

📖 Likert's System 4 Organisation

System 1: Bureaucratic — centralized, downward communication, authoritarian.

System 4: Behavioral — decentralized, free-flowing communication, participative. Likert concluded System 4 is superior.

📖 Key Definitions

Organisation Design: The total pattern of structural elements used to manage the overall organisation.

Bureaucracy: An organisation founded on a legitimate, formal system of authority with specialization, hierarchy, and rules.

Socio-Technical System: Balances the social system (human interactions) with the technical system (tasks and technology).

Unit 5: Factors Affecting Organisational Structures

Block 2Unit 5
📖 Burns & Stalker — Mechanistic vs Organic

Mechanistic: Stable environment → rules, standardization, centralized decisions.

Organic: Unstable environment → flexibility, few rules, decentralized, autonomous teams.

These are endpoints on a continuum, not binary choices.

Block-03: Organisational Design

Unit 6: Typology of Organisational Designs

Block 3Unit 6
📖 Core Concepts

10 design types from simple to complex:

  • Line: Simplest form; scalar principle — authority flows vertically. Clear authority, fast decisions.
  • Line & Staff: Line authority (command) + Staff specialists (advice). Staff supports but doesn't command.
  • Functional: Grouped by common functions (production, marketing, finance, HR). Specialization, efficiency.
  • Product/Divisional: Autonomous units for each product line. Diversification, accountability.
  • Hybrid: Combines functional (centralized) and divisional (decentralized) features.
  • Matrix: Dual authority: functional manager + project manager. Breaks unity of command.
  • Project: Temporary team for specific project; disbanded after completion.
  • Virtual/Network: Core functions in-house; rest outsourced. Connected by IT.
  • Boundaryless (Jack Welch, GE): No internal or external boundaries.
  • Horizontal: Flat structure organized around core processes. Cross-functional teams.

📖 Key Definitions

Line Authority: The right to direct the activities of subordinates in the chain of command.

Staff Authority: The right to advise but not command — advisory relationship.

Matrix Organisation: Employees report to both a functional manager and a project manager simultaneously.

Boundaryless Organisation: Eliminates internal and external barriers to free flow of ideas, resources, and people.

Block 3Unit 7

5 Disciplines:

  • Personal Mastery: Continuously learning and improving
  • Mental Models: Challenging assumptions and mental frameworks
  • Shared Vision: Common picture of the future
  • Team Learning: Dialogue and discussion in groups
  • Systems Thinking: Seeing the whole, not just parts (the 5th discipline that integrates all)

  • Network Organisation: Central hub with outsourced functions. IT-enabled coordination.

Block-04: Job Design

Unit 8: Approaches to Job Design

Block 4Unit 8
📖 Core Concepts

Job Design: Specifying tasks, methods, and relationships for each job to meet organizational and individual needs.

Traditional Approach (Taylor): Fit people to jobs. Standardization, specialization, efficiency.

Contemporary Approaches:

  • Job Rotation: Moving employees between different jobs periodically.
  • Job Enlargement: "Horizontal loading" — adding more tasks at same level.
  • Job Enrichment (Herzberg): "Vertical loading" — adding control, responsibility, freedom.
▶
Organisational Change
▶
Lewin Change Model
📖 Hackman & Oldham Job Characteristics Model

5 Core Job Dimensions:

  • Skill Variety — different activities using different skills
  • Task Identity — complete a whole piece of work
  • Task Significance — impact on others' lives
  • Autonomy — freedom in scheduling and procedures
  • Feedback — direct information about performance

3 Critical Psychological States: Experienced meaningfulness (SV+TI+TS), Experienced responsibility (Autonomy), Knowledge of results (Feedback).

MPS = (Skill Variety + Task Identity + Task Significance) / 3 × Autonomy × Feedback

📖 Key Definitions

Job Design: The process of specifying the contents, methods, and relationships of jobs.

Job Enrichment: Adding motivators to a job — more responsibility, autonomy, growth opportunities (Herzberg).

Job Enlargement: Adding more tasks at the same level — horizontal expansion of job scope.

ApproachDirectionFocusExample
Job SimplificationNarrow tasksEfficiencyAssembly line
Job RotationLateral movementVarietyCross-training
Job EnlargementHorizontal expansionMore tasksClerk handles multiple forms
Job EnrichmentVertical expansionMore responsibilityEmployee plans own work

Unit 9: Emerging Trends at Work

Block 4Unit 9
📖 Core Concepts
  • Telecommuting/Remote Work: Working from locations outside the office using technology.
  • Flexible Work Arrangements: Flextime, Compressed Workweek, Job Sharing.
  • Virtual Teams: Geographically dispersed teams collaborating through technology.
  • Gig Economy: Short-term contracts, freelance work, independent contractors.
  • Contingent Workforce: Non-permanent workers (temps, contractors, freelancers).
  • Automation & AI Impact: Routine tasks automated; new roles in oversight, creativity, problem-solving.
📖 Key Definitions

Telecommuting: Working from a remote location (usually home) using telecommunications technology.

Gig Economy: A labor market characterized by short-term contracts or freelance work rather than permanent jobs.

Unit 11: Organisational Conflict

Block 5Unit 11
📖 Core Concepts

Types of Conflict: Intrapersonal (within individual), Interpersonal (between individuals), Intragroup (within group), Intergroup (between groups).

Functional vs Dysfunctional: Functional = constructive (better decisions, innovation). Dysfunctional = destructive (stress, turnover).

▶
Organisational Development
▶
OD Interventions
📖 Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Modes
ModeAssertivenessCooperationWhen to Use
CompetingHighLowEmergency, quick decision needed
CollaboratingHighHighImportant issue, both concerns matter
CompromisingModerateModerateTime pressure, equally powerful parties
AvoidingLowLowTrivial issue, cooling off needed
AccommodatingLowHighPreserving relationships, you're wrong
📖 Key Definitions

Conflict: A process that begins when an individual or group perceives that another has frustrated or is about to frustrate a major concern.

Functional Conflict: Conflict that supports the goals of the group and improves performance.

Dysfunctional Conflict: Conflict that hinders group performance.
