⚠ IHIP Framework — The 4 Defining Characteristics

The IHIP Framework is the most widely cited model for understanding services in academic literature. It identifies four unique characteristics that distinguish services from goods:

I — Intangibility: Services cannot be seen, touched, or felt before purchase. A consulting session, a haircut, or a flight cannot be sampled in advance. This makes evaluation difficult and forces marketers to use tangible cues (branded uniforms, office décor, professional websites) to reduce perceived risk. Indian example: Apollo Hospitals invests heavily in modern lobbies and branded scrubs to make its healthcare service feel tangible.

H — Heterogeneity (Variability): No two service encounters are identical. The quality depends on who provides it, when, where, and to whom. A Domino's pizza delivery experience in Mumbai may differ from one in Lucknow. Indian example: HDFC Bank works to standardize its service across 6,000+ branches through rigorous training, yet branch-level variability still exists.

I — Inseparability: Services are produced and consumed at the same time. The provider and customer must both be present. You cannot "inventory" a doctor's consultation. This limits scalability and makes the service encounter (moment of truth) critical. Indian example: A Zomato delivery partner's interaction with the customer is the brand experience itself.

P — Perishability: Services cannot be stored. An empty hotel room tonight is revenue lost forever. An empty airline seat at departure generates zero income. This forces dynamic pricing and demand management strategies. Indian example: IRCTC uses dynamic pricing on premium trains (Vande Bharat, Rajdhani) to manage demand and maximize revenue per seat.

⚠ India's Service Sector — Key Statistics

GDP contribution: Services contribute 55%+ to India's GDP (up from 30% in 1950). India is the 5th largest services economy globally. Employment: 32%+ of India's workforce is in services (up from 15% in 1990). IT-BPM alone employs 5 million+ people. Exports: India's services exports crossed $340 billion in 2023-24. IT services are the largest component ($194 billion). FDI: Services sector attracts 60%+ of India's FDI inflows. IT, telecom, and financial services are top attractors.

Key growth drivers: 1. IT revolution (Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune). 2. Telecom boom (Jio, 800M+ internet users). 3. Financial inclusion (Jan Dhan, UPI). 4. Healthcare expansion (Apollo, Fortis). 5. Education growth (Byju's, Unacademy). 6. E-commerce (Flipkart, Amazon India). 7. Tourism (Incredible India). 8. Start-up ecosystem (100+ unicorns).

Challenges: Quality variability, infrastructure gaps, regulatory complexity, skilled labor shortage in Tier-2/3 cities, digital divide between urban and rural India.

1. Increasing affluence → more demand for convenience services. 2. More leisure time → recreation, entertainment, travel. 3. Higher % of women in workforce → crèches, domestic help, childcare. 4. Greater life expectancy → healthcare, nursing homes. 5. Increasing complexity of life → credit cards, professional services. 6. Technology advancement → IT, digital services.

⚠ Lovelock's Service Classification

People-based vs. Equipment-based: People-based = consulting, teaching, medical practice (human expertise). Equipment-based = ATM, vending machine, online banking (technology-driven). Tangible actions vs. Intangible actions: Tangible actions = hairdressing, freight transport, cleaning (on physical objects). Intangible actions = education, insurance, entertainment (on minds/perceptions). High contact vs. Low contact: High contact = hotels, hospitals, restaurants (customer present throughout). Low contact = mail sorting, credit card processing (minimal customer involvement).

Marketing implications: High-contact services need more investment in people, physical evidence, and process. Low-contact services can focus on technology and efficiency. Equipment-based services can scale faster but may feel impersonal.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 7Ps: Product, Price, Place, Promotion, People, Process, Physical Evidence
  • Service Triangle: External (promises), Internal (training), Interactive (delivery)
  • External Marketing = promises to customers; Internal Marketing = motivating employees
  • Interactive Marketing = actual service delivery ("moment of truth")
  • Lovelock classifies services by: people/equipment, tangible/intangible, high/low contact

Zone of Tolerance
📖 Core Concept

Service consumers evaluate based on expectations vs. perceptions. Three types of service qualities determine how consumers evaluate. Zone of Tolerance = range of acceptable service levels between desired service and adequate service. Wider for less important attributes; narrows when service is critical.

TypeDefinitionExampleWhen Evaluated
Search qualitiesCan evaluate before purchaseRestaurant decor, hotel lobbyBefore purchase
Experience qualitiesEvaluate only during consumptionFood taste, flight comfortDuring consumption
Credence qualitiesDifficult to evaluate even afterMedical diagnosis, legal adviceHard to judge ever
⚠ Consumer Behaviour in Services — Key Framework

How do customers evaluate services? Unlike goods, services cannot be inspected before purchase. Customers use three types of quality cues:

Search qualities: Attributes that can be evaluated before purchase. Examples: restaurant decor, hotel lobby appearance, website design, brand reputation. Marketing implication: Invest in tangible cues — clean premises, professional staff appearance, modern website. Indian example: Tanishq stores use gold-toned interiors and immaculate display cases that signal premium quality before any purchase.

Experience qualities: Attributes that can be evaluated only during or after consumption. Examples: food taste, flight comfort, doctor's bedside manner, amusement park thrill. Marketing implication: Focus on service delivery excellence and encourage trial. Indian example: Domino's "30-minute delivery or free" guarantee is a commitment to experience quality.

Credence qualities: Attributes that are difficult to evaluate even after consumption. Examples: medical diagnosis accuracy, legal advice quality, financial planning effectiveness. Marketing implication: Use trust signals — certifications, qualifications, testimonials, case studies. Indian example: Byju's displays student success stories and IIT-JEE topper testimonials to build credibility for its education service.

⚠ Zone of Tolerance — Detailed Explanation

The Zone of Tolerance (ZOT) is the range between desired service (what the customer hopes to receive) and adequate service (the minimum acceptable level). When service falls within this zone, customers are satisfied. When it drops below adequate, customers become dissatisfied. When it exceeds desired, customers are delighted.

Key dynamics of ZOT:

1. Wider for less important attributes: A customer may tolerate slow Wi-Fi in a hotel lobby (low importance) but will not tolerate a dirty room (high importance). The ZOT for cleanliness is narrow.

2. Narrows for critical services: In emergency healthcare, the ZOT is extremely narrow — patients expect near-perfect service with no room for delay or error.

3. Varies by customer segment: Business travelers have a narrower ZOT for flight punctuality than leisure travelers.

4. Shifts over time: As customers gain experience with a service provider, their adequate service level rises (they become harder to please), narrowing the ZOT.

Indian example: A first-time Zomato customer may tolerate a 10-minute delay (wide ZOT), but a loyal Zorder Gold member expects on-time delivery every time (narrow ZOT).

⚠ Consumer Expectations Influenced By

1. Word-of-mouth communications. 2. Past personal experience. 3. Personal needs. 4. External communications (advertising, PR).

💡 Real-World Example

A restaurant's decor (search quality) can be evaluated before visiting, but food taste (experience quality) only during the meal. A doctor's diagnosis (credence quality) is hard to evaluate even after treatment — you trust the expertise. Indian context: Byju's tutoring is a credence quality service — parents cannot easily judge whether learning is effective even after months of use, which is why certifications and exam results are critical marketing tools.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Zone of Tolerance = range between desired and adequate service levels
  • ZOT narrows for critical services (healthcare) and loyal customers (Zomato Gold)
  • ZOT widens for less important attributes and shifts over time with experience
  • Search, experience, and credence qualities require different marketing strategies
  • For credence quality services, trust signals (certifications, testimonials) are critical
  • 4 expectation influences: Word-of-mouth, Past experience, Personal needs, External communications
  • Indian example: Byju's is credence quality — parents cannot judge learning effectiveness

Unit 4: Product Decisions

▶
Kotler 5 Product Levels

Service Product Design
📖 Core Concept

Service product has multiple layers. Kotler's 5 Product Levels + Gronroos' 3-Level model define service architecture. New service development follows 5 stages.

LevelNameHotel Example
1Core BenefitRest and sleep
2Basic ProductRoom, bed, bathroom
3Expected ProductClean room, towels, quietness
4Augmented ProductPrompt room service, music, check-in/out
5Potential ProductAll-suite hotel, loyalty programs
⚠ Gronroos' 3-Level Service Product Model

Level 1: Basic Service Package — Core service + Facilitating services (mandatory) + Supporting services (differentiators). Level 2: Augmented Service Offering — Accessibility + Interaction + Customer participation. Level 3: Market Communication — Image management through all channels.

💡 Real-World Example

A hotel's core service is lodging; facilitating = bell boy, check-in; supporting = spa, car rental. Remove facilitating = service collapses. Remove supporting = still functional but less competitive. 5 Stages of New Service Development: Customer Benefit Concept → Service Concept → Service Offer → Service Forms → Service Delivery System.

⚠ Service Product Layers — Indian Examples

Kotler's Levels with Indian Context:

Level 1 (Core Benefit): For OYO Rooms, the core benefit is affordable accommodation. For Practo, it is access to healthcare advice. Level 2 (Basic Product): OYO provides a room with bed, bathroom, and Wi-Fi. Practo provides a video consultation with a doctor. Level 3 (Expected Product): OYO guests expect clean linens, working AC, and check-in within 10 minutes. Practo users expect prescriptions delivered digitally. Level 4 (Augmented Product): OYO offers OYO Wizard loyalty program, early check-in, and complimentary breakfast. Practo offers medicine delivery and follow-up reminders. Level 5 (Potential Product): OYO could integrate travel planning, local experiences. Practo could offer AI-driven health monitoring wearables.

Gronroos' Model with Indian Context: For an ICICI Bank savings account: Basic Package = account + debit card + passbook (facilitating) + rewards points (supporting). Augmented Offering = 24/7 customer care accessibility + personalized interaction at branch + customer participation via iMobile app. Market Communication = ICICI brand image, "Hum Hai Na" tagline, consistent premium positioning.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Kotler's 5 levels: Core, Basic, Expected, Augmented, Potential
  • Gronroos: Basic Package + Augmented Offering + Market Communication
  • Compare Kotler (5 levels) vs Gronroos (3 levels) in exams

Yield Management
📖 Core Concept

Services pricing is harder than goods pricing due to intangibility. Three basic methods: Cost-based (cost + markup), Competition-based (match/beat competitors), Value-based (customer perceived value).

GuaranteedPay only on resultsEmployment agencies

OffsetLow basic, high for extrasGynecologist consultation fees
High Price MaintenancePremium for perceived qualityTop surgeons, luxury hotels

💡 Pricing Strategies — Indian Examples

Jio entered with penetration pricing (free data), disrupted the market, then gradually increased prices after gaining 400M+ subscribers. Uber's surge pricing during rain = yield management. Additional Indian examples:

Skimming: Apple launches iPhones in India at premium prices (Rs 79,900+), then reduces prices for older models. Tata Neu launched premium services first. Penetration: Ola offered Rs 1 rides during launch to gain market share from Uber. Flipkart's Big Billion Days use deep discounts to drive volume. Diversionary: SpiceJet and IndiGo advertise Rs 999 base fares but charge extra for seats, meals, baggage, and check-in. Guaranteed: Urbanclap (Urban Company) offers a satisfaction guarantee — if you are not happy, they redo the service free. Loss Leader: Hotstar offered free IPL streaming to build user base before monetizing through ads and premium subscriptions. Offset: Private hospitals in India charge nominal consultation fees (Rs 200–500) but earn significantly from diagnostic tests and procedures. High Price Maintenance: The Taj and Oberoi hotel chains maintain premium pricing to reinforce their luxury positioning, never discounting even during off-season. Yield Management: IRCTC dynamic pricing on Shatabdi and Rajdhani trains; MakeMyTrip showing different hotel prices based on search history.

  • 3 Methods: Cost-based, Competition-based, Value-based
  • 8 Strategies: Skimming, Penetration, Diversionary, Guaranteed, Loss Leader, Offset, High Price, Yield Management
  • Always give real-world examples for each strategy in exams
  • Indian examples: Jio (penetration), Apple (skimming), SpiceJet (diversionary), Urban Company (guaranteed), Hotstar (loss leader), hospitals (offset), Taj (high price), IRCTC (yield)

⚠ Customer Switching Reasons (8)

1. Pricing issues. 2. Inconvenience. 3. Core service failure. 4. Service encounter failures. 5. Response to failed recovery. 6. Competition. 7. Ethical problems. 8. Involuntary switching.

⚠ Service Recovery — Strategies & Guarantees

5 Recovery Strategies:

1. Fix immediately: Zomato's instant refund for wrong/missing items. 2. Compensate: Ola gives ride credits for delayed pickups. 3. Follow up: Flipkart's post-return satisfaction survey. 4. Empower frontline: Taj Hotels allows managers to offer complimentary stays for failures. 5. Learn from failures: OYO uses guest feedback to delist poor properties.

Service Guarantee Criteria (5): Unconditional (no fine print), Easy to understand, Meaningful (real compensation), Easy to invoke (no bureaucracy), Credible (brand reputation). Indian example: Urban Company's "100% satisfaction or free redo" meets all 5 criteria.

Recovery paradox: Customers who experience a failure and receive excellent recovery may be MORE satisfied than those who never experienced a failure. Indian example: A Zomato customer whose order was late but received instant refund + free dessert may become more loyal than one with a perfect experience.

💡 Service Process — Indian Examples

Amazon's service blueprint: browsing → order → payment → delivery → returns. Each step has fail points identified and recovery processes built in. Indian examples:

People processing: VLCC weight loss programs require customer presence at every session. Cult.fit fitness classes need physical attendance. Possession processing: Cashify repairs and refurbishes phones — customer's device is processed without customer presence. Mental stimulus processing: Unacademy and BYJU'S process knowledge into the student's mind through online video lectures (customer presence not required). Information processing: Policybazaar processes customer data to recommend insurance policies. ClearTax processes financial data for tax filing. ⚠ SERVQUAL — Detailed Explanation

SERVQUAL (Parasuraman, Zeithaml, Berry) is the most widely used service quality measurement instrument. It uses a 22-item questionnaire measuring 5 dimensions. Each item is measured twice — once for expectations and once for perceptions. The gap between the two scores reveals service quality.

Scoring: SERVQUAL Score = Perception Score – Expectation Score. A positive score means service exceeds expectations. A negative score means service falls short. Scores are calculated for each dimension and overall.

Dimension breakdown with sample items:

Reliability (5 items): "When XYZ promises to do something by a certain time, it does so." "When you have a problem, XYZ shows a sincere interest in solving it." This is the MOST important dimension — research consistently shows it explains the largest variance in overall service quality. Indian example: Flipkart's reliable delivery within promised timelines builds trust.

Assurance (4 items):** "Employees of XYZ are consistently courteous." "Employees of XYZ have the knowledge to answer your questions." Indian example: HDFC Bank's trained relationship managers who can explain complex financial products build assurance.

Tangibles (4 items):** "XYZ has modern-looking equipment." "XYZ's physical facilities are visually appealing." Indian example: Tanishq's gold-toned interiors and immaculate display cases signal premium quality.

Empathy (5 items):** "XYZ gives you individual attention." "XYZ has operating hours convenient to all customers." Indian example: Apollo Hospitals assigns a dedicated care coordinator to each patient.

Responsiveness (4 items):** "Employees of XYZ give you prompt service." "Employees of XYZ are always willing to help." Indian example: Amazon India's 24/7 chat support resolves queries within minutes.

⚠ Gap Model — Indian Application

Gap 1 (Knowledge Gap): Indian airlines often do not understand that passengers value on-time performance more than in-flight meals. Fix: Regular customer surveys, mystery shopping, social media listening. Gap 2 (Standards Gap): Many Indian hospitals have cleanliness standards on paper but fail to translate them into actionable SOPs. Fix: Process redesign, Six Sigma, clear SOPs with accountability. Gap 3 (Delivery Gap): Call center agents may know the script but lack empowerment to resolve issues. Fix: Employee training, empowerment (like Ritz-Carlton's $2,000 rule), better HR policies. Gap 4 (Communication Gap): E-commerce sites promise "2-day delivery" but take 5 days. Fix: Under-promise and over-deliver; align marketing promises with operations capability. Technology closing gaps: Zomato's real-time tracking (Gap 4), Practo's patient feedback system (Gap 1), HDFC Bank's chatbot Eva (Gap 3).

💡 Real-World Example

Domino's Pizza Tracker lets customers track their order in real-time — reducing Gap 4 by being transparent about delivery process. Gronroos model: Technical Quality (WHAT) + Functional Quality (HOW) + Image. Indian context: OYO Rooms improved from 2.5 to 4+ ratings by addressing Gap 2 (setting quality standards for franchise partners) and Gap 3 (deploying mystery auditors to check compliance).

⚠ GATS Principles & India's Position

3 Core GATS Principles:

1. Most Favoured Nation (MFN): Every WTO member must treat all other members equally. Cannot give preferential treatment to one country's services over another. Exception: Regional trade agreements (like SAFTA) are allowed.

2. Transparency: Members must publish all laws, regulations, and practices affecting trade in services. Must notify WTO of any changes. Indian example: India publishes FDI policy changes on DPIIT website.

3. National Treatment: Foreign service providers must be treated no less favorably than domestic providers (once market access is granted). Indian example: Foreign banks in India must follow same RBI regulations as Indian banks.

India's commitments: India has made commitments in 11 out of 12 service sectors. India is a leading voice for developing countries in WTO services negotiations. India opposes "Mode 4" restrictions (visa barriers for IT professionals) and pushes for easier movement of natural persons.

💡 Demand & Capacity — Indian Examples

Uber's surge pricing during rain or rush hour is yield management — higher prices attract more drivers and manage demand. Airlines sell the same seat at different prices based on booking time. Indian examples:

Hospital chains share specialist doctors across branches. Facility expansion: Zomato expanded dark stores (Zomato Instant) to increase food delivery capacity during peak lunch hours.

Managing Waiting Lines in India: Operations: Big Bazaar uses express counters for customers with fewer than 10 items. Perception: Multiplexes play trailers and ads in waiting areas. Banks provide token systems with estimated wait times. Virtual queuing: Practo lets you "check in" online before visiting a doctor, reducing physical wait time.

⚠ Yield Management — Detailed Explanation

Yield Management (Revenue Management) is the practice of dynamically pricing a perishable service based on demand, time, and capacity to maximize revenue per available unit.

Conditions for Yield Management: 1. Fixed capacity (airline seats, hotel rooms). 2. Perishable inventory (unsold = lost). 3. Varying demand (peak vs. off-peak). 4. Segmentable customers (business vs. leisure). 5. Advance booking possible.

How it works: Airlines use algorithms to set prices based on booking velocity, days to departure, and historical demand. Early bookings are cheap (fill base). Last-minute bookings are expensive (capture high willingness-to-pay). Business travelers pay more (inflexible dates). Leisure travelers pay less (flexible dates).

Indian examples: IRCTC: Tatkal (last-minute) tickets cost 1.5–2x advance tickets. Airlines: IndiGo, SpiceJet, Air India charge different prices for same seat based on booking time and demand. Hotels: OYO and Treebo increase prices during festivals (Diwali, Dussehra) and decrease during off-season. Events: IPL match tickets are cheaper for weekday games than weekend games.

Risks of Yield Management: Customer dissatisfaction if they discover different prices for same service. Perceived unfairness. Brand damage if pricing seems exploitative.

⚠ Waiting Line Psychology (Maister's 8 Rules)

Rule 1: Unoccupied time feels longer. → Provide entertainment. Indian example: Multiplexes play trailers in waiting areas. Rule 2: Pre-process waits feel longer. → Let customers start the process. Indian example: Practo's online check-in before visiting doctor. Rule 3: Anxiety makes waits seem longer. → Provide information. Indian example: Banks show estimated wait time on token. Rule 4: Uncertain waits are longer. → Give updates. Indian example: Swiggy's real-time delivery tracking. Rule 5: Unexplained waits are longer. → Explain delays. Indian example: Airlines announcing "delay due to air traffic control." Rule 6: Unfair waits are longer. → Ensure fairness. Indian example: Token systems at government offices. Rule 7: The more valuable the service, the longer the wait. → Customers tolerate waits for premium services. Indian example: 2-hour wait at popular dhabas. Rule 8: Solo waits feel longer. → Group waiting feels faster. Indian example: Waiting rooms at hospitals.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Yield Management: dynamic pricing to maximize revenue per available unit (IRCTC, airlines, hotels)
  • Conditions: Fixed capacity, perishable, varying demand, segmentable, advance booking
  • Demand strategies: Differential pricing, Promotion, Reservation, Yield management
  • Supply strategies: Part-time staff, Cross-training, Capacity sharing, Expansion
  • Waiting line management: Operations, Perception, Virtual queuing (Maister's 8 rules)
  • Common in airlines, hotels, car rentals, events, restaurants

⚠ Common Exam Mistakes to Avoid

1. Confusing IHIP with TIPS-NP: IHIP is the standard 4-characteristic framework (most exams expect this). TIPS-NP adds Non-ownership and People as separate characteristics. Use IHIP unless specifically asked about TIPS-NP.

2. Forgetting to draw diagrams: For Gap Model, Service Triangle, and Blueprint questions, diagrams are mandatory and carry 3-5 marks. Always label all components clearly.

3. Not giving Indian examples: Examiners expect India-specific examples. Generic "McDonald's" or "Amazon US" examples score less than "Zomato," "HDFC Bank," or "Taj Hotels."

4. Mixing up Gronroos and PZB: Gronroos has 2 dimensions (Technical + Functional) + Image. PZB/SERVQUAL has 5 dimensions (RATER). Do not confuse them.

5. Ignoring the "marketing implications" part: When asked about IHIP or any framework, always conclude with "Therefore, marketers should..." to show application of theory.

6. Not knowing SERVQUAL scoring: SERVQUAL Score = Perception – Expectation. Positive = exceeds expectations. Negative = falls short. Mention this in SERVQUAL answers.

7. Writing generic answers: Avoid textbook-only answers. Examiners reward application — link theory to real companies and current trends (UPI, super-apps, omnichannel).

8. Missing the "compare" part: When asked to "compare," always present a structured table or side-by-side analysis, not just descriptions of each model separately.
