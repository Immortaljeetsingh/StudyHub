Unit 1: Marketing of Services — An Introduction

📖 Definition

Service (Kotler): "Any activity or benefit that one party can offer to another that is essentially intangible and does not result in the ownership of anything."

Service (W.J. Stanton): "Separately identifiable, essentially intangible activities which provide want-satisfaction, not necessarily tied to the sale of a product."

📊 Characteristics of Services — TIPS-NP
T — Tangibility Spectrum
↓
I — Inseparability (Production = Consumption)
↓
P — Perishability (Cannot be stored)
↓
S — Simultaneity (Producer + Consumer interact)
↓
N — Non-ownership (No transfer of title)
↓
P — People as Product (Human element crucial)
💡 Real-World Example

Urban Company (formerly UrbanClap) created a platform connecting service providers (beauty, repair, cleaning) with customers — demonstrating how technology enables service delivery at scale.

🧠 Memory Aid

"TIPS-NP" — Tangibility, Inseparability, Perishability, Simultaneity, Non-ownership, People

✅ Unit Summary

  • Economy: Primary (agriculture) → Secondary (manufacturing) → Tertiary (services)
  • USA became first "service economy" post-WWII with >50% workforce in services
  • Services have 6 characteristics: TIPS-NP
  • Key difference from goods: intangible, perishable, inseparable, no ownership transfer

Unit 2: Conceptual Framework for Services Marketing

📖 Core Concept

Traditional 4Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) are insufficient for services. Extended to 7Ps with addition of People, Process, Physical Evidence.

📊 Service Marketing Triangle
COMPANY
↘ External Marketing ↙ Internal Marketing
CUSTOMER
EMPLOYEES
Interactive Marketing
💡 Real-World Example

A hospital's external marketing promises "world-class care" (advertising), internal marketing trains nurses for empathy (HR), interactive marketing is the actual patient experience at the reception.

🧠 Memory Aid

"EII" — External, Interactive, Internal (3 types of service marketing)

✅ Unit Summary

  • 7Ps: Product, Price, Place, Promotion, People, Process, Physical Evidence
  • Service Triangle: 3 types of marketing must align
  • External Marketing = promises; Internal Marketing = training; Interactive Marketing = delivery

Unit 3: Consumer Behaviour in Services

📖 Core Concept

Service consumers evaluate based on expectations vs. perceptions. Three types of service qualities determine evaluation.

📊 Service Quality Evaluation Types
Search Qualities — Evaluate BEFORE purchase (restaurant decor)
↓
Experience Qualities — Evaluate DURING consumption (food taste)
↓
Credence Qualities — Hard to evaluate EVEN AFTER (medical diagnosis)

✅ Unit Summary

  • Zone of Tolerance = range between desired and adequate service levels
  • Consumer expectations shaped by: word-of-mouth, past experience, needs, external communications
  • Search, experience, and credence qualities require different marketing strategies

Unit 4: Product Decisions

📖 Core Concept

Service product has multiple layers. Kotler's 5 Product Levels + Gronroos' 3-Level model define service architecture.

📊 Kotler's 5 Product Levels (Hotel Example)
Level 5: Potential Product (loyalty programs, all-suite)
Level 4: Augmented Product (room service, music)
Level 3: Expected Product (clean room, towels)
Level 2: Basic Product (room, bed, bathroom)
Level 1: Core Benefit (rest and sleep)
💡 Real-World Example

A hotel's core service is lodging; facilitating = bell boy, check-in; supporting = spa, car rental. Remove facilitating = service collapses. Remove supporting = still functional but less competitive.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 Stages of New Service Development: Customer Benefit Concept → Service Concept → Service Offer → Service Forms → Service Delivery System
  • Gronroos: Basic Service Package + Augmented Service Offering + Market Communication
  • Compare Kotler (5 levels) vs Gronroos (3 levels) in exams

Unit 5: Pricing Decisions

📖 Core Concept

Services pricing is harder than goods pricing due to intangibility. Three basic methods + eight specific strategies.

📊 8 Pricing Strategies for Services
Skimming — High initial, lowers over time
Penetration — Low initial to gain share (Jio)
Diversionary — Low basic, charge for extras (budget airlines)
Guaranteed — Pay only on results
Loss Leader — Low initial, hope for repeat (gym trials)
Offset — Low basic, high for extras
High Price Maintenance — Premium for perceived quality
Yield Management — Dynamic pricing by demand (airlines, Uber)
💡 Real-World Example

Jio entered with penetration pricing (free data), disrupted the market, then gradually increased prices after gaining 400M+ subscribers.

🧠 Memory Aid

"SPY GOOD" — Skimming, Penetration, Yield, Guaranteed, Offset, Other (diversionary, loss leader), Dynamic

✅ Unit Summary

  • 3 Methods: Cost-based, Competition-based, Value-based
  • 8 Strategies: Skimming, Penetration, Diversionary, Guaranteed, Loss Leader, Offset, High Price, Yield Management
  • Always give real-world examples for each strategy in exams

Unit 6: Place Decisions

📖 Core Concept

Distribution channels for services are shorter due to inseparability. Three main channel types: Direct, Electronic, Intermediaries.

📊 Distribution Channels
Direct Channels — Company delivers directly (hospital, salon)
↓
Electronic Channels — Website, app, digital (Netflix, banking)
↓
Intermediaries — Agents, brokers, franchises (travel agents, McDonald's)

✅ Unit Summary

  • Inseparability makes direct channels most common in services
  • Key decisions: location, accessibility, multi-channel integration
  • Technology enables 24/7 availability through electronic channels

Unit 7: Promotion Decisions

📖 Core Concept

Intangibility makes service communication challenging. Must "tangibilize the intangible" through tangible cues, testimonials, and guarantees.

📊 Promotion Mix for Services
Advertising — Use tangible cues, testimonials
Personal Selling — Critical for high-contact services
Sales Promotion — Trial offers, free consultations
Public Relations — Reputation management
Direct Marketing — Personalized communication
💡 Real-World Example

Amul's advertising uses tangible imagery (the Amul girl) to make a service (dairy) feel tangible and familiar — decades of consistent branding.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Challenge: cannot show service before purchase
  • Solution: tangible cues, people, locations, testimonials
  • Word-of-mouth is most powerful but least controllable

Unit 8: Managing People

📖 Core Concept

People = 5th P. Service employees are "boundary spanners" between company and customer. Emotional labour is unique to service jobs.

📊 Service Profit Chain
Employee Satisfaction
↓
Service Quality
↓
Customer Satisfaction
↓
Revenue Growth
💡 Real-World Example

Ritz-Carlton empowers every employee with $2,000 per guest per day to resolve problems without manager approval — demonstrating trust and empowerment.

🧠 Memory Aid

"HIRE" — Hire the right people, Invest in training, Retain the best, Empower with support systems

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 Challenges: Boundary spanning, Emotional labour, Person/role conflict, Org/client conflict, Inter-client conflict
  • Cycle of Failure: Low wages → Low morale → Poor service → Reduced revenue
  • Cycle of Success: Invest in people → Better service → Customer loyalty → Higher revenue

Unit 9: Managing Physical Evidence

📖 Core Concept

Physical evidence = tangible cues customers use to evaluate service. Servicescape affects customer perceptions and behavior.

📊 Types of Physical Evidence
Ambient Conditions — Temperature, lighting, music, scent
Spatial Layout — Furniture, equipment, arrangement
Signs, Symbols, Artifacts — Logos, dress code, décor
Other Customers — Who else is using the service
💡 Real-World Example

Starbucks uses warm lighting, comfortable seating, and the smell of coffee to create a "third place" between home and work.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Physical evidence strategy: Recognize impact → Map journey → Clarify roles → Co-create → Update regularly
  • Link to SERVQUAL "Tangibles" dimension

Unit 10: Managing Service Process

📖 Core Concept

Service process classification based on what is being processed. Service blueprinting maps the entire delivery process.

📊 Service Process Classification
People Processing — Customer must be present (healthcare, haircut)
Possession Processing — Customer's belongings (repair, dry cleaning)
Mental Stimulus Processing — Customer's mind (education, entertainment)
Information Processing — Data (insurance, consulting)
💡 Real-World Example

Amazon's service blueprint: browsing → order → payment → delivery → returns. Each step has fail points identified and recovery processes built in.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Blueprint elements: Customer actions, Front-stage, Back-stage, Support processes, Fail points
  • Service recovery: Fix problem, provide compensation, follow up
  • 8 Customer switching reasons: pricing, inconvenience, core failure, encounter failures, recovery response, competition, ethics, involuntary

Unit 11: Managing Service Quality

📖 Core Concept

Service quality = perceptions vs. expectations. Two major models: Gronroos (Nordic) and PZB/SERVQUAL (American).

📊 SERVQUAL Model — 5 Dimensions (RATER)
Reliability — Perform promised service dependably (MOST important)
Assurance — Knowledge, courtesy, trust
Tangibles — Physical facilities, equipment
Empathy — Caring, individualized attention
Responsiveness — Willingness to help promptly
💡 Real-World Example

Domino's Pizza Tracker lets customers track their order in real-time — reducing Gap 4 by being transparent about delivery process.

🧠 Memory Aid

"RATER" — Reliability, Assurance, Tangibles, Empathy, Responsiveness
"KSCDC" — Knowledge (Gap 1), Standards (Gap 2), Communication (Gap 4), Delivery (Gap 3), Customer (Gap 5)

✅ Unit Summary

  • Gap Model: Customer Gap (Gap 5) caused by 4 Provider Gaps
  • Gap 1: Listening; Gap 2: Design; Gap 3: Delivery; Gap 4: Communication
  • Gronroos: Technical Quality (WHAT) + Functional Quality (HOW) + Image
  • Reliability is the MOST important SERVQUAL dimension

Unit 12: International Trade in Services

📖 Core Concept

Services trade governed by GATS (General Agreement on Trade in Services). India is a major player in global services.

📊 GATS 4 Modes of Service Trade
Mode 1: Cross-border — Service crosses border, provider stays (BPO, IT)
Mode 2: Consumption Abroad — Customer moves to provider (tourism, education)
Mode 3: Commercial Presence — Provider sets up in another country (FDI)
Mode 4: Movement of Persons — Individual providers move (IT professionals)
💡 Real-World Example

India's IT-BPM sector exports $194 billion annually, employing 5 million people — Mode 1 (cross-border) dominant.

🧠 Memory Aid

"CCMM" — Cross-border, Consumption abroad, Commercial presence, Movement of persons

✅ Unit Summary

  • India strengths: IT services, BPO/KPO, medical tourism, education, financial services
  • Always give India-specific examples for GATS modes in exams

Unit 13: Managing Demand and Capacity

📖 Core Concept

Services are perishable → unused capacity = lost revenue. Two strategies: change demand OR change supply.

📊 Demand-Capacity Strategies
Change Demand to Fit Supply
Differential pricing | Promotion | Reservation systems | Yield management
Change Supply to Fit Demand
Part-time staff | Cross-training | Capacity sharing | Expansion
💡 Real-World Example

Uber's surge pricing during rain or rush hour is yield management — higher prices attract more drivers and manage demand.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Yield Management: dynamic pricing to maximize revenue per available unit
  • Waiting line management: Operations (reduce actual time), Perception (make wait feel shorter), Virtual queuing
  • Common in airlines, hotels, car rentals, events

Unit 14: Emerging Issues in Marketing of Services

📖 Core Concept

Digital transformation is reshaping service delivery. Self-service technologies (SSTs), CRM, and omnichannel are key trends.

📊 Customer Path to Purchase
Awareness
↓
Interest → Consideration → Purchase
↓
Retention → Advocacy
💡 Real-World Example

Swiggy uses AI for personalized recommendations, real-time tracking, and instant refunds — exemplifying digital service transformation.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 6 Key Trends: SSTs, CRM, Customer Retention, Omnichannel, Service Ecosystems, Sustainability
  • Customer retention is 5x cheaper than acquisition
  • Platform-based services (Uber, Airbnb) are reshaping industries
