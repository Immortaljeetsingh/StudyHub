Unit 1: Marketing of Services — An Introduction

📖 Core Concepts

Economy divided into 3 sectors: Primary (agriculture), Secondary (manufacturing), Tertiary (services). USA became first "service economy" post-WWII with >50% workforce in services; now >80%. Daniel Bell coined "post-industrial society" — quality of life through services.

Service (Kotler): "Any activity or benefit that one party can offer to another that is essentially intangible and does not result in the ownership of anything."

Service (W.J. Stanton): "Separately identifiable, essentially intangible activities which provide want-satisfaction, not necessarily tied to the sale of a product."

📊 Characteristics of Services — TIPS-NP
T — Tangibility spectrum: Range from pure goods to pure services
↓
I — Inseparability: Production = Consumption simultaneously
↓
P — Perishability: Cannot be stored/inventoried
↓
S — Simultaneity: Producer and consumer interact
↓
N — Non-ownership: No transfer of title
↓
P — People as product: Human element crucial
💡 Real-World Example

Urban Company (formerly UrbanClap) created a platform connecting service providers (beauty, repair, cleaning) with customers — demonstrating how technology enables service delivery at scale.

🧠 Memory Aid

"TIPS-NP" — Tangibility, Inseparability, Perishability, Simultaneity, Non-ownership, People

✅ Unit Summary

  • Economy: Primary → Secondary → Tertiary (services)
  • 6 characteristics: TIPS-NP with marketing implications for each
  • Goods vs Services: tangible, standardized, storable vs intangible, variable, perishable
  • Growth drivers: affluence, leisure, women workforce, life expectancy, complexity, technology

Unit 2: Conceptual Framework for Services Marketing

📖 Core Concepts

Traditional 4Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) insufficient for services. Extended to 7Ps with addition of People, Process, Physical Evidence. The Service Marketing Triangle defines 3 types of marketing that must align.

External Marketing: Company → Customer (promises, advertising, branding). Internal Marketing: Company → Employee (training, motivation). Interactive Marketing: Employee → Customer (service delivery, "moment of truth").

📊 Service Marketing Triangle
COMPANY
External Marketing → CUSTOMER
Internal Marketing → EMPLOYEES
Interactive Marketing: EMPLOYEES ↔ CUSTOMER
📊 The 7Ps of Services Marketing Mix
1. Product
Core + supplementary
2. Price
Value-based pricing
3. Place
Channels & access
4. Promotion
Communication
5. People
Employees & customers
6. Process
Delivery procedures
7. Physical Evidence
Tangible cues
💡 Real-World Example

A hospital's external marketing promises "world-class care" (advertising), internal marketing trains nurses for empathy (HR), interactive marketing is the actual patient experience at reception.

🧠 Memory Aid

"EII" — External, Interactive, Internal (3 types of service marketing)

✅ Unit Summary

  • 7Ps: Product, Price, Place, Promotion, People, Process, Physical Evidence
  • Service Triangle: External, Internal, Interactive marketing
  • Lovelock classification: People/equipment, tangible/intangible actions, contact levels

Unit 3: Consumer Behaviour in Services

📖 Core Concepts

Service consumers evaluate based on expectations vs. perceptions. Three types of service qualities determine evaluation: search qualities (before purchase), experience qualities (during), credence qualities (hard to judge even after).

Zone of Tolerance: Range between desired service and adequate service. Wider for less important attributes; narrows when service is critical.

📊 Service Quality Evaluation Types
Search Qualities — Evaluate BEFORE purchase (restaurant decor, hotel lobby)
↓
Experience Qualities — Evaluate DURING consumption (food taste, flight comfort)
↓
Credence Qualities — Hard to evaluate EVEN AFTER (medical diagnosis, legal advice)
⚠️ Exam Tip

Explain how search, experience, and credence qualities affect marketing strategy differently. This is a common essay question.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Expectations vs Perceptions framework
  • 3 quality types: Search, Experience, Credence
  • Zone of Tolerance narrows for critical services
  • Expectations shaped by: WOM, past experience, needs, external communications

Unit 4: Product Decisions

📖 Core Concepts

Service product has multiple layers. Kotler's 5 Levels: Core Benefit → Basic Product → Expected Product → Augmented Product → Potential Product. Gronroos' 3 Levels: Basic Service Package → Augmented Service Offering → Market Communication.

📊 Kotler's 5 Product Levels (Hotel Example)
Level 5: Potential Product — All-suite hotel, loyalty programs
Level 4: Augmented — Room service, music, check-in/out
Level 3: Expected — Clean room, towels, quietness
Level 2: Basic Product — Room, bed, bathroom
Level 1: Core Benefit — Rest and sleep
💡 Real-World Example

A hotel's core service is lodging; facilitating = bell boy, check-in; supporting = spa, car rental. Remove facilitating = service collapses. Remove supporting = still functional but less competitive.

⚠️ Exam Tip

Compare Kotler's and Gronroos' models side-by-side. Examiners love model comparisons.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Kotler 5 levels: Core → Basic → Expected → Augmented → Potential
  • Gronroos 3 levels: Basic Package, Augmented Offering, Market Communication
  • 5 stages of new service development: Benefit Concept → Concept → Offer → Forms → Delivery

Unit 5: Pricing Decisions

📖 Core Concepts

Services pricing is harder than goods pricing due to intangibility. Three basic methods: Cost-based, Competition-based, Value-based. Eight specific strategies for services pricing.

📊 8 Pricing Strategies for Services
Skimming — High initial, lowers over time
Penetration — Low initial to gain share
Diversionary — Low basic, charge for extras
Guaranteed — Pay only on results
Loss Leader — Low initial, hope for repeat
Offset — Low basic, high for extras
High Price — Premium for perceived quality
Yield Management — Dynamic by demand
💡 Real-World Example

Jio entered with penetration pricing (free data), disrupted the market, then gradually increased prices after gaining 400M+ subscribers.

🧠 Memory Aid

"SPY GOOD" — Skimming, Penetration, Yield, Guaranteed, Offset, Other (diversionary, loss leader), Dynamic

✅ Unit Summary

  • 3 methods: Cost-based, Competition-based, Value-based
  • 8 strategies: SPY GOOD mnemonic
  • Yield management: dynamic pricing by demand (airlines, hotels, Uber)

Unit 6: Place Decisions

📖 Core Concepts

Distribution channels for services are shorter due to inseparability. Three main channel types: Direct channels (most common), Electronic channels, Intermediaries.

📊 Distribution Channels for Services
Direct Channels — Company delivers directly (hospital, salon)
↓
Electronic Channels — Website, app, digital (Netflix, banking)
↓
Intermediaries — Agents, brokers, franchises (travel agents, McDonald's)
⚠️ Exam Tip

Explain why inseparability makes direct channels most common in services. Use examples.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 3 channels: Direct, Electronic, Intermediaries
  • Key decisions: Location, accessibility, multi-channel integration
  • Technology enables 24/7 availability

Unit 7: Promotion Decisions

📖 Core Concepts

Intangibility makes service communication challenging. Must "tangibilize the intangible". Cannot show the service before purchase; must rely on tangible cues, testimonials, guarantees. Need to manage customer expectations (avoid overpromising).

📊 Promotion Mix for Services
Advertising — Use tangible cues, testimonials
Personal Selling — Critical for high-contact services
Sales Promotion — Trial offers, free consultations
Public Relations — Reputation management
Direct Marketing — Personalized communication
⚠️ Exam Tip

Discuss how to "tangibilize" a service in advertising — use physical cues, people, locations, testimonials.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Challenge: Intangibility requires tangibilization
  • 5 promotion tools adapted for services
  • IMC: Consistency across all touchpoints
  • Word-of-mouth is most powerful and least controllable

Unit 8: Managing People

📖 Core Concepts

People = 5th P of service marketing mix. Service employees are "boundary spanners" between company and customer. Emotional labour is unique to service jobs — displaying emotions not genuinely felt.

Internal Marketing: Treat employees as internal customers. Service Profit Chain: Employee Satisfaction → Service Quality → Customer Satisfaction → Revenue Growth.

📊 Cycle of Failure vs. Cycle of Success
Low wages → Low morale → Poor service
↓
Customer dissatisfaction → Cost cutting
Invest in people → Better service → Loyalty
↓
Higher revenue → More investment
💡 Real-World Example

Ritz-Carlton empowers every employee with $2,000 per guest per day to resolve problems without manager approval — demonstrating trust and empowerment.

🧠 Memory Aid

"HIRE" — Hire the right people, Invest in training, Retain the best, Empower with support systems

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 challenges: Boundary spanning, emotional labour, person/role, org/client, inter-client conflict
  • Internal marketing treats employees as internal customers
  • Service Profit Chain: Employee satisfaction drives revenue
  • Cycle of Failure vs Cycle of Success

Unit 9: Managing Physical Evidence

📖 Core Concepts

Physical evidence = tangible cues customers use to evaluate service. Servicescape affects customer perceptions and behavior. Types: Ambient conditions, Spatial layout, Signs/symbols/artifacts, Other customers.

📊 Types of Physical Evidence
Ambient Conditions — Temperature, lighting, music, scent (hotel lobby)
Spatial Layout — Furniture, equipment, arrangement (restaurant seating)
Signs, Symbols, Artifacts — Logos, dress code, décor (bank's professional look)
Other Customers — Who else is using the service (exclusive club)
💡 Real-World Example

Starbucks uses warm lighting, comfortable seating, and the smell of coffee to create a "third place" between home and work.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Physical evidence = tangible cues for service evaluation
  • 4 types: Ambient, Spatial, Signs/Symbols, Other customers
  • Link to SERVQUAL "Tangibles" dimension
  • Map customer journey and identify touchpoints

Unit 10: Managing Service Process

📖 Core Concepts

Service process classification based on what is being processed. Service blueprinting maps the entire delivery process with front-stage and back-stage elements, fail points, and wait points.

📊 Service Process Classification
People Processing — Customer must be present (healthcare, haircut)
Possession Processing — Customer's belongings (repair, dry cleaning)
Mental Stimulus Processing — Customer's mind (education, entertainment)
Information Processing — Data (insurance, consulting)
💡 Real-World Example

Amazon's service blueprint shows customer browsing → order → payment → delivery → returns — each step has fail points identified and recovery processes built in.

⚠️ Exam Tip

Draw a simple service blueprint for a familiar service (restaurant, hospital). Label front-stage and back-stage clearly.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 4 process types: People, Possession, Mental stimulus, Information
  • Service blueprint: Customer actions, front-stage, back-stage, support
  • Service recovery: Fix, compensate, follow up
  • 8 customer switching reasons

Unit 11: Managing Service Quality

📖 Core Concepts

Service quality = perceptions vs. expectations. Two major models: Gronroos (Nordic) and PZB/SERVQUAL (American). SERVQUAL measures 5 dimensions: Reliability, Assurance, Tangibles, Empathy, Responsiveness. Reliability is the MOST important dimension.

Gap Model: Customer Gap (Gap 5) caused by 4 Provider Gaps: Knowledge gap, Standards gap, Delivery gap, Communication gap.

📊 SERVQUAL Dimensions (RATER)
R — Reliability
Perform promised service dependably
A — Assurance
Knowledge, courtesy, trust
T — Tangibles
Physical facilities, equipment
E — Empathy
Caring, individualized attention
R — Responsiveness
Willingness to help promptly
📊 Service Quality Gap Model
Gap 5 (Customer Gap) — Expected Service ≠ Perceived Service
↑ caused by
Gap 1: Not knowing what customers expect
Gap 2: Not setting right service standards
Gap 3: Not delivering to service standards
Gap 4: Not matching performance to promises
💡 Real-World Example

Domino's Pizza Tracker lets customers track their order in real-time — reducing Gap 4 by being transparent about delivery process.

🧠 Memory Aid

"RATER" — Reliability, Assurance, Tangibles, Empathy, Responsiveness

"KSCDC" — Knowledge, Standards, Communication, Delivery, Customer (Gaps 1-4 + Gap 5)

✅ Unit Summary

  • SERVQUAL: RATER (5 dimensions) — Reliability most important
  • Gronroos: Technical quality + Functional quality + Image
  • Gap Model: 5 gaps with closure strategies
  • Technology closes gaps: SSTs, online feedback, AI chatbots

Unit 12: International Trade in Services

📖 Core Concepts

Services trade governed by GATS (General Agreement on Trade in Services) under WTO. India is a major player in global services, especially IT and BPO.

📊 GATS 4 Modes of Service Trade
Mode 1: Cross-border — Service crosses border, provider stays (BPO, IT services)
Mode 2: Consumption abroad — Customer moves to provider's country (tourism, education)
Mode 3: Commercial presence — Provider sets up in another country (FDI, Starbucks in India)
Mode 4: Movement of persons — Individual providers move (IT professionals abroad)
💡 Real-World Example

India's IT-BPM sector exports $194 billion annually, employing 5 million people — Mode 1 (cross-border) dominant.

🧠 Memory Aid

"CCMM" — Cross-border, Consumption abroad, Commercial presence, Movement of persons

✅ Unit Summary

  • GATS: WTO framework for international service trade
  • 4 modes: CCMM
  • India strengths: IT, BPO/KPO, healthcare, education, financial services

Unit 13: Managing Demand and Capacity

📖 Core Concepts

Services are perishable → unused capacity = lost revenue. Two strategies: change demand (differential pricing, reservations, yield management) OR change supply (part-time employees, cross-training, sharing capacity).

Yield Management: Dynamic pricing based on demand, time, and capacity to maximize revenue per available unit.

📊 Demand-Capacity Strategies
Change Demand to Fit Supply
Differential pricing, Promotion, Reservations, Yield management
Change Supply to Fit Demand
Part-time staff, Cross-training, Sharing capacity, Expansion
💡 Real-World Example

Uber's surge pricing during rain or rush hour is yield management — higher prices attract more drivers and manage demand.

⚠️ Exam Tip

Explain yield management with airline examples (same seat sold at different prices based on booking time).

✅ Unit Summary

  • Perishability: unused capacity = lost revenue
  • 2 strategies: change demand OR change supply
  • Yield management: dynamic pricing (airlines, hotels, Uber)
  • Waiting line management: operations, perception, virtual queuing

Unit 14: Emerging Issues in Marketing of Services

📖 Core Concepts

Digital transformation is reshaping service delivery. Self-service technologies (SSTs) are growing. Customer journey: Awareness → Interest → Consideration → Purchase → Retention → Advocacy.

📊 Key Emerging Trends
Self-Service Technologies (SSTs) — ATMs, online booking, self-checkout
Customer Relationship Management (CRM) — Data-driven personalization
Customer Retention — 5x cheaper to retain than acquire
Omnichannel Delivery — Seamless online + offline experience
Service Ecosystems — Platform-based (Uber, Airbnb)
Sustainability — Green services, ethical practices
💡 Real-World Example

Swiggy uses AI for personalized recommendations, real-time tracking, and instant refunds — exemplifying digital service transformation.

✅ Unit Summary

  • SSTs: ATMs, online booking, self-checkout
  • CRM: Data-driven personalization
  • Retention 5x cheaper than acquisition
  • Omnichannel: Seamless online + offline
  • Customer path: Awareness → Interest → Consideration → Purchase → Retention → Advocacy
