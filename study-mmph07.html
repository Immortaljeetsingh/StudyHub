Unit 1: Compensation and Rewards Management

📖 Core Concept

Compensation = all forms of financial returns and tangible services/benefits employees receive as part of employment relationship. Functionally viewed as: (a) system of rewards that motivates performance, (b) tool to foster values/culture/behaviour, (c) instrument to achieve business goals.

📊 Compensation Components
COMPENSATION
Direct (Financial)
Basic Pay, DA, Overtime
Incentive, Bonus, Commission
Indirect (Benefits)
PF, Pension, Gratuity, ESI
Medical, Housing, Insurance
📖 Key Definitions

Direct Compensation: Financial remuneration in cash - basic pay, DA, overtime, incentives, bonus, commissions.

Indirect Compensation (Fringe Benefits): Benefits beyond cash - PF, pension, medical insurance, sick leave, housing.

Cafeteria Plan: Employees choose benefit package from a list of available options, matching individual needs.

💡 Real-World Example

In Indian companies, identical basic wages across stations can lead to highly disparate total wages due to DA variations - this is why periodic DA merger with basic pay is recommended.

🧠 Memory Aid

DOS-PIF = Direct comp = DA, Overtime, Shift allowance | Indirect = PF, Insurance, Fringe benefits

📋 Exam Tip

Know the distinction between direct and indirect compensation and why fringe benefits have grown (tax advantages, retention, legal requirements).

✅ Unit Summary

  • Compensation = Direct (cash) + Indirect (benefits)
  • Direct: Basic pay, DA, overtime, incentives, bonus, commissions
  • Indirect: PF, pension, medical insurance, housing, ESI
  • Role: Attract, retain, motivate, develop skills, foster culture
  • DA compensates for inflation, linked to AICPI

📖 Total Rewards Framework (WorldatWork)

6 Elements: Compensation (base + variable), Benefits, Work-Life Balance, Performance & Recognition, Career Development, Talent Management.

📖 Key Theories

Equity Theory (Adams): Employees compare their Input-Outcome ratio with others. Perceived inequity leads to motivation problems. 3 types: Internal, External, Individual equity.

Expectancy Theory (Vroom): Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence. E = "My effort leads to performance", I = "Performance leads to reward", V = "How much I value the reward".

📖 Pay Strategies

Lead: Pay above market - attract top talent, low unemployment.

Lag: Pay below market - cost-cutting, high unemployment.

Match: Pay at market - standard approach, stable workforce.

🧠 Memory Aid

CBWPCT = Total Rewards: Compensation, Benefits, Work-life, Performance, Career, Talent

📋 Exam Tip

Expect questions comparing Equity Theory vs Expectancy Theory - know both frameworks and their application to compensation design.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Total Rewards has 6 elements: CBWPCT
  • Equity Theory: employees compare input-outcome ratios
  • Expectancy Theory: M = E × I × V
  • 3 pay strategies: Lead, Lag, Match (LLM)
  • Google uses Lead strategy; startups use Lag + equity

Unit 3: Economic and Behavioural Issues

📖 Economic Theories

Marginal Productivity Theory: Wages = marginal revenue product of labour.

Human Capital Theory (Becker): Investment in education/training increases productivity and justifies higher wages.

Efficiency Wage Theory: Paying above market increases productivity (reduces turnover, improves morale).

📊 Maslow's Hierarchy & Compensation
5. Self-Actualization (Career Development)

3. Social (Team Incentives)

📖 Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory

Hygiene Factors (Prevent Dissatisfaction): Salary, job security, working conditions, company policy, supervision quality.

Motivators (Create Satisfaction): Achievement, recognition, growth, responsibility, advancement, work itself.

A salary increase prevents dissatisfaction but doesn't create lasting motivation - recognition and growth drive sustained engagement.

🧠 Memory Aid

PSSES = Maslow: Physiological, Safety, Social, Esteem, Self-actualization

✅ Unit Summary

  • Human Capital Theory: investment in education justifies higher wages
  • Efficiency Wage: above-market pay increases productivity
  • Maslow maps to compensation: Basic pay, Benefits, Team incentives, Recognition, Career
  • Herzberg: Salary is hygiene, not motivator

Unit 4: International Trends

📖 Expatriate Compensation

Balance Sheet Approach: Maintain home country living standard abroad - most common for MNCs.

Going Rate Approach: Pay host country market rates - simpler but may cause repatriation issues.

📖 Modern Trends
  • Pay for Performance: Linking pay to results
  • Broad Banding: Replacing multiple grades with fewer, wider bands
  • Competency-Based Pay: Pay for skills/competencies, not just job title
  • Skill-Based Pay: Pay for acquired skills
  • Flexible Benefits (Cafeteria Plans): Employee choice from benefit menu
📋 Exam Tip

Know the difference between Balance Sheet and Going Rate approaches - frequently tested.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Balance Sheet: maintain home standard abroad
  • Going Rate: pay host country rates
  • Trends: pay for performance, broad banding, competency-based pay, cafeteria plans
  • MNCs hiring host-country managers to reduce expatriate costs

Unit 5: Bases of Wage Determination

📖 5 Factors Influencing Wages
  • Economic: Market forces, competing employers, cost of living, productivity
  • Sociological: Social status, equity for men/women
  • Political: Trade union bargaining, government intervention
  • Psychological: Motivation, job satisfaction
  • Ethical: Fair wages, living wages

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 factors: Economic, Sociological, Political, Psychological, Ethical
  • Hierarchy: Minimum Wage < Fair Wage < Living Wage
  • DA compensates for inflation, linked to CPI
  • Trade union bargaining power affects wage negotiations

Unit 6: Legal Framework

📖 Key Indian Labour Laws

Minimum Wages Act 1948: Fixes minimum wage rates.

Payment of Wages Act 1936: Timely payment, regulates deductions.

Equal Remuneration Act 1976: Equal pay for equal work (regardless of gender).

Payment of Bonus Act 1965: Statutory bonus for eligible employees.

Code on Wages 2019: Consolidates all 4 wage laws into one code.

📖 Statutory Deductions
  • PF: 12% of basic + DA (both employer & employee)
  • ESI: 0.75% employee + 3.25% employer (salary < Rs. 21,000)
  • Professional Tax: State-specific
  • Income Tax (TDS): As per tax slab
🧠 Memory Aid

MW-PW-ER-PB = Minimum Wages, Payment of Wages, Equal Remuneration, Payment of Bonus

📋 Exam Tip

Know the 4 key acts consolidated under Code on Wages 2019 and their individual purposes.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 key acts: Minimum Wages, Payment of Wages, Equal Remuneration, Payment of Bonus, Code on Wages 2019
  • PF: 12% basic + DA from both sides
  • ESI: 0.75% employee + 3.25% employer
  • Code on Wages 2019 consolidates 4 existing laws

Unit 7: Job Evaluation and Internal Equity

📖 Key Definitions

Job Evaluation: Systematic method to determine the relative worth of jobs within an organization, establishing rational pay differentials.

Internal Equity: Fair pay relationships within the organization - "Right Pay for the Right Job".

📊 Job Evaluation Methods
JOB EVALUATION
Qualitative
1. Ranking (simplest)
2. Classification (predefined grades)
Quantitative
3. Point Rating (most popular)
4. Factor Comparison (monetary values)
📖 Method Comparison
RankingSort jobs by overall complexity. Simple, quick but subjective.
ClassificationMatch jobs to predefined grades. Easy to understand but rigid.
Point RatingAssign points to 5-8 factors with degrees. Most popular method.
Factor ComparisonCompare jobs factor by factor with monetary values. Detailed but time-consuming.
💡 Real-World Example

Point Rating is the most widely used method in medium-to-large organizations because it provides systematic, defensible pay differentials.

🧠 Memory Aid

RCPF = Ranking, Classification, Point Rating, Factor Comparison

📋 Exam Tip

Know all 4 methods with advantages/disadvantages. Point Rating is the most frequently asked method.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Job Evaluation determines relative worth of JOBS, not people
  • 4 methods: RCPF - Ranking, Classification, Point Rating, Factor Comparison
  • Point Rating is most popular in medium-to-large organizations
  • Non-quantitative: Ranking, Classification
  • Quantitative: Point Rating, Factor Comparison

Unit 8: Pay Structure

📖 Core Concept

Pay Structure: Hierarchy of jobs with corresponding pay ranges, establishing internal equity. Components: Pay Grades, Pay Ranges (Min-Midpoint-Max), Range Overlap, Broad Banding.

📖 Broad Banding vs Traditional Grading
FeatureTraditionalBroad Banding
LevelsMany (10-20+)Few (4-6)
RangeNarrowVery wide
FlexibilityLowHigh
💡 Real-World Example

IT companies use broad banding to allow technical specialists to earn as much as managers without forcing them into management tracks.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 steps: Job evaluation, Grade grouping, Pay range, Price structure, Implement
  • Broad banding: fewer, wider bands replacing multiple grades
  • Range overlap allows career flexibility
  • IT companies favor broad banding for technical tracks

Unit 9: External Equity and Pay Surveys

📖 Key Concepts

External Equity: Ensuring pay competitiveness with the external market.

Pay Survey: Systematic collection of compensation data from other organizations.

Market Pricing: Setting pay based on survey data - organizations choose percentile positioning (25th = lag, 50th = match, 75th = lead).

📖 Survey Analysis Methods
  • Mean: Average pay
  • Median: Middle value (most reliable - less affected by outliers)
  • Percentiles: 25th, 50th, 75th positions
  • Regression Analysis: Predicting pay based on job size
📋 Exam Tip

Know why median is preferred over mean in pay surveys (less affected by outliers).

✅ Unit Summary

  • External equity maintained through pay surveys
  • Median preferred over mean (less affected by outliers)
  • Market pricing: 25th = lag, 50th = match, 75th = lead
  • Types: Industry-specific, regional, job-specific surveys

Unit 10: Institutional Mechanisms for Compensation

📖 4 Institutional Mechanisms

Pay Commission: Government employees. Example: 7th Pay Commission (2016) - Min Rs. 18,000, 2.57x fitment factor.

Wage Boards: Industry-specific. Example: Newspaper Wage Board, Sugar Wage Board.

Industrial Tribunals: Wage dispute adjudication.

Collective Bargaining: Unionised sectors - negotiated wages between union and management.

💡 Real-World Example

The 7th Central Pay Commission (2016) recommended 2.57x fitment factor for revision of pay for central government employees.

📋 Exam Tip

Know the difference between Pay Commission (government) and Wage Boards (industry-specific).

✅ Unit Summary

  • Pay Commission: government employees
  • Wage Boards: industry-specific
  • Industrial Tribunals: dispute adjudication
  • Collective Bargaining: unionised sectors

Unit 11: Reward Systems

📖 Types of Rewards

Intrinsic (Non-financial): Recognition, Autonomy, Growth opportunities.

Extrinsic (Financial): Pay, Bonuses, Benefits.

📖 Reward Components
  • Basic Wage: Fixed pay based on job evaluation
  • Dearness Allowance (DA): Compensates for price rise, linked to CPI
  • Other Allowances: HRA, Transport, Medical
  • Fringe Benefits: Insurance, PF, leave, housing

DA influenced by: union policies, wage board awards, labour court decisions, government policy. Periodic merger with basic pay recommended.

📋 Exam Tip

DA calculation methods and CPI linkage is a frequently tested topic.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Intrinsic: recognition, autonomy, growth
  • Extrinsic: pay, bonuses, benefits
  • DA linked to CPI, periodic merger recommended
  • Design: align with goals, ensure fairness, balance fixed/variable

📖 Key Definitions

Gainsharing: Sharing productivity gains with employees who contributed to improvements.

ESOPs: Employees get right to buy company shares at predetermined price.

Golden Parachute: Generous severance for executives in case of termination after merger/acquisition.

💡 Real-World Example

Indian IT companies widely use ESOPs to retain skilled employees - Infosys ESOPs created many employee millionaires.

🧠 Memory Aid

PCBM = Individual Incentives: Piece-rate, Commission, Bonus, Merit pay

📋 Exam Tip

Differentiate between Gainsharing (productivity-based) and Profit-sharing (profit-based).

✅ Unit Summary

  • 3 levels: Individual, Group, Executive
  • Individual: PCBM (Piece-rate, Commission, Bonus, Merit)
  • Group: Gainsharing, Profit-sharing, ESOPs
  • Executive: Stock options, Performance shares, Golden parachutes
  • Individual = measurable output; Group = collaborative work

Unit 13: Allowances, Perquisites and Benefits

📖 3 Categories
  • Allowances: DA, HRA, Transport, Medical, Education, Special Allowance
  • Perquisites/Perks: Company car, Housing, Club membership, Stock options, Paid holidays
  • Fringe Benefits: PF, ESI, Gratuity, Leave encashment, Group insurance, Retirement benefits
📖 Statutory Benefits
PF12% basic + DA (both employer & employee). Retirement savings.
ESI0.75% employee + 3.25% employer. Medical insurance (salary < Rs. 21,000).
GratuityEmployer only (15 days salary per year). After 5 years continuous service.
📖 Cafeteria Approach

Employees choose benefits from a menu of options. Simplest form: reimbursement account (tax-free). Growing trend with computerized benefit management.

💡 Real-World Example

Under Section 10(10) of Income Tax Act, gratuity up to Rs. 20 lakhs is tax-free for government employees.

📋 Exam Tip

Know the statutory contribution rates for PF and ESI - common numerical questions.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Allowances: DA, HRA, Transport, Medical
  • Perks: Car, Housing, Club, Stock options
  • PF: 12% basic + DA from both sides
  • ESI: 0.75% + 3.25% (salary < Rs. 21,000)
  • Gratuity: 15 days/year after 5 years
  • Cafeteria plan: employees choose benefits from menu
