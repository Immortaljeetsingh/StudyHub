Unit 1: Compensation and Rewards Management

📖 Core Concept

Compensation = all forms of financial returns and tangible services/benefits employees receive as part of employment relationship. Functionally viewed as: (a) system of rewards that motivates performance, (b) tool to foster values/culture/behaviour, (c) instrument to achieve business goals.

📖 Key Definitions

Direct Compensation: Financial remuneration in cash - basic pay, DA, overtime, incentives, bonus, commissions.

Indirect Compensation (Fringe Benefits): Benefits beyond cash - PF, pension, medical insurance, sick leave, housing.

Cafeteria Plan: Employees choose benefit package from a list of available options, matching individual needs.

📖 Compensation Structure Components (Indian Context)
  • Basic Wage: Fixed pay based on job evaluation; typically 40-50% of CTC in India
  • Dearness Allowance (DA): Compensates for inflation; linked to AICPI; revised twice yearly
  • House Rent Allowance (HRA): 40-50% of basic for metro cities; tax-free if rent paid
  • Conveyance/Transport: Fixed monthly amount or company transport
  • Medical Allowance: Rs. 15,000/year tax-free under Section 17(2)
  • Leave Travel Allowance (LTA): Tax-free for domestic travel (2 trips in 4 years)
  • Special Allowance: Balance amount to reach target CTC
💡 Real-World Examples

DA Variations: In Indian companies, identical basic wages across stations can lead to highly disparate total wages due to DA variations - a worker in Mumbai with the same basic as one in a smaller city may earn significantly more due to higher DA for metro areas.

CTC Breakdown Example (Indian IT): For a CTC of Rs. 10 lakhs: Basic = Rs. 4 lakhs, HRA = Rs. 2 lakhs, Special Allowance = Rs. 2.5 lakhs, PF (employer) = Rs. 48,000, Gratuity = Rs. 19,230, Medical = Rs. 15,000, LTA = Rs. 20,000.

Fringe Benefits Growth: In India, fringe benefits have grown from 15-20% of total compensation in the 1990s to 25-35% today, driven by tax advantages, talent competition, and statutory requirements.

🧠 Memory Aid

DOS-PIF = Direct comp = DA, Overtime, Shift allowance | Indirect = PF, Insurance, Fringe benefits

📋 Exam Tip

Know the distinction between direct and indirect compensation and why fringe benefits have grown (tax advantages, retention, legal requirements). CTC breakdown is a practical Indian example - know the components and their proportions.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Compensation = Direct (cash) + Indirect (benefits)
  • Direct: Basic pay, DA, overtime, incentives, bonus, commissions
  • Indirect: PF, pension, medical insurance, housing, ESI
  • Role: Attract, retain, motivate, develop skills, foster culture
  • DA compensates for inflation, linked to AICPI; revised twice yearly
  • Indian CTC: Basic (40-50%) + HRA + Special Allowance + PF + Gratuity + Benefits
  • Fringe benefits grew from 15-20% to 25-35% of total compensation in India

📖 Equity Theory (Adams, 1963)

Employees compare their Input-Outcome ratio with others. Perceived inequity leads to motivation problems.

3 Types of Equity:

  • Internal Equity: Fair pay within the organization
  • External Equity: Competitive with market
  • Individual Equity: Fair person-to-person comparisons

Equity Flow: Employee Input → Compare Ratio → Perceived Equity/Inequity → Behaviour Change (adjust effort, seek raise, or quit)

📖 Expectancy Theory (Vroom)

Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence

  • E (Expectancy): "My effort leads to performance"
  • I (Instrumentality): "Performance leads to reward"
  • V (Valence): "How much I value the reward"

All three must be high for motivation to work. If any one is zero, motivation is zero.

📖 Compensation Philosophy - 3 Strategies
StrategyDescriptionWhen to Use
LeadPay above marketAttract top talent, low unemployment
LagPay below marketCost-cutting, high unemployment
MatchPay at marketStandard approach, stable workforce
📖 Equity Theory - Indian Examples
  • Internal Equity: A software engineer at TCS should earn comparably to a similar role at Infosys within the same company
  • External Equity: If market rate for Java developers is Rs. 8 lakhs, paying Rs. 6 lakhs will cause attrition to competitors
  • Individual Equity: Two project managers with similar experience should not have vastly different salaries

Inequity Responses (Adams): When employees perceive inequity, they may: (1) reduce effort, (2) seek pay raise, (3) rationalize, (4) change comparison target, or (5) quit the organization.

📋 Exam Tip

Expect questions comparing Equity Theory vs Expectancy Theory - know both frameworks and their application to compensation design. Equity Theory focuses on fairness comparisons; Expectancy Theory focuses on effort-reward linkage.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Total Rewards has 6 elements: CBWPCT
  • Equity Theory: employees compare input-outcome ratios; 3 types: internal, external, individual
  • Expectancy Theory: M = E × I × V (all must be high)
  • 3 pay strategies: Lead, Lag, Match (LLM)
  • Google uses Lead strategy; startups use Lag + equity
  • Inequity responses: reduce effort, seek raise, rationalize, change target, quit

Unit 3: Economic and Behavioural Issues

📖 Economic Issues in Compensation
  • Demand & Supply of Labour: Market forces determine wage levels
  • Marginal Productivity Theory: Wages = marginal revenue product of labour
  • Human Capital Theory (Becker): Investment in education/training increases productivity and justifies higher wages
  • Efficiency Wage Theory: Paying above market increases productivity (reduces turnover, improves morale)

📖 Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory

Hygiene Factors (Prevent Dissatisfaction): Salary, job security, working conditions, company policy, supervision quality.

Motivators (Create Satisfaction): Achievement, recognition, growth, responsibility, advancement, work itself.

A salary increase prevents dissatisfaction but doesn't create lasting motivation - recognition and growth drive sustained engagement.

📖 Efficiency Wage Theory - Indian Context
  • Core Idea: Paying above market-clearing wage increases productivity through:
    • Reduced turnover (lower hiring/training costs)
    • Improved morale and effort
    • Attracting better quality applicants
    • Reduced "shirking" (employees work harder to keep good job)
  • Indian Example: Infosys and TCS pay 15-20% above market average - this reduces attrition from 20%+ industry average to 12-15%, saving significant recruitment costs
💡 Real-World Examples

Human Capital Theory: In India, IIT/IIM graduates command 2-3x higher starting salaries than regional engineering college graduates - this premium reflects the human capital investment (better education, skills, network).

Herzberg Application: Indian IT companies discovered that just increasing salaries (hygiene) didn't reduce attrition - they had to add motivators like challenging projects, career growth paths, and recognition programs (e.g., Infosys "Insta Awards", Wipro "Spirit of Wipro").

✅ Unit Summary

  • Human Capital Theory: investment in education justifies higher wages (IIT/IIM premium)
  • Efficiency Wage: above-market pay increases productivity (Infosys, TCS example)
  • Maslow maps to compensation: Basic pay, Benefits, Team incentives, Recognition, Career
  • Herzberg: Salary is hygiene, not motivator; recognition and growth are motivators
  • Indian IT: salary alone doesn't reduce attrition - need motivators (growth, recognition)

Expatriate Pay: Indian IT companies (TCS, Infosys, Wipro) send thousands of employees abroad annually. They typically use the Balance Sheet approach - maintaining Indian salary + COLA + housing + hardship allowances for US/EU/Middle East postings.

Cafeteria Plans: In India, companies like ICICI Bank, Infosys, and HUL offer flexible benefit plans where employees choose between HRA, LTA, food coupons, NPS contributions, and medical insurance from a fixed budget.

  • Trends: pay for performance, broad banding, flexible benefits

📖 Key Wage Concepts (Indian Context)
  • Organisation's Ability to Pay: Profitable companies (Reliance, TCS) can afford higher wages; struggling firms pay minimum
  • Prevailing Market Rate: Indian IT sector benchmarks against NASSCOM salary surveys
  • Cost of Living: Adjusted through DA linked to AICPI; metro cities have higher HRA

💡 Real-World Examples

Fair Wage Example: A factory worker in Mumbai may earn Rs. 15,000 (minimum wage) while a similar worker in a profitable company earns Rs. 25,000 (fair wage) - the difference reflects the organization's ability to pay and market competition.

Living Wage: The 15th Indian Labour Conference (1957) defined living wage as one that provides for "a decent standard of living" including food, clothing, housing, education, medical, and some recreation.

📖 Statutory Deductions
  • PF: 12% of basic + DA (both employer & employee)
  • ESI: 0.75% employee + 3.25% employer (salary < Rs. 21,000)
  • Professional Tax: State-specific
  • Income Tax (TDS): As per tax slab
📖 EPF - Employee Provident Fund (Detailed)
  • EPF Act 1952: Applies to establishments with 20+ employees
  • Employee Contribution: 12% of basic + DA (mandatory, deducted from salary)
  • Employer Contribution: 12% of basic + DA (split: 8.33% to EPS pension, 3.67% to EPF)
  • EPF Interest Rate: Declared annually by EPFO (typically 8.1% - 8.5%)
  • Withdrawal: Full withdrawal on retirement; partial for housing, medical, education
  • Tax Benefit: Section 80C deduction for employee contribution; employer contribution tax-free
  • UAN (Universal Account Number): Portable across jobs - single account for life
📖 ESI - Employee State Insurance (Detailed)
  • ESI Act 1948: Social security for workers in organized sector
  • Eligibility: Employees earning up to Rs. 21,000/month (Rs. 25,000 for disabled)
  • Employee Contribution: 0.75% of gross salary
  • Employer Contribution: 3.25% of gross salary
  • Benefits Provided:
    • Medical benefit (self + family)
    • Sickness benefit (70% wages for 91 days)
    • Maternity benefit (full wages for 26 weeks)
    • Disablement benefit
    • Dependants' benefit (fatal injury)
    • Funeral expenses

✅ Unit Summary

  • EPF: 12% basic + DA from both sides; employer split: 8.33% EPS + 3.67% EPF

Unit 8: Pay Structure

📖 Pay Structure Components
  • Pay Grades: Groups of jobs with similar worth
  • Pay Ranges: Min → Midpoint → Max for each grade
  • Range Overlap: Overlap between adjacent grades
  • Broad Banding: Replacing multiple grades with fewer, wider bands

5 Steps to Design Pay Structure:

  1. Conduct job evaluation
  2. Group jobs into grades
  3. Determine pay range for each grade
  4. Price the structure (using market data)
  5. Implement and maintain

💡 Real-World Examples

Broad Banding in IT: Companies like Infosys, TCS, and Wipro use broad banding to allow technical specialists (architects, principal engineers) to earn as much as directors without forcing them into management tracks.

Indian Pay Grades: Central government uses Pay Matrix (7th CPC) with 18 levels (Level 1: Rs. 18,000 to Level 18: Rs. 2,50,000). PSUs like ONGC, BHEL use structured grade systems with defined pay ranges.

Range Overlap Example: A senior software engineer (Grade 5) at the top of their range may earn more than a newly promoted team lead (Grade 6) at the bottom - this overlap allows lateral moves without immediate pay cuts.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 steps: Job evaluation, Grade grouping, Pay range, Price structure, Implement
  • Broad banding: fewer, wider bands replacing multiple grades
  • Range overlap allows career flexibility
  • IT companies favor broad banding for technical tracks
  • 7th CPC Pay Matrix: 18 levels from Rs. 18,000 to Rs. 2,50,000
  • PSUs use structured grade systems with defined pay ranges

Unit 9: External Equity and Pay Surveys

📖 Key Concepts

External Equity: Ensuring pay competitiveness with the external market.

Pay Survey: Systematic collection of compensation data from other organizations.

Market Pricing: Setting pay based on survey data - organizations choose percentile positioning (25th = lag, 50th = match, 75th = lead).

Types of Pay Surveys:

  • Industry-specific surveys
  • Regional surveys
  • Job-specific surveys
▶
Pay Equity
▶
Equal Pay
📖 Survey Analysis Methods
  • Mean: Average pay
  • Median: Middle value (most reliable - less affected by outliers)
  • Percentiles: 25th, 50th, 75th positions
  • Regression Analysis: Predicting pay based on job size
📖 Survey Design Considerations
  • Job Matching: Match survey jobs to internal jobs based on job content, not title
  • Data Sources: Salary.com, Glassdoor, NASSCOM (IT), AIMA, Mercer, Aon Hewitt
  • Confidentiality: Survey data must be aggregated to protect individual company data
  • Frequency: Annual surveys typical; quarterly for high-turnover sectors
  • Scope: Industry, geography, company size, job family
📖 Compensable Factors in Surveys
  • Base Salary: Fixed pay component
  • Total Cash: Base + variable pay (bonuses, incentives)
  • Total Compensation: Total cash + benefits + perquisites
  • Total Rewards: Total compensation + non-financial rewards
💡 Real-World Examples

Indian Pay Surveys: NASSCOM conducts annual salary surveys for IT/BPO sector; AIMA surveys for management positions; Mercer and Aon Hewitt provide comprehensive compensation benchmarks for MNCs in India.

Market Pricing Example: An Indian IT company positioning at 75th percentile (Lead strategy) for software engineers will pay more than 75% of competitors - this attracts top talent but increases costs.

Regression Analysis: Used to predict pay based on job size (points from job evaluation). Creates a "pay line" - jobs above the line are overpaid, below are underpaid relative to market.

📋 Exam Tip

Know why median is preferred over mean in pay surveys (less affected by outliers). Understand the difference between base salary, total cash, total compensation, and total rewards. Job matching based on content (not title) is crucial for survey accuracy.

✅ Unit Summary

  • External equity maintained through pay surveys
  • Median preferred over mean (less affected by outliers)
  • Market pricing: 25th = lag, 50th = match, 75th = lead
  • Types: Industry-specific, regional, job-specific surveys
  • Job matching based on content, not title
  • Indian surveys: NASSCOM (IT), AIMA, Mercer, Aon Hewitt
  • Compensable factors: base salary, total cash, total compensation, total rewards
  • Regression analysis creates pay line to identify overpaid/underpaid jobs

Industrial Disputes Act, 1947: Provides for conciliation, arbitration, and adjudication of industrial disputes including wage disputes. Labour Courts and Industrial Tribunals handle wage-related cases.

📋 Exam Tip

📖 Reward Components
  • Basic Wage: Fixed pay based on job evaluation
  • Dearness Allowance (DA): Compensates for price rise, linked to CPI
  • Other Allowances: HRA, Transport, Medical
  • Fringe Benefits: Insurance, PF, leave, housing

📖 Designing Effective Reward Systems
  • Alignment: Reward system must align with organizational goals and strategy
  • Fairness: Internal equity (within org) and external equity (with market)
  • Balance: Fixed vs variable pay ratio (typically 70:30 in India for managers)
  • Transparency: Employees should understand how pay is determined
  • Communication: Total reward statements showing full compensation value
💡 Real-World Examples

DA in India: DA is revised twice yearly (January and July) based on AICPI (All-India Consumer Price Index). When DA exceeds 50% of basic, it is typically merged into basic pay - this happened in 2004 and 2007 for central government employees.

Recognition Programs: Tata Group has the "Tata Innovista" awards for innovation; Infosys has "Infy Awards" for outstanding performers; L&T has "L&T Best Employee" awards with monetary rewards.

Total Reward Statements: Companies like HUL, P&G India provide annual total reward statements showing employees the full value of their compensation (base + variable + benefits + perks).

📋 Exam Tip

DA calculation methods and CPI linkage is a frequently tested topic. Know that DA merger happens when DA exceeds 50% of basic. Intrinsic rewards (recognition, growth) are increasingly important for knowledge workers.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Intrinsic: recognition, autonomy, growth
  • Extrinsic: pay, bonuses, benefits
  • DA linked to AICPI, revised twice yearly (Jan/Jul), merger at 50% of basic
  • Design: align with goals, ensure fairness, balance fixed/variable (70:30 typical)

📖 Key Definitions

Gainsharing: Sharing productivity gains with employees who contributed to improvements.

ESOPs (Employee Stock Options Plans): Employees get right to buy company shares at predetermined price.

Golden Parachute: Generous severance for executives in case of termination after merger/acquisition.

📖 ESOPs in India - Detailed
  • Regulation: Governed by SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits) Regulations, 2014
  • Vesting Period: Minimum 1 year; typically 3-4 years with graded vesting
  • Exercise Window: Usually 3-7 years after vesting
  • Tax Impact: Perquisite tax at exercise (FMV - Exercise price); Capital gains at sale
  • Why Companies Use ESOPs:
    • Retain talent in competitive markets (especially IT/startups)
    • Align employee interests with shareholders
    • Conserve cash (pay less salary, give equity)
    • Reward long-term commitment
  • Famous Indian ESOP Stories:
    • Infosys (2000s): Early employees with ESOPs became crorepatis as stock rose 100x
    • Flipkart (2018): Walmart acquisition paid out Rs. 6,000+ crore to ESOP holders
    • Zomato (2021): IPO made many employee ESOP holders wealthy
    • Paytm (2021): Pre-IPO ESOP holders saw significant gains
💡 Real-World Examples

ESOPs: Indian IT companies widely use ESOPs to retain skilled employees. Infosys ESOPs created many employee millionaires in the 2000s. When Flipkart was acquired by Walmart in 2018, employees with ESOPs received payouts worth crores.

Profit-Sharing: India's Payment of Bonus Act, 1965 mandates statutory bonus (8.33% to 20% of wages) for employees earning up to Rs. 21,000/month - this is a form of mandatory profit-sharing.

Gainsharing: Tata Steel's manufacturing units use gainsharing where production teams share cost savings from improved efficiency.

🧠 Memory Aid

PCBM = Individual Incentives: Piece-rate, Commission, Bonus, Merit pay

📋 Exam Tip

Differentiate between Gainsharing (productivity-based, team level) and Profit-sharing (profit-based, company level). ESOPs are the most tested incentive - know vesting, exercise, and taxation. Statutory bonus under Payment of Bonus Act is a form of mandatory profit-sharing in India.

📖 Gratuity - Detailed (Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972)
  • Eligibility: Employees with 5+ years continuous service (in same or multiple establishments under same employer)
  • Who Pays: Employer only - no employee contribution
  • Formula: Gratuity = (Last drawn salary × 15 × Years of service) / 26
  • Last Drawn Salary: Basic + DA (not including HRA, bonuses, other allowances)
  • Maximum Limit: Rs. 20 lakhs (tax-free for government employees under Section 10(10))
  • Tax Treatment:
    • Government employees: Fully tax-free (no upper limit)
    • Private sector (covered under Act): Tax-free up to Rs. 20 lakhs
    • Private sector (not covered): Tax-free up to Rs. 10 lakhs
  • Forfeiture: Gratuity can be forfeited if employee terminated for riotous/violent behaviour or moral turpitude
📊 Gratuity Calculation Example

Scenario: Employee with Basic + DA = Rs. 50,000/month, 12 years of service

Calculation: (50,000 × 15 × 12) / 26 = Rs. 3,46,154

This amount is tax-free up to Rs. 20 lakhs limit.

📖 ESOPs - Employee Stock Options (Indian Context)
  • What: Right to buy company shares at a predetermined (exercise) price after a vesting period
  • Vesting Period: Typically 1-4 years; employee must stay to exercise options
  • Tax Treatment (India):
    • At exercise: Perquisite tax (difference between FMV and exercise price) taxed as salary income
    • At sale: Capital gains tax on difference between sale price and FMV at exercise
  • Indian Companies Using ESOPs:
    • Infosys: Created many employee millionaires through ESOPs in early 2000s
    • Wipro: Regular ESOP grants to retain tech talent
    • TCS: Performance-linked stock grants
    • Flipkart: ESOPs worth crores for early employees at Walmart acquisition
    • Startups: ESOPs used as compensation substitute for cash-poor startups
📖 Cafeteria Approach

Employees choose benefits from a menu of options. Simplest form: reimbursement account (tax-free). Growing trend with computerized benefit management.

Indian Companies with Cafeteria Plans: ICICI Bank, Infosys, Wipro, and other large IT/financial companies offer flexible benefit plans where employees can allocate a fixed budget across HRA, LTA, medical, food coupons, NPS, etc.

💡 Real-World Examples

Gratuity: Under Section 10(10) of Income Tax Act, gratuity up to Rs. 20 lakhs is tax-free for government employees. Private sector employees covered under the Payment of Gratuity Act also get Rs. 20 lakhs exemption.

ESOPs: When Flipkart was acquired by Walmart in 2018, employees with ESOPs received payouts worth crores - some junior employees became millionaires overnight. This is why ESOPs are called the "golden handcuffs" of Indian IT.

EPF: TCS and Infosys contribute to EPF for all employees regardless of salary, going beyond the statutory minimum - this is a key employee retention strategy.

📋 Exam Tip

Know the statutory contribution rates for PF and ESI - common numerical questions. Gratuity formula is frequently tested: (Last salary × 15 × Years) / 26. Remember ESOP taxation: perquisite at exercise, capital gains at sale.

⚡ Quick Revision Sheet

📖 Key Formulas
  • Motivation (Vroom): M = E × I × V
  • Equity Ratio: Outcomes(self)/Inputs(self) vs Outcomes(other)/Inputs(other)
  • PF Contribution: 12% of (Basic + DA)
  • Gratuity: (Last salary × 15 × Years of service) / 26
  • DA Merger: When DA exceeds 50% of basic, typically merged into basic
  • ESI: 0.75% employee + 3.25% employer (salary < Rs. 21,000)
  • EPF Split: 12% total = 3.67% EPF + 8.33% EPS (employer side)

✅ One-Liner Revision

  • Compensation = Direct (cash) + Indirect (benefits)
  • Total Rewards has 6 elements: CBWPCT
  • Equity Theory: employees compare input-outcome ratios (internal, external, individual)
  • Expectancy Theory: E × I × V = Motivation (all must be high)
  • 3 pay strategies: Lead, Lag, Match (LLM)
  • 4 JE methods: RCPF - Ranking, Classification, Point Rating, Factor Comparison
  • Point Rating is the most popular JE method
  • Job Evaluation evaluates the JOB, not the person (vs Merit Rating)
  • Median preferred over mean in pay surveys (less affected by outliers)
  • Intrinsic: recognition, autonomy, growth
  • Extrinsic: pay, bonuses, benefits
  • Individual incentives: PCBM (Piece-rate, Commission, Bonus, Merit)
  • Group incentives: Gainsharing (productivity), Profit-sharing (profits), ESOPs (ownership)
