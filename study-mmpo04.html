What is MIS?
▶
MIS Overview
  • IS: People + procedures + hardware + software + data + networks for collecting, storing, processing, disseminating information
  • Data vs Information: Raw facts (data) > Processed meaningful context (information)
  • IS Types (T-M-D-E-E): TPS (operational), MIS (reports), DSS (analytical), EIS (dashboards), ESS (AI-powered)
  • IS Components: Hardware, Software, Data, Procedures, People, Networks
  • Competitive Advantage: Cost leadership, differentiation, innovation through IS
▶
PERT and CPM Explained
▶
Critical Path Method
📖 Key Definitions

IS: Organized combination of people, hardware, software, networks, data, and policies that stores, retrieves, transforms, and disseminates information

TPS: System that records and processes daily business transactions

DSS: Interactive system supporting semi-structured and unstructured decision-making

💡 Real-World Example

Amazon uses TPS for order processing, MIS for daily sales reports, DSS for demand forecasting, EIS for executive dashboards showing KPIs.

🧠 Memory Aid

"T-M-D-E-E" = TPS, MIS, DSS, EIS, ESS (bottom to top of organization)

⭐ Exam Tip

IS types (TPS, MIS, DSS, EIS, ESS) and their characteristics are the most frequently tested. Always give examples.

✅ Unit Summary

  • IS = People + HW + SW + Data + Procedures + Networks
  • 5 IS types from operational (TPS) to strategic (ESS)
  • IS enables competitive advantage through cost leadership and differentiation

  • Waterfall: Sequential, rigid, well-defined requirements

📊 SDLC Waterfall Model
Planning (Feasibility Study)
↓
Analysis (Requirements Gathering)
↓
Design (System Architecture)
↓
Implementation (Coding & Testing)
↓
