Q1. Define corporate planning. Explain the five paradigm shifts in the evolution of corporate management.

Answer:Corporate planning, as defined by Peter Drucker, is a continuous process of making entrepreneurial decisions systematically with the best possible knowledge of their futurity. Hussey adds that it is not a technique but a complete way of running a business. Corporate management goes further, encompassing planning plus implementation plus evaluation and control of plans. The evolution of corporate management can be traced through five paradigm shifts, remembered by the mnemonic A-P-E-C-C: Adhocism (till 1930), where decisions were reactive and exigency-driven; Planned Policy, which emerged after the Great Depression to anticipate contingencies; the Environment-Strategy Interface, where strategy copes with environmental forces; Corporate Planning, moving from environmental appraisal to strategic alternatives; and finally Corporate Management, which integrates implementation and control. Reliance Industries under Mukesh Ambani illustrates this journey, planning Jio's entry into telecom years in advance and evaluating performance through subscriber growth metrics. For scoring marks, present the five shifts chronologically and contrast the definitions of Drucker and Hussey in your answer.

  • Answer: Corporate planning, as defined by Peter Drucker, is a continuous process of making entrepreneurial decisions systematically with the best possible knowledge of their futurity.
  • Reliance Industries under Mukesh Ambani illustrates this journey, planning Jio's entry into telecom years in advance and evaluating performance through subscriber growth metrics.
  • Hussey adds that it is not a technique but a complete way of running a business.

Q2. Explain Ansoff's product-market expansion grid with suitable examples of each quadrant.

Answer:Ansoff's grid is a 2x2 matrix that classifies growth strategies on the basis of products and markets. Market Penetration uses existing products in existing markets and is the least risky; a firm can increase purchase frequency or attract competitors' customers. Market Development introduces existing products to new markets, such as new geographies or segments. Product Development creates new products for existing markets and requires R&D investment. Diversification involves new products in new markets and is the most risky. Amazon exemplifies all four quadrants: increasing Prime subscriptions in existing markets (penetration), expanding to India and the Middle East (market development), launching AWS cloud services for existing customers (product development), and entering entertainment through Amazon Studios (diversification). The grid also connects to the four generic corporate strategies of stability, growth, retrenchment and combination. A good assignment answer states the risk level of each quadrant and includes at least one Indian example, such as ITC moving from cigarettes into packaged foods and hotels.

Key points:

  • Answer: Ansoff's grid is a 2x2 matrix that classifies growth strategies on the basis of products and markets.

Q3. What are strategic alliances? Explain their types and how they differ from mergers and acquisitions.

Answer:A strategic alliance is a cooperative agreement between two or more firms to achieve shared objectives while remaining independent organisations. There are four main types, remembered as J-E-N-C. A Joint Venture creates a new entity with shared ownership, control and risk. An Equity Strategic Alliance involves one partner purchasing equity in the other, creating a financial bond with partial control. A Non-equity Alliance shares resources through contracts without creating a new entity, offering low control but low risk. A Consortium pools resources of several firms for a common project, common in large infrastructure ventures. Alliances differ fundamentally from mergers and acquisitions: M&A results in one entity absorbing another and surrendering independence, whereas alliances preserve each partner's identity and autonomy. Firms form alliances to share risk and resources, access new markets and technology, achieve economies of scale and circumvent government restrictions. The Starbucks-Tata joint venture in India is a classic example: Tata contributed local market knowledge and sourcing while Starbucks brought its global brand, and both companies retained their independence. Compare the control-investment-risk profile of each type.

Key points:

  • A Joint Venture creates a new entity with shared ownership, control and risk.

Q4. Explain the SECI model of knowledge management with examples of each knowledge conversion.

Answer:Knowledge management is the process of creating, capturing, storing, sharing and applying organisational knowledge to improve performance. Knowledge is information plus experience plus context, and exists in two forms: tacit knowledge, which is personal and hard to formalise, and explicit knowledge, which is codified and easily transmitted. Nonaka's SECI model describes four modes of converting knowledge between these forms. Socialization converts tacit to tacit through training, mentoring and observation. Externalization converts tacit to explicit by documenting best practices and writing manuals. Combination converts explicit to explicit by combining databases and reports into new knowledge. Internalization converts explicit to tacit through learning by doing and practice. Infosys operates exactly this cycle through its knowledge management system: employees document project learnings (externalization), share best practices through wikis (combination), transfer tacit knowledge through mentoring and video platforms (socialization), and apply learning on new projects (internalization). Benefits of KM include reduced rework, faster decision-making, improved innovation and competitive advantage, though cultural resistance and knowledge hoarding remain challenges. Draw the four-quadrant SECI diagram in your assignment.
