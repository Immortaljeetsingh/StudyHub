Unit 1: Concept and Approaches to Industrial Relations

📖 Core Definition

Industrial Relations = relationship between employers and employees in the course of employment. ILO: relationships between the state, employers' and employees' organisations. IR encompasses cooperation, negotiation, and mutual adjustment.

✅ Unit 1 Summary

  • IR = employer-employee-government relationship
  • 4 approaches: Unitary, Pluralistic, Marxist, Systems
  • Dunlop: Actors + Contexts + Ideology = Rules

Unit 8: Managerial Unionism

📖 Definition

Managerial Unions = associations of supervisors, managers, and officers. NOT traditional trade unions — represent white-collar/professionals.

Examples in India

📋 Organised Sectors
  • AIBOC (Bank officers)
  • IMA (Medical doctors)
  • Airline Pilots' Associations
📋 Emerging
  • IT employee forums
  • Gig worker associations
💡 Real-World Example

AIBOC successfully negotiated salary revisions for bank officers in bipartite settlements.

🧠 Memory Aid

BAM — Bank officers, Airline pilots, Medical doctors.

✅ Unit 8 Summary

  • Managerial unions = white-collar associations
  • Key issue: right to collective bargaining for managers
  • AIBOC, IMA, pilots' associations as examples

Unit 9: Employment Relations in Non-Union Firms

📖 Definition

Non-Union Firms = companies without formal trade unions (common in IT, start-ups, MNCs). They use alternative mechanisms.

Alternative Mechanisms

MechanismHow It Works
Employee Participation ForumsCommittees, town halls, suggestion schemes
Open-Door PoliciesDirect access to management
HR-Driven WelfareBenefits, wellness, perks
ESOPsFinancial stake in company
✅ Advantages
  • Faster decisions, flexible policies
  • Direct communication, individual recognition
⚠ Challenges
  • No collective voice, management dominance
  • Arbitrary decisions, exploitation risk
💡 Real-World Example

Infosys/TCS historically non-union — but NITES (2021) formed, showing IT workers seeking collective representation.

🧠 Memory Aid

E-O-H-D-E — Employee forums, Open-door, HR welfare, Direct communication, ESOPs.

✅ Unit 9 Summary

  • Non-union firms use alternative mechanisms
  • Pros: speed, flexibility. Cons: no collective voice
  • IT sector: NITES formed 2021

Unit 10: Collective Bargaining

📖 Definition

Collective Bargaining: Process of negotiation between employers and employees for determining terms of employment — ILO. Coined by Sidney & Beatrice Webb (1897).

4 Types of Bargaining

TypeNatureOutcome
DistributiveWin-lose (zero-sum)One side gains
IntegrativeWin-win (expand pie)Both benefit
AttitudinalRelationship buildingCreates trust
Intra-organisationalWithin each partyInternal consensus
💡 Real-World Example

2019 Tata Steel-Workers' Union settlement was integrative — productivity-linked pay (company) in exchange for job security (workers).

🧠 Memory Aid

DIAI — Distributive, Integrative, Attitudinal, Intra-organisational.

✅ Unit 10 Summary

  • CB = negotiation for employment terms
  • 4 types: DIAI. Integrative is ideal
  • 5 functions: bipartism, flexibility, peace, democracy, equalisation

Unit 12: Negotiation

📖 Definition

Negotiation: Process of discussion between two or more parties aimed at reaching a mutually acceptable agreement.

Negotiation Strategies

StrategyApproachOutcome
Win-LoseHard bargainingOne winner
Win-WinProblem-solvingBoth benefit
CompromiseGive and takeBoth give up
AvoidanceSidestepNo resolution

Fisher & Ury's Principled Negotiation

📊 4 Principles
1. Separate
People from Problem
2. Focus
Interests not Positions
3. Generate
Options for Mutual Gain
4. Use
Objective Criteria
💡 Real-World Example

2012 Maruti Manesar — failure to separate people from problem (personal animosity) escalated wage dispute into violence.

🧠 Memory Aid

W-W-C-A-A — Win-lose, Win-win, Compromise, Avoidance, Accommodation. S-F-G-U — Separate, Focus, Generate, Use (Fisher & Ury).

✅ Unit 12 Summary

  • 5 strategies: W-W-C-A-A. BATNA = your fallback
  • Fisher & Ury: Separate, Focus, Generate, Use
  • Win-win (integrative) is the ideal approach
