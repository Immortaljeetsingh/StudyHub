Unit 1: Basic Concepts and Methods

📖 Definitions

ISO 8402: "Totality of features and characteristics of a product or service that bears on its ability to satisfy stated or implied needs"

Deming: "Quality is a predictable degree of uniformity and dependability, at low cost, suited to the market"

Juran: "Fitness for use"

Crosby: "Conformance to requirements"

Feigenbaum: "Total composite product and service characteristics through which the product in use will meet customer expectations"

TQM: A management philosophy and company-wide approach focusing on continuous improvement to meet or exceed customer expectations

📖 8 TQM Principles (C-T-P-I-S-C-F-C)

1. Customer focus • 2. Total employee involvement • 3. Process-centered approach • 4. Integrated system • 5. Strategic and systematic approach • 6. Continual improvement • 7. Fact-based decision making • 8. Communications

📊 Evolution of Quality

💡 Real-World Example

Toyota's production system is a classic TQM implementation — every worker is empowered to stop the production line (andon cord) if they detect a defect, embodying the "total employee involvement" principle.

🧠 Memory Aid

D-J-C-F = Deming(predictable), Juran(fitness), Crosby(conformance), Feigenbaum(totality)

✅ Unit Summary

  • Quality has multiple definitions — Deming = predictability, Juran = fitness, Crosby = conformance, Feigenbaum = totality
  • TQM evolved from Inspection (1920s) → SQC (1940s) → QA (1960s) → TQM (1980s)
  • 8 TQM Principles: Customer focus, Total involvement, Process-centered, Integrated, Strategic, Continuous improvement, Fact-based, Communications

Unit 2: Quality Management — Leading Thinkers

📖 6 Quality Gurus

Deming: System thinking & statistical variation; 85% of quality problems are management's responsibility.

Juran: Quality Trilogy (Planning, Control, Improvement) — project-by-project improvement.

Crosby: "Zero Defects" and "Quality is Free" — prevention costs less than failure.

Ishikawa: Fishbone diagram, quality circles, company-wide quality control.

Taguchi: Robust design and loss function — any deviation from target causes societal loss.

Feigenbaum: "Total Quality Control" — quality is everyone's job across all departments.

📖 Juran's Quality Trilogy & Crosby's 4 Absolutes

Juran Trilogy: 1. Quality Planning → 2. Quality Control → 3. Quality Improvement

Crosby's 4 Absolutes: 1. Quality = conformance to requirements • 2. Prevention = the system • 3. Zero defects = the standard • 4. Cost of quality = the measurement

Crosby's Vaccine: Determination → Education → Implementation

💡 Real-World Example

Motorola invented Six Sigma in the 1980s using Taguchi's robust design principles, achieving 3.4 defects per million opportunities and saving billions.

🧠 Memory Aid

D-J-C-I-T-F = Deming, Juran, Crosby, Ishikawa, Taguchi, Feigenbaum — all 6 gurus

✅ Unit Summary

  • Deming = system & variation (PDCA, 14 Points) • Juran = project-by-project (Quality Trilogy)
  • Crosby = zero defects (4 Absolutes) • Ishikawa = fishbone & quality circles
  • Taguchi = robust design & loss function • Feigenbaum = total quality control

Unit 4: Economics of Quality

📖 Key Definitions

Cost of Quality (COQ): Total cost of ensuring quality — prevention + appraisal + failure costs.

COPQ: Hidden and visible costs when products fail to meet standards — typically 15-25% of sales revenue.

📊 4 Quality Cost Categories (P-A-I-E)
CategoryWhenExamplesRelative Cost
PreventionBefore productionTraining, quality planning, process designLowest
AppraisalDuring productionInspection, testing, auditsMedium
Internal FailureBefore deliveryScrap, rework, re-inspectionHigh
External FailureAfter deliveryWarranty claims, returns, recallsHighest
📊 Deming's Chain Reaction
Improve Quality
↓
Costs Decrease → Productivity Improves
↓
Market Share Increases → Stay in Business
💡 Real-World Example

Philip Crosby's "Quality Is Free" (1979) demonstrated that every dollar spent on prevention saves $10 in failure costs.

🧠 Memory Aid

PAIE = Prevention, Appraisal, Internal, External (cheapest to most expensive)

✅ Unit Summary

  • Cost hierarchy: Prevention < Appraisal < Internal Failure < External Failure
  • COPQ = 15-25% of sales revenue (only 3-5% visible, rest hidden)
  • Higher quality → LOWER costs (Deming's counterintuitive insight)

Unit 7: Statistical Quality Control (SQC)

📖 Key Definitions

SQC: Application of statistical methods to monitor and control processes.

Common Cause: Natural, inherent variation — process is stable and in control.

Special Cause: Specific, identifiable factors — process is out of control.

Process Capability: Ability to produce within specification limits (Cp, Cpk).

📊 7 Basic Quality Tools (C-C-C-H-P-S-F)
#ToolPurposeWhen to Use
1Cause-and-Effect (Fishbone)Identify root causesProblem cause unknown
2Check SheetData collectionGathering facts
3Control ChartMonitor stabilityTracking over time
4HistogramFrequency distributionViewing patterns
5Pareto ChartPrioritize (80/20)Deciding what to fix
6Scatter DiagramVariable relationshipTesting correlations
7FlowchartMap process stepsUnderstanding process
📖 Process Capability Formulas

Cp = (USL - LSL) / 6σ — Process potential (can it fit?)

Cpk = min[(USL - μ)/3σ, (μ - LSL)/3σ] — Actual capability (is it fitting?)

Cp ValueInterpretation
Cp < 1.0Process NOT capable
Cp = 1.0Barely capable (3σ)
Cp ≥ 1.33Capable (4σ)
Cp ≥ 1.67Highly capable (5σ)
Cp ≥ 2.0Six Sigma capable

Cpk = Cp → centered; Cpk < Cp → off-center; Cpk ≥ 1.33 → centered AND capable

💡 Real-World Example

Bottle cap factory: USL=26.4mm, LSL=25.6mm, Mean=26.0mm, σ=0.1mm. Cp = (26.4-25.6)/(6×0.1) = 1.33 → Process is capable.

🧠 Memory Aid

CCCHPSF = Cause-effect, Check sheet, Control chart, Histogram, Pareto, Scatter, Flowchart

✅ Unit Summary

  • Common cause = natural variation; Special cause = investigate
  • 7 tools: Fishbone, Check Sheet, Control Chart, Histogram, Pareto, Scatter, Flowchart
  • Cp ≥ 1.33 = capable; Cpk ≥ 1.33 = centered AND capable
  • Control limits = Mean ± 3σ

Unit 8: Tools and Techniques of TQM

📖 Key Definitions

Six Sigma: Data-driven methodology targeting 3.4 DPMO. Uses DMAIC (existing) and DMADV (new).

Kaizen: "Change for better" — continuous, incremental improvement by every employee.

QFD: Translating customer requirements into technical specs using "House of Quality."

FMEA: Identifying potential failure modes and prioritizing using Risk Priority Number (RPN).

Poka-Yoke: "Mistake-proofing" — designing processes so errors are impossible or immediately detected.

Unit 11: ISO 9000 Quality Management System

📖 Key Definitions

ISO 9001:2015: International QMS standard — certifiable, process approach, risk-based thinking.

QMS: Formalized system documenting policies, procedures, responsibilities for quality objectives.

Risk-Based Thinking: Systematic approach to identifying risks/opportunities (replaces "preventive action").

ClauseTitleKey Content
1-3Scope, References, TermsStandard coverage & terminology
4Context of OrganizationInternal/external issues, interested parties
5LeadershipTop management commitment, quality policy
6PlanningRisk assessment, quality objectives
7SupportResources, competence, communication
8OperationPlanning, control, production, delivery
9Performance EvaluationMonitoring, measurement, internal audit
10ImprovementNonconformity, corrective action, continual improvement
📖 7 QMPs (C-L-E-P-I-E-R)

1. Customer focus • 2. Leadership • 3. Engagement of people • 4. Process approach • 5. Improvement • 6. Evidence-based decision making • 7. Relationship management

💡 Real-World Example

Over 1 million organizations in 170+ countries hold ISO 9001 certification — the most widely used management standard in the world.

🧠 Memory Aid

S-N-C-L-P-S-O-E-I = Scope, Normative, Context, Leadership, Planning, Support, Operation, Evaluation, Improvement

✅ Unit Summary

  • ISO 9001 is the only certifiable standard in the ISO 9000 family
  • 10 clauses; 7 Quality Management Principles
  • 2015 changes: risk-based thinking, no mandatory management rep, context of organization

Unit 12: ISO 14000 Environmental Management System

📖 Key Definitions

EMS: Systematic framework for managing environmental responsibilities.

Environmental Aspect: Element of activities that interacts with environment (e.g., emissions, waste).

Environmental Impact: Change to environment resulting from an aspect.

Life Cycle Perspective: Considering impacts from cradle to grave (raw materials → disposal).

💡 Real-World Example

Unilever and P&G use ISO 14001 to manage environmental footprint across global supply chains.

🧠 Memory Aid

ALP-CER = Aspects, Life cycle, Policy, Compliance, Emergency, Review

✅ Unit Summary

  • ISO 14001 = environmental management; uses same HLS as ISO 9001
  • Requires life cycle perspective (cradle to grave)
  • ISO 9001 = customer requirements; ISO 14001 = environmental aspects & regulatory compliance

Unit 14: Management System for Safety and Health

Hazard: Source, situation, or act with potential to cause harm.

Risk: Likelihood × severity of hazardous event.

Hierarchy of Controls: Elimination → Substitution → Engineering → Administrative → PPE

💡 Real-World Example

Construction companies use ISO 45001 to systematically reduce workplace accidents and ensure regulatory compliance.

  • Hierarchy of Controls: Elimination → Substitution → Engineering → Administrative → PPE
  • Near misses = critical learning opportunities

Unit 15: Quality Auditing and Certification

📖 Key Definitions

Quality Audit: Systematic, independent, documented process for evaluating QMS fulfillment.

First Party (Internal): Organization audits itself — self-improvement.

Second Party: Customer audits supplier — supplier qualification.

Third Party: Certification body audits — leads to certification (BSI, TÜV, SGS).

Nonconformity: Non-fulfillment of a requirement.

Corrective Action: Eliminating the cause of a nonconformity to prevent recurrence.

📊 Audit Process (7 Steps)

1. Planning & preparation → 2. Opening meeting → 3. Document review → 4. On-site audit → 5. Closing meeting → 6. Audit report → 7. Follow-up (corrective actions)

📊 Certification Process (7 Steps)

1. Application → 2. Document review (Stage 0) → 3. Stage 1 audit (readiness) → 4. Stage 2 audit (implementation) → 5. Certification decision → 6. Surveillance audits (annual) → 7. Re-certification (every 3 years)

💡 Real-World Example

Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) is India's national certification body for ISO standards.

🧠 Memory Aid

POD-OC-F = Planning, Opening meeting, Document review, On-site audit, Closing meeting, Audit report, Follow-up

Stage 1 = "Are you ready?" (documentation) | Stage 2 = "Are you doing it?" (implementation)

✅ Unit Summary

  • 3 audit types: Internal (self), Second party (customer), Third party (certification body)
  • Certification: Stage 1 (readiness) → Stage 2 (implementation) → Surveillance (annual) → Re-certification (3 years)
  • BIS = India's national certification body
