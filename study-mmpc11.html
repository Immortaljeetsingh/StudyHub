Unit 1: Individual Behaviour and Personality

📖 Unit 1: Individual Behaviour and Personality

Individual behaviour is influenced by personality, perception, learning, and attitudes. Personality theories (Big Five, Myers-Briggs) help understand individual differences in organizations. OB studies human behaviour at three levels: individual, group, and organizational.

▶
Introduction to Organizational Behaviour
▶
Big Five Personality Traits (OCEAN)

Point 1: Definition and Scope of OB

  • Organizational Behaviour = Study of human behaviour in organizational context to improve effectiveness
  • Three levels of analysis: Individual (personality, perception), Group (dynamics, teams), Organization (culture, structure)
  • Micro OB focuses on people inside the organization; Macro OB focuses on the organization in its environment
  • OB draws from Psychology, Sociology, Anthropology, and Management disciplines

Point 2: Big Five Personality Traits (OCEAN)

📊 Big Five Personality Traits (OCEAN)
Openness
Conscientiousness
Extraversion
Agreeableness

The Big Five model is the most widely accepted framework for understanding personality differences in organizations.

Point 3: Personality vs Temperament vs Attitude

ConceptDefinitionStabilityExample
PersonalityUnique psychological characteristicsRelatively stableIntroversion, dominance
TemperamentBiologically-based emotional tendenciesInnate, stableActivity level, mood
AttitudeEvaluative statements about objects/peopleChangeableJob satisfaction, commitment

Point 4: Perception and Attribution

  • Perception = Process of organizing and interpreting sensory information
  • Attribution Theory (Kelley): People attribute causes to behaviour based on consistency, distinctiveness, consensus
  • Fundamental Attribution Error: Overestimate internal factors, underestimate external factors
  • Self-Serving Bias: Attribute success to internal factors, failure to external factors
💡 Indian Example

Infosys uses personality assessments (Big Five) in recruitment to evaluate cultural fit. Tata Group emphasizes attitude over skills in hiring, believing skills can be taught but attitude is innate.

⚠ Exam Tip

Know Big Five traits: OCEAN (Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism). Questions ask about perception errors and attribution theory.

Unit 2: Motivation — Theories and Applications

📖 Unit 2: Motivation — Theories and Applications

Motivation drives behaviour towards goals. Content theories (Maslow, Herzberg, McClelland) explain what motivates. Process theories (Vroom, Adams) explain how motivation works.

▶
Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs
▶
Herzberg Two-Factor Theory
▶

Point 1: Content Theories of Motivation

📊 Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs
🌟 Self-Actualization
Realize potential
👑 Esteem
Recognition, status
👥 Love/Belonging
Social connections
🛡 Safety
Security, stability
🍎 Physiological
Food, water, shelter

Maslow's hierarchy: lower needs must be satisfied before higher needs can motivate behaviour.

Point 2: Herzberg Two-Factor Theory

🚫 Hygiene Factors
  • Salary, working conditions, company policy
  • Prevent dissatisfaction (but don't motivate)
  • Must be adequate to avoid demotivation
🌟 Motivators
  • Achievement, recognition, responsibility
  • Drive satisfaction and motivation
  • Must be present for true motivation

Point 3: Process Theories

TheoryKey IdeaFormula
Vroom ExpectancyEffort depends on expected outcomesMotivation = E x I x V
Adams EquityFairness of reward relative to inputsCompare own ratio to others
Locke Goal SettingSpecific, challenging goals improve performanceSMART goals
💡 Indian Example

Google India offers 20% time for personal projects, applying Herzberg motivators (achievement, responsibility). TCS uses McClelland achievement motivation in leadership training programs.

⚠ Exam Tip

Compare Maslow vs Herzberg. Know Vroom expectancy theory: Motivation = Expectancy x Instrumentality x Valence. Questions ask about hygiene vs motivator factors.

Point 3: Transformational vs Transactional Leadership

💫 Transformational
  • Inspire and motivate followers
  • Intellectual stimulation
  • Individualized consideration
  • Idealized influence
💰 Transactional
  • Contingent rewards
  • Management by exception
  • Clear expectations
  • Exchange-based

⚠ Exam Tip

Point 3: Culture Types (Cameron & Quinn)

🏠 Clan
  • Family-like, collaborative
  • Focus: mentorship, teamwork
  • Example: Google, Infosys
🚀 Adhocracy
  • Dynamic, entrepreneurial

  • Example: Startups, Tesla

Unit 10: Stress Management

📖 Unit 10: Stress Management

Stress is the body's response to demands. Eustress (positive) motivates; distress (negative) harms health and performance. Sources include role ambiguity, workload, and interpersonal conflict.

▶
Workplace Stress Management

Point 1: Types of Stress

💪 Eustress
  • Positive stress that motivates
  • Challenges that drive performance
  • Example: promotion, new project
😔 Distress
  • Negative stress that harms
  • Excessive demands beyond capacity
  • Example: job insecurity, burnout
