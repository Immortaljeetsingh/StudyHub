Unit 1: Operations Management — An Overview

Operations Management (OM) designs, operates, and improves systems that create and deliver a firm's primary products and services. It involves managing resources (people, equipment, technology) to efficiently produce goods and services across both manufacturing and service sectors.

Unit 2: Work Study and Job Design

Work Study systematically examines work methods to improve productivity. Job Design determines the content and methods of jobs. Methods Study and Time Study are the two main components that form the foundation of industrial engineering.

Unit 3: Value Engineering and Quality Management

Value Engineering improves value by examining functions. Value = Function / Cost. Quality Management ensures products meet customer expectations through TQM, SPC, and quality tools.

Unit 4: Purchase Systems and Inventory Management

Purchase Management acquires materials at the right quality, quantity, time, and price. Inventory Management controls the flow of materials to minimize costs while ensuring availability.

Unit 5: Standardisation, Simplification, and Waste Management

Standardisation establishes uniform specifications. Simplification reduces variety. Waste Management minimizes material waste through lean principles and 5S methodology.

Unit 6: Production Planning and Control

Production Planning determines what, when, and how much to produce. PPC coordinates materials, machines, and manpower to achieve production targets efficiently.

Unit 7: Facilities Planning and Layout

Facilities Planning determines the location, layout, and design of production facilities. Plant Layout arranges machines, equipment, and work areas for optimal material flow.

Unit 8: Capacity Planning

Capacity Planning determines the production capacity needed to meet demand. It involves long-term (strategic), medium-term (tactical), and short-term (operational) decisions.

Unit 9: Aggregate Planning

Aggregate Planning develops a medium-term production plan (3-18 months) that balances supply and demand. It uses strategies like level production, chase demand, and mixed strategies.

Unit 10: Planning and Control for Services

Service operations differ from manufacturing: intangibility, inseparability, heterogeneity, perishability. Service planning focuses on capacity management, queue management, and service quality.

Unit 11: Maintenance Management

Maintenance Management ensures equipment reliability and availability. It includes preventive maintenance (scheduled), predictive maintenance (condition-based), and breakdown maintenance.

Unit 12: Supply Chain Management

SCM manages the flow of goods from raw materials to end customers. It integrates suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, and retailers to optimize the entire chain.

Unit 13: Lean Manufacturing and JIT

Lean Manufacturing eliminates waste (Muda) while maximizing value. JIT (Just-In-Time) produces only what is needed, when needed, in the quantity needed.
