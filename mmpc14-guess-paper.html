Unit 1: Financial Management — An Introduction

Financial Management: Planning, organizing, directing, and controlling financial activities in an organization. Finance is the lifeblood of any business — needed even before a firm begins operations.

Unit 2: Time Value of Money

A rupee today is worth more than a rupee tomorrow due to earning power , inflation , and uncertainty . Compounding: Finding future value of present money. Discounting: Finding present value of future money.

Read Full Notes →

Unit 3: Risk and Return

Risk = Variability/uncertainty of returns (not just negative outcomes). Higher risk demands higher return (risk-return tradeoff). Beta (β) measures systematic risk relative to the market.

Read Full Notes →

Unit 4: Valuation of Securities

Three approaches: Income (DCF), Market (comparable companies), Asset (book value). Three-step analysis: Economy → Industry → Company.

Read Full Notes →

Unit 5: Cost of Capital

Cost of Capital = Minimum rate of return to keep firm value intact. It is the opportunity cost of investing in a project vs alternative investments. Used as discount rate in capital budgeting.

Read Full Notes →

Unit 6: Investment Appraisal Methods

Capital Budgeting = Long-term investment decision-making. Evaluating projects where cash flows occur over multiple years. Hurdle Rate = Minimum acceptable rate (usually WACC).

Read Full Notes →

Unit 7: Management of Working Capital

Working Capital = Current Assets - Current Liabilities . Gross WC = Total Current Assets; Net WC = Current Assets - Current Liabilities. Balance between profitability and liquidity .

Read Full Notes →

Unit 8: Financial Markets and Institutions

Financial markets channel funds from savers to investors . Enable price discovery , liquidity , and cost reduction . SEBI regulates Indian capital markets, protects investor interests, prevents fraud.

Unit 9: Sources of Finance

Funds can be raised from multiple sources: Long-term (equity, preference, debentures, retained earnings, VC, leasing), Short-term (trade credit, bank overdraft, CP, factoring), Emerging (crowdfunding, angel investors, asset securitisation).

Unit 10: Capital Structure

Capital Structure = Mix of long-term debt and equity financing. Optimal Capital Structure = Mix that minimizes WACC and maximizes firm value.

Read Full Notes →

Unit 11: Leverage Analysis

Leverage = Use of fixed costs to magnify returns. Three types: Operating (DOL), Financial (DFL), and Combined (DCL).

Read Full Notes →

Unit 12: Dividend Theories

Does dividend policy affect firm value? Two schools: Relevance (Walter, Gordon) says YES; Irrelevance (MM) says NO in perfect markets.

Read Full Notes →

Unit 13: Dividend Policies

Forms: Cash Dividend, Stock Dividend (Bonus Shares), Stock Split, Stock Buyback, Property Dividend, Scrip Dividend.

Unit 14: Behavioural Finance

Traditional finance assumes rational investors and efficient markets. Behavioural finance recognizes cognitive biases and irrational behavior. Prospect Theory (Kahneman & Tversky): People evaluate gains and losses relative to a reference point. Loss aversion: Losses hurt ~2x more than equivalent gains feel good.

Unit 15: Financial Restructuring

Corporate Restructuring = Redefining business for value maximization or survival. Methods: Buyback, Debt-to-Equity Conversion, CDR, LBO, Divestiture, M&A.
