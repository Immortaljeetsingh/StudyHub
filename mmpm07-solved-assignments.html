Q1. Define IMC and explain the TAPIC process of integrated marketing communication.

Answer: Integrated Marketing Communication is the careful integration and coordination of all communication channels to deliver a clear, consistent, and compelling message (Kotler & Armstrong). The American Association of Advertising Agencies defines it as a comprehensive plan that evaluates the strategic roles of communication disciplines, including advertising, PR, personal selling, sales promotion, and direct marketing, for maximum impact. IMC emerged in the 1990s because fragmented agencies produced inconsistent messaging that confused consumers and wasted budgets. The TAPIC process guides implementation in five steps. Target: identify the target market segments. Ascertain: fix communication objectives, which may be informative, persuasive, reminder, or institutional. Plan: design messages suited to the audience and objectives. Implement: execute the promotional strategy across the chosen channels. Collect: gather feedback to evaluate and adjust. In today's fragmented media landscape a consumer may see a TV ad, search online, read reviews, receive an email offer, and finally buy in-store; IMC ensures every touchpoint reinforces the same brand story. Brands like Amul, Zomato, and Fevicol are instantly recognizable across billboards, tweets, and TV, proving that integration builds recognition, trust, and profitability.

Q2. Explain the seven tools of the promotions mix with Indian examples.

Answer: The promotions mix is the specific blend of communication tools a company uses to pursue its marketing objectives. The seven tools, remembered as APPS-DDG, are as follows. Advertising is paid, non-personal communication through mass media that builds brand awareness at scale, as with Fevicol's iconic print campaigns. Public relations is earned media through press releases, events, and CSR that builds long-term credibility. Personal selling is face-to-face interaction, essential in B2B and high-value products, and handles objections directly. Sales promotion uses short-term incentives such as coupons, samples, and contests to drive immediate action, as in festive-season retail offers. Direct marketing is database-driven, targeted communication, such as email and telemarketing, for measurable response. Digital marketing covers online channels, including SEO, social media, and content marketing, with data-driven targeting, as with Meesho's Facebook ads in tier 2/3 cities. Guerrilla marketing uses unconventional, low-cost tactics for maximum exposure with viral potential. The right mix depends on product type, market stage, budget, and target audience: consumer goods rely more on advertising, industrial products on personal selling, and new products on awareness-building tools.

Q3. What are the strategic considerations in campaign planning?

Answer: A campaign is a planned series of communication steps built around a single theme, and strategic planning determines what the campaign must achieve and how. The first consideration is objectives and target audience: objectives may be informative, persuasive, reminder, or institutional (IPRI), and the audience must be defined precisely, from broad segments to niche groups. The second is budget allocation, which shapes the scale and media choices; small budgets favor digital and guerrilla tactics, while large budgets enable television and print. The third is creative strategy, developing the big idea, message, and execution style that will achieve the objectives. The fourth is media selection, choosing channels that reach the audience efficiently and reinforce each other. The fifth is the timeline and milestones, followed by execution and monitoring, and finally evaluation and optimization based on results. Campaigns can be local, regional, national, or international and must align with the overall marketing strategy. The Amul Butter Cookies launch illustrates the discipline: clear objectives, a creative idea built on comparative "25% vs 2% butter" content, social media channels chosen for reach, and success measured through trending and sales.

Q4. Explain the role of digital marketing in IMC and the challenges of the digital era.

Answer: Digital marketing has transformed IMC by enabling two-way conversations instead of one-way broadcasts. The eight digital tools, remembered as WSSCEMVI, are websites, search engine marketing, social media marketing, content marketing, email marketing, mobile marketing, video marketing, and influencer marketing. Integration across online and offline channels is essential for a consistent brand experience: Zomato's witty Twitter presence, Instagram food content, email notifications, and in-app messaging all carry the same voice. Digital marketing enables precise targeting, including demographic, behavioral, contextual, and retargeting approaches, and offers measurable, cost-effective campaigns, as with Flipkart bidding on "buy smartphone" keywords and Mamaearth's micro-influencer strategy. SEO builds long-term organic visibility while SEM delivers immediate paid results. However, the digital era brings challenges: information overload, ad blocking, and rising privacy regulation, including GDPR in Europe, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act with penalties up to Rs 250 crore, and third-party cookie deprecation. Marketers must therefore build trust through transparent data practices, shifting to first-party data gathered with consent through loyalty programs and login-based ecosystems.
