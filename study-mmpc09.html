Block-01: Operations Fundamentals

Unit 1: Operations Management — An Overview

📖 Unit 1: Operations Management — An Overview

Operations Management (OM) designs, operates, and improves systems that create and deliver a firm's primary products and services. It involves managing resources (people, equipment, technology) to efficiently produce goods and services across both manufacturing and service sectors.

▶
Operations Management Introduction
▶
Production Systems Overview

Point 1: Definition and Scope of Operations Management

  • Operations Management is the systematic design, direction, and control of processes that transform inputs into finished goods and services
  • It encompasses both manufacturing operations (producing tangible goods) and service operations (delivering intangible services)
  • OM decisions span five areas: Process, Quality, Capacity, Inventory, and Workforce
  • The goal is to achieve competitive advantage through cost efficiency, quality, flexibility, and speed
  • Productivity = Output / Input (measure of efficiency)
  • Lead Time = Total time from order placement to delivery

Unit 2: Work Study and Job Design

📖 Unit 2: Work Study and Job Design

Work Study systematically examines work methods to improve productivity. Job Design determines the content and methods of jobs. Methods Study and Time Study are the two main components that form the foundation of industrial engineering.

▶
Work Study Methods
▶
Job Design Approaches

Point 1: Work Study — Definition and Components

  • Work Study is a systematic study of work to improve productivity by eliminating unnecessary activities and finding the best method
  • Method Study: Finds the best way to do a job by analyzing each step
  • Time Study: Determines the standard time required to perform a job at normal pace
  • Both together form the foundation of industrial engineering

Point 2: Method Study vs Time Study

AspectMethod StudyTime Study
ObjectiveFind the best methodFind the standard time
FocusHow the work is doneHow long it takes
ToolsFlow process charts, therbligsStopwatch, PMTS
OutputImproved work methodStandard time for the task
SequenceDone FIRST (improve method)Done SECOND (time the improved method)

Point 3: Therbligs — Gilbreth's Motion Study

SearchFindSelectGraspHoldTransportPositionAssembleUseDisassembleInspectRelease

Therbligs are 17 basic motions identified by Frank Gilbreth. They classify every movement into effective (productive) and ineffective (wasteful) categories. By eliminating ineffective therbligs, productivity improves.

Point 4: Job Design Approaches

🎯 Job Simplification
  • Break jobs into small, repetitive tasks
  • High efficiency but low motivation
  • Suitable for mass production
🔄 Job Rotation
  • Move workers between different tasks
  • Reduces monotony, builds versatility
  • Used in Toyota and Tata Motors
📈 Job Enlargement
  • Expand scope of tasks horizontally
  • More variety, same skill level
  • Increases task significance
🚀 Job Enrichment
  • Add responsibility vertically (Herzberg)
  • More autonomy, decision-making
  • Increases motivation and satisfaction

Point 5: Standard Time Calculation

📊 Standard Time Formula
Standard Time
=
Normal Time
+
Allowances

Normal Time = Observed Time x Rating Factor. Allowances include fatigue, personal needs, delays (typically 10-15%).

💡 Indian Example

Tata Steel used work study to increase productivity by 25% at Jamshedpur plant. They applied method study to optimize material flow and time study to set standards for each operation. Toyota Production System is built on detailed work study principles.

⚠ Exam Tip

Distinguish between Method Study and Time Study. Remember: Method Study comes FIRST (improve the method), then Time Study (time the improved method). Questions ask about therbligs and flow process charts.

🧠 Memory Aid

✅ Unit 2 Summary

  • Work Study systematically examines work to improve productivity
  • Two components: Method Study (best way) + Time Study (standard time)
  • Method Study comes FIRST, Time Study SECOND
  • Therbligs: 17 basic motions (Gilbreth) for motion analysis
  • Job Design: Simplification, Rotation, Enlargement, Enrichment
  • Standard Time = Normal Time + Allowances

📖 Unit 3: Value Engineering and Quality Management

Value Engineering improves value by examining functions. Value = Function / Cost. Quality Management ensures products meet customer expectations through TQM, SPC, and quality tools.

Point 1: Value Engineering — Concept

  • Value Engineering (VE) is a systematic method to improve the "value" of products by examining functions
  • Value = Function / Cost — increase value by improving function OR reducing cost
  • VE was developed by Lawrence Miles at GE in the 1940s
  • VE focuses on functions, not costs — asks "what does it do?" not "what does it cost?"

=
sqrt(2DS/H)

D = Annual demand (units)

S = Ordering cost per order (Rs)

H = Holding cost per unit per year (Rs)

Unit 8: Capacity Planning

📖 Unit 8: Capacity Planning

Capacity Planning determines the production capacity needed to meet demand. It involves long-term (strategic), medium-term (tactical), and short-term (operational) decisions.

▶
Capacity Planning Strategies

Point 1: Capacity Planning Levels

LevelTime HorizonDecisions
Strategic (Long-term)1-5+ yearsFacility size, location, technology
Tactical (Medium-term)3-18 monthsWorkforce levels, shifts, subcontracting
Operational (Short-term)Daily/WeeklyScheduling, overtime, maintenance

Point 2: Capacity Strategies

🔥 Lead Strategy
  • Add capacity BEFORE demand materializes
  • Risk: excess capacity if demand doesn't come
  • Example: Jio built 4G capacity before launch
🧊 Lag Strategy
  • Add capacity AFTER demand is proven
  • Risk: lost sales if capacity is insufficient
  • Conservative, lower risk approach
⚖ Match Strategy
  • Add capacity incrementally as demand grows
  • Balanced approach between Lead and Lag
  • Most commonly used in practice
📊 Key Metrics
  • Capacity Utilization = Actual Output / Design Capacity
  • Efficiency = Actual Output / Effective Capacity
  • Break-even Analysis for capacity decisions
💡 Indian Example

Jio invested Rs 1.5 lakh crore in capacity planning for 4G network. They built capacity for 400M+ subscribers before launching — a classic Lead Strategy. This aggressive approach disrupted the telecom industry.

⚠ Exam Tip

Know capacity strategies: Lead, Lag, Match. Questions ask about capacity utilization rate and break-even analysis. Jio is the classic example of Lead Strategy.

🧠 Memory Aid

CAPACITY: Calculate demand, Assess alternatives, Plan resources, Allocate, Control, Improve, Track, Yield

✅ Unit 8 Summary

  • Capacity Planning: 3 levels (strategic, tactical, operational)
  • 3 strategies: Lead (before demand), Lag (after demand), Match (incremental)
  • Key metrics: Capacity Utilization, Efficiency, Break-even
  • Capacity Utilization = Actual Output / Design Capacity
  • Jio: Rs 1.5 lakh crore investment, 400M+ capacity, Lead Strategy

Maruti Suzuki uses aggregate planning to manage seasonal demand spikes during festival season (Oct-Dec). They plan production 6 months ahead, using a mixed strategy: level production for base demand + overtime and subcontracting for festival surge.

Point 3: Bullwhip Effect

  • Bullwhip Effect: Small changes in consumer demand cause increasingly larger swings in orders upstream
  • Causes: Demand forecasting errors, order batching, price fluctuations, rationing
  • Solution: Information sharing, EDI, VMI (Vendor Managed Inventory)

Point 4: Push vs Pull Systems

  • Build inventory before demand
  • Risk: overproduction
  • Example: Traditional manufacturing
💧 Pull System
  • Production based on actual demand
  • Produce only when ordered
  • Risk: stockouts if demand spikes
  • Example: JIT, Dell's build-to-order
💡 Indian Example

Point 1: Lean Manufacturing Principles

  • Lean = eliminate waste while maximizing customer value
  • 5 Lean Principles: Define Value, Map Value Stream, Create Flow, Establish Pull, Seek Perfection
  • Origin: Toyota Production System (TPS) developed by Taiichi Ohno

Point 2: 7 Wastes of Lean (TIMWOOD)

📊 7 Wastes (TIMWOOD)
Transport
Inventory
Motion
Waiting
Overproduction
Overprocessing
Defects
  • Transport: Unnecessary movement of materials
  • Inventory: Excess stock hiding problems
  • Motion: Unnecessary movement of people
  • Waiting: Idle time between steps
  • Overproduction: Making more than needed (most dangerous)
  • Overprocessing: Doing more work than required
  • Defects: Rework and scrap

Point 3: JIT (Just-In-Time)

📖 Unit 14: Project Management (PERT/CPM)

Project Management plans, schedules, and controls unique activities with defined start and end. PERT handles uncertainty with 3 time estimates. CPM uses deterministic times.

Point 1: Project Characteristics

  • Unique (one-time activity, not repetitive)
  • Temporary (defined start and end dates)
  • Resources (requires allocation of people, money, materials)
  • Interdependent (activities have precedence relationships)

Point 4: Critical Path and Float

  • Critical Path: Longest path through the network; determines minimum project duration
  • Float (Slack): Time an activity can be delayed without delaying the project
  • Free Float: Time an activity can be delayed without delaying the next activity
  • Activities on the critical path have ZERO float

Point 5: Crashing

  • Crashing: Reducing project duration by adding resources to critical activities
  • Always crash the cheapest critical activity first
  • Crash cost = (Crash cost - Normal cost) / (Normal time - Crash time)
💡 Indian Example

Delhi Metro was completed using PERT/CPM. Phase 1 (65 km) was delivered on time using critical path analysis across 50+ work packages. DMRC used PERT for uncertain activities (tunneling, land acquisition) and CPM for deterministic tasks (track laying).

📐 Case Study: Delhi Metro — India's PERT/CPM Success Story

Key lesson: Identifying the critical path allowed DMRC to focus resources on activities that directly impacted the completion date. Float management enabled parallel execution of non-critical tasks. The project set a benchmark for infrastructure project management in India.
