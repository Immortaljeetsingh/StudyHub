Q1. What is Operations Management? How does it differ from Production Management? (10 Marks)

Answer: Operations Management (OM) is the systematic design, direction, and control of processes that transform inputs into finished goods and services. It manages resources such as people, equipment, and technology to produce goods and services efficiently in both manufacturing and service sectors. OM decisions span five areas: Process, Quality, Capacity, Inventory, and Workforce, and productivity is measured as output divided by input. Production Management is narrower: it is confined to manufacturing and focuses on factory-floor efficiency and tangible goods only. Operations Management is broader because it includes services and covers the entire value chain, so it applies to hospitals, banks, IT firms, and retail along with factories. For example, Maruti Suzuki manages production of 1.5 million cars per year by coordinating 500-plus suppliers and 2,000-plus dealers; at Manesar one car rolls out every 45 seconds using a blend of automated and manual operations. In short, Production Management is a subset of Operations Management.

Key points:

  • Operations Management (OM) is the systematic design, direction, and control of processes that transform inputs into finished goods and services.
  • It manages resources such as people, equipment, and technology to produce goods and services efficiently in both manufacturing and service sectors.
  • OM decisions span five areas: Process, Quality, Capacity, Inventory, and Workforce, and productivity is measured as output divided by input.

Takeaway: In short, Production Management is a subset of Operations Management.

Q2. Distinguish between Method Study and Time Study. (10 Marks)

Answer: Method Study and Time Study are the two main components of Work Study, which systematically examines work to improve productivity. Method Study finds the best way to do a job by analysing each step of the process. It asks how the work is done and uses tools such as flow process charts and therbligs, the 17 basic motions identified by Frank Gilbreth, and its output is an improved work method that eliminates ineffective motions. Time Study determines the standard time required to perform a job at normal pace. It asks how long the work takes and uses a stopwatch or predetermined motion time systems, and its output is the standard time, computed as Normal Time plus Allowances, where Normal Time equals Observed Time multiplied by the Rating Factor and allowances cover fatigue, personal needs, and delays of 10-15 per cent. The sequence is crucial: Method Study is done FIRST to improve the method, and Time Study is done SECOND to time the improved method. Tata Steel used this combination at Jamshedpur to raise productivity by 25 per cent, optimising material flow and setting standards for every operation.

Key points:

  • Method Study and Time Study are the two main components of Work Study, which systematically examines work to improve productivity.
  • It asks how the work is done and uses tools such as flow process charts and therbligs, the 17 basic motions identified by Frank Gilbreth, and its output is an improved work method that eliminates ineffective motions.
  • Method Study finds the best way to do a job by analysing each step of the process.

Takeaway: Tata Steel used this combination at Jamshedpur to raise productivity by 25 per cent, optimising material flow and setting standards for every operation.

Q3. What is Value Engineering? Explain the Value Engineering job plan. (10 Marks)

Answer: Value Engineering (VE) is a systematic method to improve the value of a product by examining its functions rather than its costs. Value is defined as Function divided by Cost, so value can be increased either by improving the function or by reducing the cost. VE was developed by Lawrence Miles at General Electric in the 1940s, and it always asks what the product does rather than what it costs. The VE job plan has six phases. The first is Information, where data about the product is gathered. The second is Function Analysis, where the functions are defined. The third is the Creative phase, where alternatives are generated. The fourth is Evaluation, where the best alternative is selected. The fifth is Development, where a detailed plan is prepared, and the sixth is Presentation, where the proposal is implemented. In India, Infosys reportedly saved Rs 500 crore by applying VE to software projects, and Tata Motors evaluates every component for its function-versus-cost trade-off. Examiners expect the six phases in sequence and the formula Value = Function / Cost.

Key points:

  • Value Engineering (VE) is a systematic method to improve the value of a product by examining its functions rather than its costs.
  • In India, Infosys reportedly saved Rs 500 crore by applying VE to software projects, and Tata Motors evaluates every component for its function-versus-cost trade-off.
  • Value is defined as Function divided by Cost, so value can be increased either by improving the function or by reducing the cost.

Q4. Explain Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) and ABC Analysis as inventory management techniques. (10 Marks)

Answer: Inventory management controls the flow of materials to minimise costs while ensuring availability, and two widely used techniques are EOQ and ABC analysis. Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) is the optimal order quantity that minimises the total cost of ordering and holding inventory. The EOQ formula is the square root of (2DS divided by H), where D is annual demand in units, S is the ordering cost per order, and H is the holding cost per unit per year. Ordering larger quantities reduces ordering costs but increases holding costs, so EOQ balances the two. ABC analysis categorises inventory items by value: A items are the few high-value items, typically 20 per cent of items accounting for 80 per cent of value, and they need the tightest control; B items are of medium value; and C items are numerous but low-value, requiring only simple controls. This is the 80/20 rule applied to inventory. Amazon India manages 200 million-plus products across 60-plus fulfilment centres, using ABC analysis to prioritise high-value items and AI-based demand prediction to set stock levels.

Key points:

  • Inventory management controls the flow of materials to minimise costs while ensuring availability, and two widely used techniques are EOQ and ABC analysis.
  • Amazon India manages 200 million-plus products across 60-plus fulfilment centres, using ABC analysis to prioritise high-value items and AI-based demand prediction to set stock levels.
  • Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) is the optimal order quantity that minimises the total cost of ordering and holding inventory.
