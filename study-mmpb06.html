Unit 1: Business Ethics

📖 Definition

Business Ethics = moral principles guiding business behavior. Three ethical theories: Utilitarianism (greatest good), Deontology (duty-based), Virtue Ethics (character-based).

TheoryFocusExample
UtilitarianismGreatest good for greatest numberCost-benefit analysis
DeontologyDuty-based, rules matter"Honesty is always right"
Virtue EthicsCharacter-based"What would a good person do?"

Good Governance Principles (T-A-F-R)

  • Transparency
  • Accountability
  • Fairness
  • Responsibility
💡 Real-World Example

The Satyam scam (2009) — India's largest corporate fraud — led to major governance reforms including stricter audit requirements and independent director mandates.

🧠 Memory Aid

"U-D-V" for ethical theories: Utilitarianism, Deontology, Virtue Ethics

✅ Unit Summary

  • Three ethical theories: U-D-V
  • Good governance: Transparency, Accountability, Fairness, Responsibility
  • Satyam scam triggered major governance reforms

Unit 2: Corporate Governance — Concepts and Theories

📖 Definition

Agency Theory = separation of ownership (shareholders) and control (management). Agency Costs = Monitoring + Bonding + Residual Loss.

Board Structure

  • Executive Directors: Full-time employees (CEO, CFO)
  • Independent Directors: No material relationship with company
  • Non-Executive Directors: Not involved in daily operations
Shareholder TheoryStakeholder Theory
Maximize shareholder valueBalance all stakeholder interests
Profit-focusedMulti-dimensional
Milton FriedmanR. Edward Freeman
Short-term emphasisLong-term emphasis
💡 Real-World Example

Infosys has a strong board with majority independent directors, separate Chairman and CEO roles, and active board committees.

🧠 Memory Aid

"M-B-R" for agency costs: Monitoring, Bonding, Residual loss

✅ Unit Summary

  • Agency theory = ownership vs control separation
  • Agency costs: Monitoring + Bonding + Residual loss
  • Stakeholder vs Shareholder: Friedman vs Freeman

Unit 3: Institutional Mechanism

📖 Definition

Institutional Investors = mutual funds, pension funds, insurance companies. Proxy Voting = shareholders vote without attending meetings. Executive Compensation = salary, bonuses, stock options.

Governance Codes

  • SOX Act (2002): US — response to Enron/WorldCom scandals
  • Companies Act 2013: India — enhanced governance requirements
  • SEBI Regulations: India — listed company governance
💡 Real-World Example

HDFC Bank's institutional investors actively participate in governance, voting on key resolutions and engaging with management on risk practices.

🧠 Memory Aid

"S-E-P" for compensation: Salary, ESOPs, Performance bonus

✅ Unit Summary

  • Institutional investors drive better governance
  • SOX Act, Companies Act 2013, SEBI regulations
  • Proxy voting enables remote shareholder participation

Unit 5: Basel Framework

📖 Definition

Basel Committee (BCBS) = global banking regulator. Basel I (1988) = 8% CAR. Basel II (2004) = three pillars. Basel III (2010) = strengthened post-2008 crisis.

BaselYearFocusKey Requirement
Basel I1988Credit risk8% CAR
Basel II2004Three pillarsMore risk-sensitive
Basel III2010Post-crisis reformHigher capital, liquidity

Basel II Three Pillars

  • Pillar 1 — Minimum Capital: Credit, Market, Operational risk. CAR = (Tier 1 + Tier 2) / RWA >= 8%
  • Pillar 2 — Supervisory Review: Internal capital assessment, regulatory review
  • Pillar 3 — Market Discipline: Disclosure requirements, public transparency

Basel III Key Ratios

RatioMinimum
CET14.5%
Tier 16%
Total Capital (CAR)8%
Capital Conservation Buffer2.5%
Leverage Ratio3%
LCR100%
💡 Real-World Example

State Bank of India maintains CAR of ~13-14%, well above the Basel III minimum of 10.5% (8% + 2.5% buffer).

❗ Exam Tip

Basel framework is HIGH-FREQUENCY. Memorize all three pillars, Basel III ratios, and CAR formula.

🧠 Memory Aid

"M-S-M" for Basel II pillars: Minimum capital, Supervisory review, Market discipline

✅ Unit Summary

  • CAR = (Tier 1 + Tier 2) / RWA >= 8%
  • Basel II: 3 pillars (M-S-M). Basel III: higher capital + liquidity
  • SBI: CAR ~13-14%, above 10.5% minimum

Unit 7: Disclosure and Transparency in Banking

📖 Definition

NPA (Non-Performing Asset) = loan overdue 90+ days. CRILC = RBI's database for borrowers with exposure >= Rs 5 crore. Asset Classification: Standard, Sub-standard, Doubtful, Loss.

"S-S-D-L" for asset classification: Standard, Sub-standard, Doubtful, Loss

Unit 8: Global Perspective on Banking Governance

📖 Definition

2008 Financial Crisis exposed governance failures. G-SIBs = Globally Systemically Important Banks. Ring-fencing = separating retail from investment banking. Stress testing = assessing resilience under adverse scenarios.

Post-Crisis Reforms

  • Higher capital requirements (Basel III)
  • Stress testing programs
  • Living will requirements
  • Ring-fencing of operations
  • Resolution frameworks
  • Compensation restrictions
💡 Real-World Example

After 2008, Lehman Brothers collapsed (no ring-fencing), while JP Morgan survived but required $25B government bailout (too big to fail).

🧠 Memory Aid

"S-L-R-S" for post-crisis reforms: Stress testing, Living wills, Ring-fencing, Strict capital

✅ Unit Summary

  • 2008 crisis exposed governance failures globally
  • G-SIBs: additional capital buffer 1-3.5% of RWA
  • "Too big to fail" = systemic risk from large interconnected banks

Unit 10: Corporate Governance in Mutual Funds

📖 Definition

Mutual Fund = pool of money managed by AMC. Structure: Sponsor > Trust > AMC > Fund. NAV = (Total Assets - Liabilities) / Units. SEBI (Mutual Fund) Regulations, 1996.

MF Structure

Sponsor > Trust > AMC > Fund. Trustee (oversight) + Custodian (safekeeping).

💡 Real-World Example

SBI Mutual Fund has a trustee company overseeing the AMC, ensuring investor interests are protected.

🧠 Memory Aid

"T-A-C" for MF structure: Trustee, AMC, Custodian

✅ Unit Summary

  • NAV = (Assets - Liabilities) / Units
  • Structure: Trustee > AMC > Custodian
  • SEBI regulations govern MF governance

Unit 13: Business Ethics, Governance and CSR

📖 Definition

CSR = Companies Act 2013, Section 135. Threshold: net worth >= Rs 500 crore OR turnover >= Rs 1000 crore OR net profit >= Rs 5 crore. Spend 2% of average net profit of last 3 years.

CSR Activities

  • Education, Healthcare, Environment, Rural development, Gender equality, Sports
💡 Real-World Example

Infosys Foundation spends on education, healthcare, and rural development, exceeding the 2% CSR requirement.

🧠 Memory Aid

"5-1-5" for CSR threshold: 500 Cr worth OR 1000 Cr turnover OR 5 Cr profit

✅ Unit Summary

  • CSR: Section 135 — 2% of average net profit
  • Threshold: 500 Cr / 1000 Cr / 5 Cr
  • ESG = Environmental, Social, Governance factors

Unit 14: Corporate Governance Reforms and Challenges

📖 Definition

Major reforms: SOX Act (2002, US — Enron/WorldCom response), Companies Act 2013 (India), SEBI regulations. AML = Anti-Money Laundering (KYC, CTR, STR).

AML Mechanisms

  • KYC (Know Your Customer): Identity verification
  • CTR (Cash Transaction Report): Transactions > Rs 10 lakh
  • STR (Suspicious Transaction Report): Unusual patterns

Governance Challenges

  • Family-owned business governance
  • Independent director effectiveness
  • Related party transactions
  • Executive compensation excess
  • Whistleblower protection
💡 Real-World Example

The Companies Act 2013 introduced independent director requirements, audit committee powers, and CSR mandates, strengthening Indian corporate governance.

🧠 Memory Aid

"K-C-S" for AML: KYC, CTR, STR

✅ Unit Summary

  • SOX Act = Enron response. Companies Act 2013 = India reform
  • AML: KYC, CTR (>10 lakh), STR
  • Future: ESG integration, digital governance, climate risk disclosure
