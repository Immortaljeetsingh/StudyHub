Block-01: R&D Fundamentals

Unit 1: R&D Management — An Overview

📖 Definition

R&D = Systematic investigation to discover new knowledge and applications.

  • Types: Basic Research, Applied Research, Experimental Development
  • R&D as investment (long-term) vs cost (short-term accounting view)
  • R&D intensity = R&D expenditure / Sales revenue

Key Definitions

  • Basic Research: Original investigation to gain new knowledge without specific application
  • Applied Research: Directed toward a specific practical aim or objective
  • Experimental Development: Systematic work using existing knowledge to produce new materials, products, or processes
  • R&D Intensity: Ratio of R&D spending to sales revenue; indicator of innovation commitment

Unit 2: Innovation Concepts & Types

📖 Definition

Innovation = converting ideas into commercially viable products/processes/services.

  • Types: Product, Process, Service, Business Model, Organizational
  • Incremental vs Radical vs Disruptive innovation
  • Innovation funnel: many ideas > few successful innovations

Key Definitions

  • Innovation: Implementation of a new or significantly improved product, process, or method
  • Incremental Innovation: Small improvements to existing products/processes
  • Radical Innovation: Breakthrough changes creating new markets or disrupting existing ones
  • Disruptive Innovation: Innovation that creates new market by displacing established competitors (Christensen)
  • Open Innovation: Using external ideas and paths to market alongside internal R&D (Chesbrough)

Unit 3: Technology Lifecycle & Diffusion

📖 Definition

Technology evolves through S-curve: introduction, growth, maturity, decline. Diffusion follows bell curve: innovators (2.5%) > early adopters (13.5%) > early majority (34%) > late majority (34%) > laggards (16%).

  • Technology Push: Innovation driven by R&D capabilities (supply-side)
  • Market Pull: Innovation driven by customer needs (demand-side)
  • Crossing the Chasm: Gap between early adopters and early majority (Moore)

Key Definitions

  • Technology Lifecycle: Stages a technology goes through from emergence to obsolescence
  • Technology Diffusion: Process by which innovation spreads through market/social system
  • Crossing the Chasm: Gap between early adopters and early majority (Moore)

Unit 4: National Systems of Innovation

📖 Definition

Innovation system = network of institutions affecting how technology is developed and diffused.

  • Triple Helix Model: University-Industry-Government interaction
  • National Innovation System (NIS): policies, institutions, and practices supporting innovation
  • India's NIS: DST, DSIR, CSIR (37 national labs), IITs, NITs, startup ecosystem

Key Definitions

  • National Innovation System (NIS): Set of institutions whose interactions determine innovative performance of national firms
  • Triple Helix Model: Interaction between university, industry, and government for innovation
  • Science & Technology Policy: Government framework for directing R&D investments

India's Innovation Ecosystem

  • DST (Department of Science & Technology) — policy and funding
  • CSIR (Council of Scientific & Industrial Research) — 37 national labs
  • DSIR (Department of Scientific & Industrial Research) — industrial R&D recognition
  • Atal Innovation Mission, Startup India, Make in India

Block-02: R&D Strategy & Planning

Unit 5: R&D Strategy Formulation

📖 Definition

R&D strategy must align with corporate and business strategy.

  • Three R&D strategies: Leader, Fast Follower, Niche
  • Technology portfolio management (balance risk/reward)
  • R&D budget allocation: offensive vs defensive R&D

Key Definitions

  • R&D Strategy: Plan for managing research and development to achieve competitive advantage
  • Technology Portfolio: Collection of R&D projects balanced across risk, time horizon, and strategic importance
  • Offensive R&D: Creating new products/processes to be first mover
  • Defensive R&D: Improving existing products/processes to protect market position

Apple invests heavily in offensive R&D (new categories). Samsung is a fast follower (improves on Apple's innovations quickly).

Link R&D strategy to Porter's generic strategies. Know when each strategy is appropriate.

  • Align R&D strategy with corporate and business strategy
  • Technology portfolio: balance risk/reward across projects

📖 Definition
  • Centralized R&D: All R&D activities under one organizational unit
  • Decentralized R&D: R&D distributed across business units/divisions
  • Matrix Structure: Dual reporting (functional + project) for R&D personnel
  • Cross-functional teams for innovation
  • R&D labs: corporate, divisional, university-affiliated

Google uses hybrid structure — centralized Google Research (AI/ML) plus decentralized product teams (Search, Cloud, Android). 3M uses matrix structure where researchers report to both lab directors and product divisions, enabling cross-pollination of ideas.

Know when each structure is appropriate. Large diversified firms often use hybrid structures.

Unit 8: R&D Project Selection & Evaluation

📖 Definition
  • Project selection criteria: strategic fit, technical feasibility, market potential, financial return
  • Methods: scoring models, benefit-cost analysis, real options analysis
  • Portfolio approach: balance exploratory, platform, and incremental projects
  • TRL (Technology Readiness Level): Scale from 1 (basic principles) to 9 (proven technology)

Key Definitions

  • Project Scoring Model: Weighted criteria approach for evaluating and ranking R&D projects
  • Real Options Analysis: Treats R&D investment as options that can be exercised based on future information
  • Technology Readiness Level (TRL): Scale from 1 (basic principles) to 9 (proven technology)

TRL Scale

  • TRL 1-3: Concept/Feasibility (basic research)
  • TRL 4-6: Development (applied research)
  • TRL 7-9: Deployment (demonstration to proven)
💡 Real-World Example

NASA uses TRL scale — James Webb Space Telescope progressed from TRL 1 (1996) to TRL 9 (2021 launch). ISRO's Chandrayaan-3 used scoring models to prioritize landing technology over orbiter improvements.

❗ Exam Tip

Know TRL scale and project selection methods. Real options analysis is a sophisticated approach worth explaining.

🧠 Memory Aid

"SST-FTRL" — Strategic fit, Scoring model, Technical feasibility, Financial return, TRL levels

✅ Unit Summary

  • TRL: 9-level scale from basic research to proven technology
  • Selection criteria: strategic fit, feasibility, market potential, financial return
  • Portfolio approach: balance exploratory, platform, and incremental projects

Unit 11: Technology Transfer

📖 Definition

Technology Transfer = movement of technology from source to recipient.

  • Modes: licensing, joint ventures, FDI, turnkey projects, technical agreements
  • Absorptive capacity: ability to recognize, assimilate, and apply new knowledge
  • Barriers: tacit knowledge, cultural differences, IP concerns

Key Definitions

  • Technology Transfer: Process of transferring technology, knowledge, and skills from one organization to another
  • Absorptive Capacity: Ability of a firm to recognize the value of new external information, assimilate it, and apply it
  • Tacit Knowledge: Knowledge that is difficult to transfer through writing or verbalization

Block-04: Intellectual Property

IPR = legal rights protecting creations of the mind.

  • TRIPS Agreement (WTO) sets minimum IP standards

  • TRIPS Agreement: WTO agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights
  • Fair Use: Limited use of copyrighted material without permission for education, criticism, etc.

Unit 14: Patent Law & Practice

📖 Definition
  • Patent = exclusive right to make, use, sell an invention for limited period
  • Patentability: Novelty, Non-obviousness, Utility, Sufficient disclosure
  • Indian Patents Act 1970 (amended 2005 for TRIPS compliance)
  • Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) for international filing
  • Section 3d = anti-evergreening provision

Key Definitions

  • Patent: Exclusive right granted for an invention, providing the patent holder with the right to decide how the invention can be used by others
  • Prior Art: All publicly available information before filing date that relates to invention's novelty
  • Patent Infringement: Unauthorized use, manufacture, or sale of a patented invention

  • Methods: cost approach, market approach, income approach (DCF)
  • Licensing: granting permission to use IP in exchange for royalties
  • Royalty structures: lump sum, running royalty, milestone payments

Key Definitions

  • Technology Valuation: Process of determining the monetary value of technology or intellectual property
  • Licensing Agreement: Contract granting permission to use IP under specified terms and conditions
  • Royalty: Payment made to IP holder for the right to use their technology

Licensing Structures

❗ Exam Tip

📖 One-Liner Concepts
  • R&D types: basic research, applied research, experimental development
  • Innovation types: incremental, radical, disruptive, open
  • Technology follows S-curve: introduction, growth, maturity, decline
  • Rogers' adoption: innovators > early adopters > early majority > late majority > laggards
  • Triple Helix: university-industry-government interaction
  • R&D strategies: leader, fast follower, niche, imitator
  • Stage-gate process separates innovation into stages with decision gates
  • IP types: patent (20y), copyright (life+60y), trademark (10y renewable), trade secret (indefinite)
  • Patentability: novelty, non-obviousness, utility, sufficient disclosure
  • Valuation: cost approach, market approach, income approach (DCF)
  • India's R&D: DST, CSIR, DSIR, Atal Innovation Mission
  • Frugal innovation = jugaad = doing more with less
  • Reverse innovation: developed in emerging markets, adapted for developed markets
  • Absorptive capacity = recognize + assimilate + apply new knowledge
  • Absorption stages: Acquisition > Assimilation > Adaptation > Improvement > Innovation

📋 Key Formulas & Ratios

  • R&D Intensity = R&D Expenditure / Sales Revenue
  • India's R&D spending: ~0.7% of GDP
  • Patent duration: 20 years from filing date
  • Copyright: Life + 60 years (India)
  • Trademark: 10 years, renewable
  • Industrial Design: 15 years
  • GI: 10 years, renewable
