Q1. Explain the three financial decisions of a firm and show how they are inter-related. (Unit 1, 10 Marks)

Answer: The three financial decisions are the investment, financing and dividend decisions. The investment decision decides which assets to buy; it is the most important financial decision because it fixes the total amount of assets, their composition and the business risk of the firm. The financing decision decides how the assets will be funded, i.e., the debt–equity mix, which determines financial risk. The dividend decision decides how much profit to distribute and how much to retain, thereby affecting internal financing and shareholder wealth. These decisions are inter-related. The NPV rule links them: NPV = C0 + C1/(1+r), where the project cash flows come from the investment decision and the discount rate r is the cost of capital set by the financing decision. The dividend decision supplies retained earnings, an internal source of equity capital for financing. For example, Reliance Jio's Rs 1.5 lakh crore telecom investment (investment decision) was funded through internal accruals, debt and equity (financing decision), while its dividend policy balances payout and reinvestment (dividend decision). Because all three decisions are taken simultaneously and jointly determine firm value, none can be taken in isolation.

Key points:

  • The three financial decisions are the investment, financing and dividend decisions.
  • For example, Reliance Jio's Rs 1.5 lakh crore telecom investment (investment decision) was funded through internal accruals, debt and equity (financing decision), while its dividend policy balances payout and reinvestment (dividend decision).
  • The investment decision decides which assets to buy; it is the most important financial decision because it fixes the total amount of assets, their composition and the business risk of the firm.

Takeaway: Because all three decisions are taken simultaneously and jointly determine firm value, none can be taken in isolation.

Q2. What is the weighted average cost of capital? A company has equity of Rs 60 lakh at Ke = 15% and debt of Rs 40 lakh at Kd = 10%. If the tax rate is 30%, compute the WACC and explain the tax shield on debt. (Unit 2, 10 Marks)

Answer: The weighted average cost of capital (WACC) is the average rate of return required by all providers of capital — equity, debt and preference — weighted by their proportion in the capital structure. It is the minimum return a firm must earn on its investments to satisfy all investors. The component costs are: cost of equity (Ke) using CAPM, Ke = Rf + Beta × (Rm − Rf), or the dividend growth model; cost of debt (Kd) = interest × (1 − tax); and cost of preference (Kp) = dividend / net proceeds. Here, WACC = (E/V × Ke) + (D/V × Kd × (1 − t)) = (60/100 × 15%) + (40/100 × 10% × 0.70) = 9% + 2.8% = 11.8%. The firm must therefore earn at least 11.8% on its projects. The tax shield arises because interest on debt is tax-deductible: it reduces taxable profit, so the effective cost of debt is lower than the coupon rate, which is why Kd is multiplied by (1 − t). A lower WACC is a competitive advantage because more projects become viable. For example, Infosys, which is largely equity-funded, enjoys a low WACC, while highly leveraged firms such as Bharti Airtel carry a higher WACC.

Key points:

  • The weighted average cost of capital (WACC) is the average rate of return required by all providers of capital — equity, debt and preference — weighted by their proportion in the capital structure.
  • For example, Infosys, which is largely equity-funded, enjoys a low WACC, while highly leveraged firms such as Bharti Airtel carry a higher WACC.
  • It is the minimum return a firm must earn on its investments to satisfy all investors.

Takeaway: For example, Infosys, which is largely equity-funded, enjoys a low WACC, while highly leveraged firms such as Bharti Airtel carry a higher WACC.

Q3. Project A costs Rs 10,000 and returns Rs 15,000 in Year 1 (IRR = 50%). Project B costs Rs 1,00,000 and returns Rs 1,30,000 in Year 1 (IRR = 30%). The cost of capital is 10%. Which project should be accepted and why? (Unit 5, 10 Marks)

Answer: Compute the NPV of both projects. NPV of A = 15,000/1.10 − 10,000 = Rs 3,636. NPV of B = 1,30,000/1.10 − 1,00,000 = Rs 18,182. On IRR, A looks better (50% > 30%), but NPV clearly prefers B (Rs 18,182 > Rs 3,636), and for mutually exclusive projects NPV is always reliable. First, NPV assumes reinvestment of intermediate cash flows at the cost of capital, which is realistic, whereas IRR assumes reinvestment at the IRR itself, which is usually unrealistic. Second, NPV measures value added in rupees and therefore respects project scale, while IRR is a percentage and ignores size. Third, non-conventional cash flows can produce multiple IRRs, while NPV always gives a unique answer. Hence Project B should be accepted because it adds more value to the firm. As a check, the profitability index of B is (1,30,000/1.10) / 1,00,000 = 1.18 > 1, consistent with acceptance. The decision rules used here are: accept if NPV > 0, accept if IRR > cost of capital, and accept if PI > 1.

Key points:

  • Compute the NPV of both projects.
  • NPV of A = 15,000/1.10 − 10,000 = Rs 3,636.
  • The decision rules used here are: accept if NPV > 0, accept if IRR > cost of capital, and accept if PI > 1.

Takeaway: The decision rules used here are: accept if NPV > 0, accept if IRR > cost of capital, and accept if PI > 1.

Q4. An activity in a PERT network has an optimistic time of 4 days, a most likely time of 7 days and a pessimistic time of 16 days. Compute the expected time, variance and standard deviation, and explain the critical path. (Unit 6, 10 Marks)

Answer: PERT uses three time estimates: optimistic (O), most likely (M) and pessimistic (P). Expected time = (O + 4M + P)/6 = (4 + 4 × 7 + 16)/6 = (4 + 28 + 16)/6 = 48/6 = 8 days. Variance = [(P − O)/6]² = [(16 − 4)/6]² = (12/6)² = 4 days², and standard deviation = √4 = 2 days. PERT is probabilistic and event-oriented, and is best suited to research and uncertain projects, while CPM uses a single time estimate, is deterministic and activity-oriented, and suits construction and routine projects. The critical path is the longest sequence of activities through the network; it determines the minimum project duration, and every activity on it has zero float, so any delay in such an activity directly delays the whole project — for example, laying the foundation in a construction project. The project completion time is the sum of expected times on the critical path, and the probability of completing by a target date is computed using the variance of that path. Gantt charts are a simple visual way to track the same schedule.

Key points:

  • PERT uses three time estimates: optimistic (O), most likely (M) and pessimistic (P).
  • The critical path is the longest sequence of activities through the network; it determines the minimum project duration, and every activity on it has zero float, so any delay in such an activity directly delays the whole project — for example, laying the foundation in a construction project.
  • Expected time = (O + 4M + P)/6 = (4 + 4 × 7 + 16)/6 = (4 + 28 + 16)/6 = 48/6 = 8 days.

Takeaway: Gantt charts are a simple visual way to track the same schedule.
