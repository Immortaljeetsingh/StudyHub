Block-01: Introduction to Human Resource Management

Unit 1: Concept & Evolution of HRM

📖 Definition: Human Resource Management

"The process of acquiring, training, appraising, compensating, and attending to employees' labor relations, health, safety, and fairness concerns." — Gary Dessler

HRM is the art of procuring, developing, and maintaining a competent workforce to achieve organizational goals while satisfying individual employee needs.

1.1 — The Three Words of HRM

Human: The workforce — people who contribute skills, time, and effort to the organization.

Resource: Workforce as valuable assets, not costs. Human capital is the most critical competitive advantage.

Management: Efficient planning, organizing, directing, and controlling of human capital to achieve goals.

1.3 — Objectives of HRM

🏢 Organizational Objectives
  • Achieve goals through effective human capital utilization
  • Build competitive advantage via superior talent
  • Ensure legal compliance and ethical practices
  • Maximize return on human resource investment
👤 Individual Objectives
  • Career growth and fair compensation
  • Safe and healthy work environment
  • Skill development and personal growth
  • Job satisfaction and work-life balance

1.4 — Nature of HRM

  1. Interdisciplinary: Draws from psychology, sociology, economics, and management
  2. People-Centric: Focuses on human behavior, motivation, and development
  3. Dynamic: Continuously evolving with organizational and environmental changes
  4. Strategic: Aligned with organizational goals and competitive advantage
  5. Collaborative: Involves line managers and employees at all levels
💡 Indian Example: TCS

TCS employs 6 lakh+ people globally. Its HR evolution from a basic personnel department to the "TCS Learning Hub" with AI-driven skill mapping represents Strategic HRM in action. TCS uses iEvolve, an internal learning platform, to train employees on emerging technologies.

🧠 Memory Aid

A-C-Q-M-R: Acquiring → Compensation → Quality workforce → Maintaining → Retention

⚠️ Exam Tip

Efficiency vs Effectiveness: Efficiency = "doing things right" (minimizing cost, time, resources). Effectiveness = "doing the right things" (achieving goals, creating value). HRM must balance BOTH. A company can be efficient in hiring (fast, cheap) but ineffective (wrong people). Always mention both dimensions in exam answers about HRM objectives.

📖 Case Study: Tata Group — Pioneer of Indian HRM

Tata Group has been at the forefront of Indian HRM for over a century. In 1912, Tata Steel introduced the 8-hour workday, free medical aid, and provident fund — decades before Indian labor laws mandated them. J.R.D. Tata established the Tata Administrative Service (TAS) in 1956, modeled on the IAS, to create a pipeline of future business leaders. Today, Tata companies are known for progressive HR policies including generous maternity leave, mental health support, and flexible work arrangements, setting benchmarks for Indian industry.

Key Takeaway: HRM evolved from welfare to strategic partner. Modern HRM is interdisciplinary, people-centric, and aligned with business strategy.

✅ Unit 1 Summary

  • HRM = Acquiring, Training, Appraising, Compensating, Maintaining workforce
  • Evolution: Welfare → Personnel → HRM → Strategic HRM → Digital HR
  • Objectives: Organizational (efficiency, compliance) + Individual (growth, satisfaction)
  • Nature: Interdisciplinary, people-centric, dynamic, strategic, collaborative

⚠️ Exam Tip

Managerial vs Operative Functions: Managerial functions (P-O-S-D-C) are performed by ALL managers at every level. Operative functions are specific to the HR department. Don't confuse them — a line manager does Planning and Directing, but the HR department handles Recruitment and Compensation. Both work together for effective HRM.

📖 Case Study: Hindustan Unilever (HUL) — HR Excellence

HUL's HR analytics team uses predictive models to identify flight risk employees and intervene proactively, reducing attrition to under 5% in a sector averaging 15%.

Key Takeaway: Managerial functions (Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Controlling) are done by ALL managers. Operative functions (Employment, Compensation, Development, Integration, Maintenance) are done by the HR department specifically.

InternalExternalStrategyAlignmentEvaluation
⚠️ Exam Tip

Harvard vs Michigan Model: Harvard Model = SOFT HRM (people are valuable assets, focus on commitment, trust, loyalty). Michigan Model = HARD HRM (people are resources to be deployed efficiently, focus on strategic fit). Exam questions often ask you to compare these two — remember: Harvard = Heart (emotions), Michigan = Machine (efficiency).

Key Takeaway: Strategic HRM = HR practices aligned with overall business strategy. The Harvard Model focuses on stakeholder interests (soft HRM), while the Michigan Model focuses on strategic fit (hard HRM).

  • Strategic HRM Framework: Scan → Formulate → Implement → Evaluate
  • Harvard Model (stakeholder-focused) vs Michigan Model (strategic-fit focused)

4.3 — Demand Forecasting Methods

👤 Qualitative Methods
  • Delphi Technique: Expert opinions iteratively refined
  • Managerial Judgment: Top-down or bottom-up estimates
  • Nominal Group Technique: Structured group decision-making
📊 Quantitative Methods
  • Trend Analysis: Historical data extrapolation
  • Ratio Analysis: Staff-to-output ratios
  • Work Study Method: Time and motion studies
  • Regression Analysis: Statistical relationship modeling

4.4 — Supply Forecasting Methods

  • 🔄 Replacement Charts: Visual displays showing who can replace whom in key positions
  • 📋 Skills Inventory: Database of employee skills, qualifications, and experience
  • 🔄 Markov Analysis: Transition probabilities between job levels over time
  • 🎯 Succession Planning: Identifying and developing future leaders for key roles
  • 📈 Turnover Analysis: Predicting attrition patterns based on historical data
💡 Indian Example: Reliance Industries

Reliance Industries uses sophisticated HR planning tools across its refinery and Jio divisions to manage 2+ lakh employees. Their detailed succession planning system identifies high-potential employees at every level, with Jio specifically leveraging data analytics to forecast technology skill requirements 3–5 years ahead.

🧠 Memory Aid

D-S-G-A: Demand forecast → Supply forecast → Gap analysis → Action plan

⚠️ Exam Tip

Demand vs Supply Forecasting: Demand forecasting answers "How many people do we NEED?" while supply forecasting answers "How many people do we HAVE or can GET?" The gap between them drives recruitment, training, or restructuring decisions. Never confuse the two in exam answers.

📖 Case Study: Infosys — Data-Driven HRP

Infosys uses predictive analytics for HRP across 300,000+ employees. Their "Talent Edge" platform analyzes attrition patterns, skill availability, and project pipeline to forecast demand 12–18 months ahead. When they identified a shortage of cloud architects in 2022, they launched an internal reskilling program training 5,000 engineers, reducing external hiring costs by 40%. This demonstrates how HRP bridges the demand-supply gap proactively.

Key Takeaway: HRP ensures no surplus and no shortage. Demand = what we need, Supply = what we have, Gap = the difference that needs action.

✅ Unit 4 Summary

  • HRP: Forecasting future demand and supply of the right people
  • Process: Scan → Demand → Supply → Gap → Action → Implement → Monitor
  • Demand Methods: Delphi, Judgment, Trend, Ratio, Work Study, Regression
  • Supply Methods: Replacement Charts, Skills Inventory, Markov, Succession Planning

⚠️ Exam Tip

Job Analysis = What the job IS. Job Specification = Who is NEEDED. Job Evaluation = How much the job is WORTH. Do not confuse these three concepts. Also: Job Enlargement = adding more tasks at the SAME level (horizontal). Job Enrichment = adding more responsibility and autonomy (vertical). Enlargement = breadth, Enrichment = depth.

  • Job Evaluation: Ranking, Classification, Point Method, Factor Comparison

💡 Indian Example: HDFC Bank

🧠 Memory Aid

S-C-G-I-O: Screen → Call → Group discussion → Interview → Offer

⚠️ Exam Tip

Recruitment vs Selection: Recruitment is POSITIVE (attracting as many qualified candidates as possible). Selection is NEGATIVE (rejecting unsuitable candidates to find the best fit). This distinction is a frequent exam question — remember: Recruitment = Marketing, Selection = Filtering.

📖 Case Study: TCS — AI-Powered Recruitment

TCS receives 3+ lakh applications annually. Their AI-driven recruitment platform "TCS iON" automates resume screening using NLP, conducts initial aptitude assessments online, and uses video interview analytics to evaluate communication skills. This reduced their time-to-hire from 45 days to 18 days while improving selection quality. The system also flags unconscious bias by anonymizing candidate data during initial screening rounds.

Key Takeaway: Recruitment = attracting candidates (positive process). Selection = choosing the best (negative process). Placement = putting them in the right role.

✅ Unit 6 Summary

  • Recruitment: Internal (promotions, transfers, referrals) vs External (campus, portals, agencies)
  • Selection Process: Screen → Test → GD → Interview → Reference → Medical → Offer
  • Interview Types: Structured, Behavioral (STAR), Situational, Panel, Stress
  • Legal: EEO, non-discrimination, privacy rights, documentation

⚠️ Exam Tip

BARS vs MBO vs 360°: BARS uses behavior-based anchors for each performance dimension. MBO sets measurable objectives jointly by manager and employee. 360° collects feedback from multiple sources (self, supervisor, peers, subordinates, customers). Know when to recommend each: BARS for behavioral roles, MBO for goal-driven roles, 360° for leadership development.

📖 Case Study: Wipro — Performance Transformation

Wipro moved from annual appraisals to a continuous feedback model called "Spirit of Wipro." They replaced the traditional bell curve with a "talent grid" that evaluates both performance and potential. Managers conduct quarterly "connect" conversations instead of annual reviews. This shift reduced appraisal-related grievances by 60% and improved employee engagement scores by 25%, demonstrating how modern appraisal methods can transform organizational culture.

Key Takeaway: 360-degree feedback = Self + Supervisor + Peers + Subordinates + Customers. Modern methods (BARS, MBO, 360°) are more objective than traditional methods.

✅ Unit 8 Summary

  • Purpose: Development, Compensation, Training, Succession, Documentation
  • Traditional: Rating scales, Essay, Critical Incident, Confidential Report
  • Modern: BARS, MBO, 360° Feedback, Assessment Centers
  • Errors: Halo, Recency, Central Tendency, Leniency

Training vs Development vs Education: Training = current job skills (short-term, specific). Development = future career growth (long-term, broad). Off-the-job methods (lectures, case studies, simulations) are best for theoretical knowledge.

📖 Case Study: Mahindra & Mahindra — Corporate University

Key Takeaway: Training = present job, Development = future roles, Education = broader knowledge. Know the Kirkpatrick model for evaluation: Reaction, Learning, Behavior, Results.

⚠️ Exam Tip

Grievance vs Complaint: A grievance is a formal, documented feeling of dissatisfaction related to employment (usually backed by evidence). A complaint may be informal and not always documented. Grievance handling follows a structured process (present → acknowledge → investigate → discuss → decide → appeal). Progressive discipline starts with the mildest form (oral warning) and escalates only if needed.

Key Takeaway: Prevention is better than cure. Clear policies, regular training, open communication channels, and a positive work environment prevent most grievances.

C-C-C-A: Collective Bargaining → Conciliation → Compulsory Arbitration → Award (Dispute Resolution Chain)

⚠️ Exam Tip

Collective Bargaining vs Conciliation vs Arbitration: Collective Bargaining = direct negotiation between union and management (no third party). Conciliation = a third party facilitates discussion but cannot impose a solution. Arbitration = a third party makes a BINDING decision. Strikes and lockouts are weapons of last resort in industrial disputes. The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 provides for conciliation, arbitration, and adjudication.

Key Takeaway: Collective Bargaining = negotiations between union and management. Collective Agreement = the resulting contract. Conciliation = mediation by third party. Arbitration = binding decision by third party. Know the difference for exam questions.
