Unit 1: Business Ethics — An Overview

📖 Definition

Business Ethics = Study of business situations, activities, and decisions where issues of right and wrong are addressed (Crane & Matten).

Law = Common minimum acceptable standard in any civilized society; codified and enforceable.

Ethics = Moral principles guiding behavior; not codified, contextual, and evolving.

📊 Ethics vs Law
Law = Minimum Standard (Codified)
↓
Ethics = Higher Standard (Contextual)
↓
"Business ethics begins where the Law ends"
💡 Real-World Example

Volkswagen emission scandal (Dieselgate) — EPA's stringent emission norms led VW to resort to unethical practices, later recalling 11,000 cars. Shows how sustainability pressures can lead to ethical breaches.

🧠 Memory Aid

"EEL" = Ethics Evolves from Law (ethics goes beyond what law requires)

✅ Unit Summary

  • Ethics goes beyond law — contextual and evolving
  • Relevance in globalization: corporates between state and civil society
  • Sustainability driving regulatory frameworks

Unit 2: Concepts and Theories of Business Ethics

📖 Key Theories

Consequentialism (Teleology): Actions judged by outcomes/consequences.

Deontology (Kant): Actions judged by inherent rightness, regardless of outcomes. Categorical Imperative — act only according to rules you could will to be universal laws.

Utilitarianism (Bentham/Mill): Greatest good for greatest number.

Virtue Ethics (Aristotle): Focus on character and moral virtues.

Social Contract Theory (Rawls): Justice as fairness behind "veil of ignorance."

Stakeholder Theory (Freeman): Balance interests of all stakeholders.

📊 Ethical Theories Spectrum
Consequentialism (Outcomes matter)
↓
Deontology (Duty matters)
↓
Virtue Ethics (Character matters)
💡 Real-World Example

Pharma company pricing AIDS drugs affordably in Africa (utilitarian approach) vs. maintaining high prices to fund R&D (consequentialist approach to long-term innovation).

🧠 Memory Aid

"DUTCH" = Deontology, Utilitarianism, Teleology, Character (virtue), Hypothetical (social contract)

✅ Unit Summary

  • Two schools: Consequentialism (outcomes) vs Non-consequentialism (duty)
  • Compare teleological vs deontological with examples — examiners love this
  • Stakeholder Theory balances ALL interests

Unit 3: Ethical Dilemmas

📖 Definition

Ethical Dilemma = Situation where all available options are ethically problematic.

Ethical Navigation Wheel (Kvalnes & Ovarenget): Framework for navigating dilemmas.

Moral Compass (Lynn Paine): Guide for ethical decision-making.

Social Accounting = Measuring and reporting social and environmental impact.

💡 Real-World Example

Online gaming companies designing addictive games — balancing profit maximization against potential harm to youth mental health. Is it ethical to create products designed to be addictive?

🧠 Memory Aid

"NICE" = Navigation wheel, Identify stakeholders, Check points, Evaluate alternatives

✅ Unit Summary

  • Use structured framework: identify stakeholders, apply theories, evaluate alternatives, recommend
  • Link between ethical dilemmas, stakeholder management, and social audit

Unit 4: Ethics in Business (IBS Framework)

📖 IBS Framework

Individual Level: Personal values, moral development, cognitive moral development.

Business Level: Organizational culture, ethical climate, codes of conduct.

Society Level: Social norms, cultural values, legal frameworks.

Corporate Governance = System of rules, practices, and processes by which a company is directed and controlled.

📊 Shareholder vs Stakeholder Debate
Friedman: Maximize shareholder value
VS
Freeman: Balance ALL stakeholder interests
💡 Real-World Example

The Enron scandal (2001) — failure of corporate governance, auditor independence, and ethical leadership led to one of the largest corporate bankruptcies in history.

🧠 Memory Aid

"IBS" = Individual, Business, Society (three levels of ethical analysis)

✅ Unit Summary

  • IBS = Individual, Business, Society levels
  • Freeman's 4 propositions: Separation Fallacy, Integration Thesis, Open Question, Responsibility
  • Code of Ethics = formal document outlining ethical standards

Unit 5: CSR — An Overview

📖 Definition

CSR = Company's commitment to managing social, environmental, and economic effects responsibly and in line with public expectations.

Corporate Citizenship = Relationship a company develops with its stakeholders.

Ethical Consumerism = Purchase decisions based on human rights, social justice, environment.

Socially Responsible Investment (SRI) = Investment considering financial returns AND social/environmental impact.

💡 Real-World Example

BP Amoco shareholders supporting climate change resolutions; Greenpeace boycott of Shell; Coca-Cola pesticide controversy in India (CSE report 2003).

🧠 Memory Aid

"SEE" = Stakeholders, Ethics, Environment (three pillars of business ethics)

✅ Unit Summary

  • Evolution from Shareholder Primacy to Stakeholder Primacy
  • 4 types of activism: Consumer, CSO, Shareholder, Employee
  • Compare Friedman vs Freeman explicitly in exam

Unit 6: Business Strategy in CSR

📖 Key Concepts

Peripheral Approach: CSR separated from core business (charities, donations).

Integrated Approach: CSR embedded in organizational culture and core competencies.

Cause-Related Marketing (CRM): Linking philanthropy with marketing for reputational gains.

Shared Value (Porter & Kramer): Creating economic value while addressing societal needs.

License to Operate: Social license from communities, not just government permits.

💡 Real-World Example

Cisco Networking Academy trained computer network administrators, minimizing growth constraints while providing job opportunities. Toyota Prius became Toyota's third best-selling vehicle through ethical branding.

🧠 Memory Aid

"CROSS" = Cost reduction, Reputation, Opportunities, Synergistic value, Stakeholder value

✅ Unit Summary

  • 5 CSR strategy approaches: Cost/risk reduction, Competitive advantage, Reputation, Synergistic value, Multi-stakeholder value
  • Use Porter & Kramer's shared value framework for strategic CSR questions

Unit 7: CSR in Global Context

📖 Key Frameworks

SDGs: 17 UN Sustainable Development Goals for 2030.

Triple Bottom Line (Elkington): People, Planet, Profit.

GRI: Global Reporting Initiative standards for sustainability reporting.

ISO 26000: International standard for social responsibility.

📊 4 Types of Social Activism
Consumer Activism (Boycotts, ethical purchasing)
↓
CSO Activism (NGO campaigns)
↓
Shareholder Activism (Proxy resolutions)
↓
Employee Activism (Whistleblowing)
💡 Real-World Example

Honey Care Africa — unique business model partnering with rural farmers, microfinance institutions, and NGOs to create sustainable income for 7000+ workers while protecting biodiversity.

🧠 Memory Aid

"SDG 17" = 17 Sustainable Development Goals; "4 Activisms" = Consumer, CSO, Shareholder, Employee

✅ Unit Summary

  • Link CSR initiatives to specific SDGs (education = SDG 4, gender = SDG 5, environment = SDGs 13-15)
  • Triple Bottom Line = People, Planet, Profit

Unit 8: Business Ethics and CSR — Linkages

📖 Key Concepts

Ethics and CSR are intertwined — ethics provides the moral foundation for CSR.

Ethics = internal behavior; CSR = external impact on society.

ESG Framework: Environmental, Social, Governance criteria connecting ethics, CSR, and corporate governance.

Social Audit: Systematic evaluation of organization's social impact and ethical performance.

💡 Real-World Example

Abbott Laboratories — strict code of conduct, anti-corruption stance, community health programs in Tanzania, environmental sustainability initiatives.

🧠 Memory Aid

"ESG" = Environmental, Social, Governance (the three pillars connecting ethics and CSR)

✅ Unit Summary

  • Corporate governance bridges ethics and CSR
  • ESG as the bridge concept for modern evaluation

Unit 9: CSR in Indian Context

📖 4 Phases of CSR in India

Phase 1 (1850-1914): Charity & Philanthropy driven by culture, religion (Tatas, Birlas, Godrej).

Phase 2 (1914-1960): Social Development driven by Gandhian Trusteeship theory.

Phase 3 (1960-1980): Mixed Economy, License Raj, public enterprises.

Phase 4 (1980-present): Interface between philanthropic and business approaches, liberalization.

India = First country to mandate CSR by law (2013).

ModelBasisKey Feature
EthicalGandhian TrusteeshipSurplus wealth for society
StatistNehru's SocialismState ownership, legal requirements
LiberalMilton FriedmanMaximize profits within rules
StakeholderEdward FreemanWelfare of all stakeholders
💡 Real-World Example

Tata Chemicals Society for Rural Development (1980) — 30% on wildlife conservation. Infosys Foundation (1996) — social innovation awards. HUL's Project Shakti — empowering rural women as entrepreneurs.

🧠 Memory Aid

"PENS" = Phases (4), Ethical model, NVGs, Statist/Liberal/Stakeholder models

✅ Unit Summary

  • 4 Phases: Charity → Social Dev → Mixed Economy → Business Integration
  • 4 Models: Ethical (Gandhi), Statist (Nehru), Liberal (Friedman), Stakeholder (Freeman)
  • NVGs = 9 principles for social, environmental, economic responsibilities

Unit 10: CSR Legislations and Policy Guidelines

📖 Companies Act 2013, Section 135

Threshold (any one): Net worth ≥ ₹500 Cr OR Turnover ≥ ₹1,000 Cr OR Net profit ≥ ₹5 Cr.

2% Rule: Must spend 2% of average net profits of last 3 years on CSR.

CSR Committee: Minimum 3 directors, at least 1 independent director.

Schedule VII: Education, healthcare, environment, gender equality, rural development, etc.

2021 Amendments: Admin overheads cap (5%), impact assessment, CSR-1 registration, excess spend carry-forward (3 years).

⚠ Key Rule

"2-3-5 Rule" = 2% of profits, 3-year average, ₹5 Cr net profit threshold. India = first country to give statutory recognition to CSR.

🧠 Memory Aid

"2-3-5 Rule" = 2% of profits, 3-year average, ₹5 Cr threshold; "Section 135" = CSR section

✅ Unit Summary

  • Memorize the 2-3-5 Rule and Section 135
  • Schedule VII = list of approved CSR activities
  • Unspent amount must be transferred to specified fund within 6 months

Unit 11: CSR in Public Sector Units (PSUs)

📖 Key Concepts

CPSEs = Central Public Sector Enterprises with specific CSR guidelines from DPE.

60% Rule: 60% of annual CSR expenditure required for thematic programs.

112 Aspirational Districts identified by NITI Aayog for priority CSR intervention.

School education and healthcare identified as focus themes.

💡 Real-World Example

Mission 2007 — partnership of 180 partners (NASSCOM Foundation, Microsoft India, ICRISAT) to provide IT skills and services including micro-credit and disaster management.

🧠 Memory Aid

"60-112" = 60% of CSR for thematic programs, 112 aspirational districts

✅ Unit Summary

  • CPSE-specific guidelines separate from private sector
  • Thematic approach and aspirational districts focus

Unit 12: CSR Reporting Process & Auditing

📖 Key Concepts

CSR Reporting: Mandatory annual report on CSR activities as part of Board's Report.

BRR: Business Responsibility Reporting — 9 principles for listed companies.

CSR Audit: Verifies compliance with Companies Act provisions.

Impact Assessment: Required for companies with average CSR obligation ≥ ₹10 crore (2021 amendment).

🧠 Memory Aid

"BRR 9" = 9 principles of Business Responsibility Reporting; "RIPE" = Reporting, Impact assessment, Policy disclosure, Expenditure tracking

✅ Unit Summary

  • CSR reporting = disclosure; CSR audit = verification
  • Must disclose CSR policy on company website
  • Surplus from CSR must be credited back to community/CSR corpus

Unit 13: Roles and Responsibilities of CSR Department

📖 Key Concepts

CSR Committee (Section 135): Minimum 3 directors, 1 independent.

Admin Overhead Cap: 5% of total CSR expenditure (2021 amendment).

Excess CSR Spend: Can be set off against obligations for next 3 years.

CSR Implementing Agency: Must have 3+ years track record, registered with MCA via CSR-1.

Tax Issues: CSR expenditure generally not deductible as business expense.

💡 Real-World Example

Mahindra & Mahindra's Nanhi Kali programme for girls' education; Lifeline Express (hospital on train); Mahindra Hariyali (0.95 million trees planted).

🧠 Memory Aid

"3-1-5" = 3 directors minimum, 1 independent, 5% admin overhead cap

✅ Unit Summary

  • Make vs Buy decision: in-house vs implementing agencies
  • Know CSR Committee composition for public, private, foreign companies

Unit 14: CSR and Sustainable Development

📖 Key Concepts

Sustainable Development: Meeting present needs without compromising future generations (Brundtland Commission, 1987).

Circular Economy: Minimize waste, maximize resource utilization through recycling, reuse, regeneration.

Carbon Neutrality: Net-zero carbon emissions.

ESG Framework: Environmental, Social, Governance criteria for evaluating corporate responsibility.

📊 Triple Bottom Line
People (Social)
↓
Planet (Environmental)
↓
Profit (Financial)
💡 Real-World Example

Mark & Spencer became UK's greenest retailer, pledging carbon neutrality and zero waste to landfill. Toyota Prius pioneered hybrid electric vehicles for mainstream consumers.

🧠 Memory Aid

"PPP" = People, Planet, Profit (Triple Bottom Line); "ESG" = Environmental, Social, Governance

✅ Unit Summary

  • Link sustainable development to SDGs and Triple Bottom Line
  • ESG framework for modern CSR evaluation
  • Circular economy = minimize waste, maximize resources
