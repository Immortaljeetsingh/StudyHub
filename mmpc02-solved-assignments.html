Q1. Discuss the evolution of Human Resource Management and the changing role of the HR function.

Answer: HRM has evolved through five stages. Industrial Welfare (1880s–1910s) began with the Factories Act 1881 and early welfare programs such as Tata Steel's 1912 initiatives, which introduced the eight-hour workday, free medical aid, and provident fund. Personnel Management (1910s–1960s) focused on record keeping and payroll administration. Industrial Relations (1930s–1980s) emphasized unions and collective bargaining, as seen in the Birla Group's labor welfare initiatives. Strategic HRM (1980s–2000s) aligned HR with business strategy, with Infosys building global HR systems in the 1990s to support its global delivery model. Digital HR (2000s–present) uses AI, people analytics, and remote work platforms, such as TCS's AI-driven learning platform iEvolve. The role has changed from a clerical welfare function to a strategic business partner. As Gary Dessler defined it, HRM covers acquiring, training, appraising, and compensating employees, plus labor relations, health, safety, and fairness. J.R.D. Tata's Tata Administrative Service (1956), modeled on the IAS, created a pipeline of future leaders and set benchmarks that Indian industry still follows.

Key points:

  • HRM has evolved through five stages.
  • Industrial Welfare (1880s–1910s) began with the Factories Act 1881 and early welfare programs such as Tata Steel's 1912 initiatives, which introduced the eight-hour workday, free medical aid, and provident fund.
  • Personnel Management (1910s–1960s) focused on record keeping and payroll administration.

Takeaway: Tata's Tata Administrative Service (1956), modeled on the IAS, created a pipeline of future leaders and set benchmarks that Indian industry still follows.

Q2. Differentiate between training and development. Describe the training process.

Answer: Training is a systematic process of enhancing knowledge, skills, and abilities for the employee's current job; it is short-term, specific, has measurable outcomes, and offers immediate application. Development is broader and long-term, preparing employees for future roles through conceptual knowledge and career growth. Training typically uses on-the-job methods such as job instruction, coaching, mentoring, and job rotation, while development leans on off-the-job methods like lectures, case studies, role play, and simulations, supplemented by technology-based e-learning. The training process has five steps: needs assessment to identify skill gaps; setting measurable objectives; program design covering curriculum and methods; implementation of the training; and evaluation of effectiveness. Kirkpatrick's model evaluates at four levels: reaction, learning, behavior, and results. Mahindra's Leadership University runs 500+ programs, and its "Born Digital" initiative trained 15,000 employees in AI, cloud, and analytics, cutting external tech hiring by 40%. Marico invests 2% of its HR budget in training, focusing on digital marketing and data analytics skills.

Key points:

  • Training is a systematic process of enhancing knowledge, skills, and abilities for the employee's current job; it is short-term, specific, has measurable outcomes, and offers immediate application.
  • Training typically uses on-the-job methods such as job instruction, coaching, mentoring, and job rotation, while development leans on off-the-job methods like lectures, case studies, role play, and simulations, supplemented by technology-based e-learning.
  • Development is broader and long-term, preparing employees for future roles through conceptual knowledge and career growth.

Q3. Explain the modern methods of performance appraisal. What rating errors should appraisers avoid?

Answer: Modern appraisal methods include BARS (Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales), which anchor each performance dimension with concrete behavior descriptions; MBO (Management by Objectives), where manager and employee jointly set measurable objectives; 360-degree feedback, which collects ratings from self, supervisor, peers, subordinates, and customers, and is best suited to leadership development; and assessment centers, which combine multiple exercises. These methods are more objective than traditional graphic rating scales, essays, and confidential reports. Common rating errors are the halo effect (one strong trait inflates all ratings), recency bias (judging only recent performance), central tendency (rating everyone average, countered by forced distribution), and leniency or strictness (consistently high or low ratings, countered by calibration sessions). Appraisal serves career development, compensation, training needs identification, succession planning, and legal documentation. Tata Steel's TBEM uses a balanced scorecard of financial, customer, process, and learning measures, while Wipro's "Spirit of Wipro" replaced annual reviews with quarterly "connect" conversations and a talent grid of performance and potential, cutting appraisal grievances by 60%.

Q4. What is collective bargaining? Differentiate between collective bargaining, conciliation, and arbitration.

Answer: Collective bargaining is the direct negotiation between a trade union and management over wages, working conditions, and terms of employment, without any third party; its outcome is a written collective agreement. The process runs through preparation, negotiation, agreement, implementation, and administration. Conciliation is a form of third-party intervention in which the conciliator facilitates discussion between the parties but cannot impose a solution. Arbitration goes further: the arbitrator, a neutral third party, hears both sides and gives a binding decision. Strikes and lockouts are weapons of last resort. Trade unions — craft, industrial, general, or white-collar — are regulated in India under the Trade Unions Act, 1926, and their functions include collective bargaining, representation, welfare, and political action. The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 provides for conciliation, arbitration, and adjudication of disputes. Air India illustrates both the power and the challenges of unionization in the public sector: its recognized unions for pilots (IPG), cabin crew, and ground staff negotiate salary revisions, working hours, and safety protocols.

Key points:

  • Collective bargaining is the direct negotiation between a trade union and management over wages, working conditions, and terms of employment, without any third party; its outcome is a written collective agreement.
  • Trade unions — craft, industrial, general, or white-collar — are regulated in India under the Trade Unions Act, 1926, and their functions include collective bargaining, representation, welfare, and political action.
  • The process runs through preparation, negotiation, agreement, implementation, and administration.
