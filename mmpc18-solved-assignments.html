Q1. Define entrepreneurship. Distinguish between an entrepreneur, a manager and an intrapreneur.

Answer:Entrepreneurship is the capacity to assume risk, accountability and challenges in order to explore opportunities, disrupt market norms and create value. The concept has evolved through three eras of thinking: the risk-bearing era associated with Cantillon, the trait-based era linked to Schumpeter's innovation and creative destruction, and the modern opportunity-based approach of Shane and Venkataraman. An entrepreneur differs from a manager in several ways: the entrepreneur is a risk bearer while the manager avoids risk; the entrepreneur is opportunity-oriented while the manager is resource-oriented; the entrepreneur is motivated by achievement while the manager seeks power; innovation is the entrepreneur's core function while the manager performs routine tasks; and the entrepreneur owns the venture while the manager is hired. An intrapreneur, by contrast, is an entrepreneur within an existing organisation: the company bears the risk and provides resources, while rewards come as salary and bonus rather than profit ownership. Elon Musk exemplifies the entrepreneur through SpaceX and Tesla, while Sundar Pichai's innovations within Google, such as Gmail and Chrome, illustrate the intrapreneur. Present the comparison as a table in your assignment for clarity and marks.

Key points:

  • Answer: Entrepreneurship is the capacity to assume risk, accountability and challenges in order to explore opportunities, disrupt market norms and create value.
  • Elon Musk exemplifies the entrepreneur through SpaceX and Tesla, while Sundar Pichai's innovations within Google, such as Gmail and Chrome, illustrate the intrapreneur.
  • The concept has evolved through three eras of thinking: the risk-bearing era associated with Cantillon, the trait-based era linked to Schumpeter's innovation and creative destruction, and the modern opportunity-based approach of Shane and Venkataraman.

Q2. Explain the contribution of MSMEs to Indian economic development and the key government schemes supporting them.

Answer:Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises are the backbone of the Indian economy. MSMEs are classified by investment in plant and machinery and annual turnover: Micro up to Rs 1 crore, Small up to Rs 10 crore and Medium up to Rs 50 crore, remembered as the 1-10-50 rule. Their contribution follows the 30-45-40 formula: MSMEs contribute roughly 30 percent of GDP, 45 percent of manufacturing output and 40 percent of exports, while employing more than 11 crore people, making them the largest employer after agriculture. They drive inclusive growth, regional development and export promotion; the Tirupur knitwear cluster in Tamil Nadu generates thousands of crores of annual exports from over 800 MSME units. Government support has evolved through the industrial policies of 1948, 1956, 1991 (liberalisation) and 2020 (Atmanirbhar Bharat), supported by schemes such as the MSME Development Act 2006, Make in India, Startup India, Stand-Up India for SC/ST and women entrepreneurs, MUDRA Yojana with loans up to Rs 10 lakh, and PMEGP for employment generation. Institutions like SIDBI, NSIC, KVIC and EDII provide financing and skill support. Quote the 30-45-40 figures precisely in your answer.

Q3. Explain the SCAMPER technique of business idea generation with suitable examples.

Answer:SCAMPER is a seven-step technique for systematically generating and refining business ideas. Each letter stands for an action: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate and Rearrange. Substitute replaces one element with another, such as using bamboo instead of plastic. Combine merges two ideas or products, as the smartphone combined a phone with a camera. Adapt transfers a successful model to another context, as Airbnb adapted the sharing model to housing. Modify changes size, shape or features, like the Mini Cooper offering a small car in a big-car market. Put to another use finds new applications, such as baking soda as a cleaning agent. Eliminate removes unnecessary elements, as Google eliminated clutter from its search page. Rearrange reorders the experience, as Netflix allowed watching anytime against the fixed TV schedule. In the Indian food-delivery space, Zomato combined food ordering, technology and delivery, while Swiggy modified the approach by building a logistics-first model. Ideas generated through SCAMPER are then validated with SWOT analysis and break-even analysis. Present each element with at least one example in your assignment.

Key points:

  • Answer: SCAMPER is a seven-step technique for systematically generating and refining business ideas.
  • Substitute replaces one element with another, such as using bamboo instead of plastic.
  • Each letter stands for an action: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate and Rearrange.

Takeaway: Present each element with at least one example in your assignment.

Q4. Discuss the major challenges of family businesses and the strategies to manage them, with Indian examples.

Answer:Family businesses are organisations where ownership and management are concentrated within a family. They face four major challenges, remembered as S-F-P-G: Succession planning, where a lack of clarity about who takes over creates a leadership vacuum; Family versus business conflict, where personal and professional decisions get mixed, leading to poor decision-making; Professionalization, where hiring non-family managers meets resistance from family members; and Governance issues, where the absence of formal structures creates accountability gaps. Coping strategies include a clear governance structure, professional management, a written family constitution, formal conflict-resolution mechanisms and merit-based promotions. The contrasting cases of Tata and Reliance illustrate these points. The Tata Group maintained professional management through Tata Sons, family oversight through charitable trusts, and smooth succession from Ratan Tata to N. Chandrasekaran. Reliance, by contrast, faced a bitter split between Mukesh and Anil Ambani after Dhirubhai's death in the absence of a formal family constitution, demonstrating why governance structures matter. Present the challenge-strategy pairing as a table in your assignment.

Key points:

  • Answer: Family businesses are organisations where ownership and management are concentrated within a family.
  • The contrasting cases of Tata and Reliance illustrate these points.
  • Coping strategies include a clear governance structure, professional management, a written family constitution, formal conflict-resolution mechanisms and merit-based promotions.
