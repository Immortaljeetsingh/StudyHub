Block-01: Foundations of Business Law

Unit 1: Introduction to Business Law

📖 Definition

Business Law: That portion of the legal system which guarantees orderly conduct of business affairs and settlement of legitimate disputes in a just manner. It is the body of rules governing commercial relationships.

Three Pillars: Law (formal social force, rules laid down by state, backed by enforcement), Rule of Law (laws apply equally to all members of society), Property (right to possess, use, and transfer private resources).

Unit 3: The Partnership Act, 1932

📖 Partnership (Section 4)

Relationship between persons agreeing to share profits of business carried on by all or any of them. Firm: Collective name for partners. Partnership Deed: Written agreement containing terms.

5 Essentials: 1. Minimum 2 persons (max 50 per Companies Act, 2013) 2. Agreement (contract) 3. Share profits 4. Business carried on by all or any acting for all 5. Mutual agency

⚠ Position of Minor

Can be admitted to benefits of partnership (not as partner). Rights: Share of profits, access to accounts. Liability: Limited to share in firm. On attaining majority: 6 months to decide (become partner or leave). If no notice given: deemed to become partner.

⚠ Registration & Dissolution

Registration: Not mandatory, but consequences: Cannot sue third parties, cannot enforce rights against other partners. Dissolution: By agreement, Compulsory (illegal business), By court, By notice (partnership at will).

⚠ LLP (Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008)

Separate legal entity. Limited liability of partners. Perpetual succession. Minimum 2 partners, no maximum. No requirement of partnership deed (LLP Agreement required).

💡 Real-World Example

A law firm with 3 partners: one active (manages cases), one sleeping (invested capital), one nominal (gave name for reputation). All liable to third parties, but only active partner manages daily operations.

🧠 Memory Aid

"PARTNER" = Profit sharing — Agreement — Registration — Types — Nominal — Equity — Reconstitution

⚠ Exam Tip

Compare partnership types and explain the difference between reconstitution and dissolution. Know the 6 types of partners with their liability and notice requirements.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Partnership: 2+ persons, agreement, profit sharing, mutual agency
  • 6 types of partners: Active, Sleeping, Nominal, Profit-only, Sub, Estoppel
  • Minor can only be admitted to benefits, not as partner
  • LLP: Separate entity, limited liability, perpetual succession
  • Registration not mandatory but required to sue third parties

Unit 5: General Principles of Contracts

📖 Contract (Section 2(h))

Agreement enforceable by law. All contracts are agreements, but not all agreements are contracts.

Essentials (Section 10): 1. Free consent 2. Competency of parties 3. Lawful consideration 4. Lawful object 5. Not expressly void

▶
Indian Contract Act Basics
▶
Vitiating Elements of Contract
⚠ Offer, Acceptance & Consideration

Offer (Section 2(a)): Willingness to do/abstain from doing something. Must be communicated, create legal relations, terms must be certain. Different from invitation to offer.

Acceptance (Section 2(b)): Must be absolute, unqualified, communicated, in prescribed manner.

Consideration (Section 2(d)): Price for promise. Need not be adequate, must be lawful.

⚠ Capacity (Section 11)

Major (18+), Sound mind, Not disqualified by law.

⚠ Doctrine of Frustration (Section 56)

Contract becomes impossible to perform due to supervening impossibility. Both parties discharged. Example: A agrees to sell house to B. Before sale, house destroyed by fire. Contract frustrated — both parties discharged.

💡 Real-World Example

A agrees to sell his house to B. Before sale, house destroyed by fire. Contract frustrated — both parties discharged. This illustrates supervening impossibility under Section 56.

🧠 Memory Aid

"ECLAB-OC" = Essentials — Consent — Lawful — Agreement — Breach + Offer — Capacity

⚠ Exam Tip

Focus on the 5 vitiating elements of consent and their differences. Know breach remedies. Explain the difference between agreement and contract.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Contract = Agreement + Enforceability by law
  • 5 Essentials: Consent, Competency, Lawful consideration, Lawful object, Not void
  • 5 Vitiating elements: Coercion, Undue Influence, Fraud, Misrepresentation, Mistake
  • Breach: Anticipatory vs Actual — Remedies: Damages, Specific Performance, Injunction
  • Doctrine of Frustration: Impossibility discharges both parties

Block-03: International & Banking Law

Unit 6: International Contracts of Sale

📖 CISG

UN Convention on Contracts for International Sale of Goods. Most widely adopted treaty — 94 countries ratified as of 2020. Applies to commercial cross-border sale of goods. Does NOT apply to: consumer goods, auctions, ships, electricity.

History: Ernst Rabel (1930s) → UNIDROIT → Hague Conference (1964) → UNCITRAL → CISG (1980, effective 1988).

▶
CISG Explained
⚠ Key Principle

Party Autonomy: Parties can adopt or exclude CISG provisions. Choice of law allowed. This is the fundamental principle of CISG.

Unit 7: Banking and Allied Regulations

📖 Banking Regulation Act, 1949

RBI = Primary regulator, lender of last resort. Key characteristics: Demand deposits, Payment mechanism, Maturity transformation (short-term liabilities vs long-term assets).

⚠ Basel Norms

Basel I (1988): Risk-weighted assets, minimum 8% capital adequacy. Basel II (2004): Three pillars (minimum capital, supervisory review, market discipline). Basel III (2010): Higher capital requirements, liquidity ratios, leverage ratio.

⚠ FATF & NBFC

FATF: Financial Action Task Force — Anti-money laundering guidelines. NBFC: RBI given regulatory powers over Non-Banking Financial Companies. Cannot accept demand deposits.

⚠ Deposit Insurance

DICGC insures deposits up to Rs. 5 lakhs per depositor, per bank.

💡 Real-World Example

Yes Bank crisis (2020): RBI imposed moratorium, deposits insured up to Rs. 5 lakhs per depositor. Demonstrates the importance of deposit insurance.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Banking Regulation Act 1949 — RBI as primary regulator
  • 3 bank types: Payment, Small Finance, Commercial
  • Basel I/II/III: Progressive capital adequacy standards
  • DICGC: Rs. 5 lakhs deposit insurance
  • FATF: Anti-money laundering guidelines

Unit 8: Foreign Exchange Management (FEMA)

📖 FEMA 1999

Replaced FERA 1973. Shift from regulation to management. 7 chapters, 49 sections. 6 Rules (Central Government), 23 Regulations (RBI). Objective: Facilitate external trade, orderly forex market.

⚠ Key Definitions

Person Resident in India: 182+ days in preceding financial year, or registered/incorporated in India. Authorized Person: Entity authorized to deal in foreign exchange. Foreign Currency: Any currency other than Indian Rupee.

⚠ Penalties & Enforcement

Contravention penalties, Adjudication process, Compounding of offences. Enforcement Directorate investigates FEMA violations.

  • Capital Account (FDI, FPI) vs Current Account (exports, imports)
  • 100% FDI in most sectors via automatic route
  • Structure: 7 chapters, 49 sections, 6 Rules, 23 Regulations

Unit 9: Intellectual Property Rights

📖 IPR

Legal rights protecting creations of the mind. Not a single body of law, but a collection of distinct legal doctrines. Justification: Incentive theory (encourages creation), Non-rivalrous consumption, Trademarks reduce search costs.

⚠ Patent Requirements

1. Novelty: Not anticipated by prior art. 2. Inventive Step: Not obvious to PSITA (Person Skilled In The Art). 3. Industrial Application: Capable of industrial use.

Exclusions from Patent (Section 3): Business methods, computer programmes per se, traditional knowledge, plants/animals.

⚠ Copyright Rights & Moral Rights

Copyright Rights: Reproduce, issue copies, perform, translate, adapt. Moral Rights: 1. Right of attribution (be named as author) 2. Right of integrity (prevent harmful changes).

⚠ TRIPS Agreement

WTO framework for IP protection. Minimum standards for all member countries. Dispute settlement mechanism. Allows compulsory licensing for public health emergencies.

💡 Real-World Example

COVID-19 vaccine patent: 20-year protection, but compulsory licensing allowed under TRIPS for public health emergencies.

🧠 Memory Aid

"PTCG-TD" = Patents — Trademarks — Copyrights — Geographical + TRIPS — Designs

⚠ Exam Tip

Compare the 6 types of IP with their duration and governing law. Know patent requirements and TRIPS provisions.

✅ Unit Summary

  • 6 types: Patents (20yr), Trademarks (10yr), Copyrights (life+60yr), Designs (15yr), GI (10yr), Trade Secrets (unlimited)
  • Patent requirements: Novelty, Inventive Step, Industrial Application
  • TRIPS: WTO framework for IP protection, minimum standards
  • Moral Rights: Attribution + Integrity
  • Justification: Incentive theory, non-rivalrous consumption

Block-05: Data Protection & Environmental Law

Unit 10: Data Protection and Privacy

📖 Data Protection

Safeguarding personal information from misuse. Data Principal: Individual whose data is processed. Data Fiduciary: Entity determining purpose of processing. Data Processor: Processes on behalf of fiduciary.

▶
DPDP Act 2023
⚠ 6 Key Principles

1. Lawfulness 2. Purpose limitation 3. Data minimization 4. Accuracy 5. Storage limitation 6. Integrity & confidentiality

⚠ Consent Requirements

Free, Informed, Specific, Unambiguous, Withdrawable.

⚠ Rights of Data Principal

1. Right to access 2. Right to correction 3. Right to erasure 4. Right to portability 5. Right to object

⚠ DPDP Act

India's data protection framework. Applies to digital personal data. Extraterritorial application. Data breach: Unauthorized access, disclosure, or loss. Notification requirements. Significant penalties for non-compliance.

💡 Real-World Example

Aadhaar data protection: Supreme Court upheld right to privacy as fundamental right (Puttaswamy case), leading to DPDP Act.

🧠 Memory Aid

"DPC-CLR" = Data Protection Consent + Correction — Lawfulness — Rights

⚠ Exam Tip

Compare GDPR (EU) with India's DPDP Act. Know the 6 principles and consent requirements. Explain the rights of data principal.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Key roles: Data Principal, Data Fiduciary, Data Processor
  • 6 Principles: Lawfulness, Purpose limitation, Minimization, Accuracy, Storage limitation, Integrity
  • Consent: Free, Informed, Specific, Unambiguous, Withdrawable
  • DPDP Act: India's data protection framework, extraterritorial application
  • Rights: Access, Correction, Erasure, Portability, Object

Block-06: CSR, Competition & Consumer Protection

Unit 12: Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)

📖 CSR (Section 135)

Companies must spend 2% of average net profits on CSR. Applicable if: Net worth ≥ Rs. 500 crore, OR Turnover ≥ Rs. 1000 crore, OR Net profit ≥ Rs. 5 crore.

▶
CSR in India Explained
⚠ CSR Committee

Board committee to oversee CSR activities. Minimum 3 directors, At least 1 independent director.

⚠ Schedule VII Activities

1. Education 2. Healthcare 3. Environment 4. Rural development 5. Slum area development 6. Gender equality, women empowerment 7. Contributions to PM funds 8. Disaster management 9. Protection of national heritage 10. Armed forces welfare

StandardDescription
UN Global Compact10 principles for responsible business
ISO 26000Social responsibility guidance
GRI StandardsSustainability reporting
⚠ Reporting & Penalty

Annual CSR report in Board's report. CSR policy disclosure. Spending disclosure. Non-compliance requires disclosure of reasons. No specific penalty for not spending.

💡 Real-World Example

Reliance Industries: Spends Rs. 1000+ crore annually on CSR through Reliance Foundation on education, healthcare, rural development.

🧠 Memory Aid

"CSR-2%" = Company Social Responsibility = 2% of profits

⚠ Exam Tip

Explain the 2% rule and Schedule VII activities. Know the CSR committee composition. Compare global standards.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Section 135: 2% of average net profits on CSR
  • CSR Committee: Min 3 directors, 1 independent
  • Schedule VII: 10 activity areas
  • Non-compliance: Must disclose reasons, no specific penalty
  • Global Standards: UN Global Compact, ISO 26000, GRI

Unit 13: Competition Law

📖 Competition Act, 2002

Replaced MRTP Act, 1969. CCI (Competition Commission of India) regulates competition. Objectives: Prevent anti-competitive practices, Promote competition, Protect consumer interests, Ensure freedom of trade.

▶
Competition Act 2002
Agreement TypeBetweenExamples
HorizontalCompetitorsPrice fixing, market allocation, bid rigging
VerticalDifferent levelsTie-in arrangements, exclusive dealing, refusal to deal
⚠ Abuse of Dominant Position

Unfair pricing, Predatory pricing, Denial of market access, Limiting production, Imposing supplementary obligations.

⚠ Combination Regulation

Mergers/acquisitions above threshold need CCI approval. Threshold: Asset value or turnover. CCI examines: market share, competition, consumer interest.

⚠ Penalties & Leniency

Up to 10% of average turnover for anti-competitive agreements. Up to 3x profits for cartel participation. Leniency Program: First applicant up to 100% reduction, subsequent up to 50% reduction.

💡 Real-World Example

CCI fined Google Rs. 136 crore for abusing dominant position in Android mobile ecosystem (2018).

🧠 Memory Aid

"CCI-HVAC" = Competition Commission of India + Horizontal — Vertical — Abuse — Combination

⚠ Exam Tip

Compare horizontal vs vertical agreements and explain abuse of dominance. Know the leniency program and penalties.

✅ Unit Summary

  • Competition Act 2002 — CCI as regulator
  • Horizontal (competitors) vs Vertical (different levels) agreements
  • Abuse of dominance: Unfair pricing, predatory pricing, denial of market
  • Penalties: 10% turnover / 3x profits for cartels
  • Leniency: Up to 100% reduction for first applicant

Contract: Agreement enforceable by law (Section 2(h))

Partnership: Relationship between persons agreeing to share profits (Section 4)

Company: Incorporated association, artificial person, separate legal entity

MOA: Charter document defining company's relationship with outside world

Consideration: Price for promise (Section 2(d))

Free Consent: Consent without coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, mistake
