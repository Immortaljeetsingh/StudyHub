Question Paper - MMPM-07 Integrated Marketing Communication (June 2023)

Actual IGNOU Term-End Examination question paper (June 2023, from the official IGNOU previous-year papers archive). Answer any three questions from Section A; Section B is compulsory.

Course: MMPM-007 - Integrated Marketing Communication (MBA)

Examination: Term-End Examination, June 2023  |  Time: 3 Hours  |  Maximum Marks: 100 (Weightage: 70%)

Note: Answer any three questions from Section A. Section B is compulsory.

Section—A (60 marks)

  • Q1. (a) Explain the following terms: (i) Integrated Marketing Communication (ii) Advertising Campaign (iii) Sales Promotion (iv) Legal Issues. (b) Discuss the steps involved in integrated marketing communication process with a hypothetical example. [10 + 10 marks]
  • Q2. (a) What is Strategy? Explain the strategic decision-making process in promotion mix with the help of a diagram. (b) Distinguish publicity from public relations. Explain the various types of public relations that you are familiar with. [10 + 10 marks]
  • Q3. (a) What is consumer movement? Highlight the main features of consumer movement and its implications on integrated marketing communication. (b) Elaborate the various steps that a sales manager should consider while planning for a sales promotion programme. [10 + 10 marks]
  • Q4. Write short notes on any three of the following: (a) Disadvantages of Personal Selling (b) Humor as an Advertising Appeal (c) Rebates and its characteristics (d) Owned media and its benefits (e) Marketing Collateral and IMC. [20 marks]

Section—B (40 marks, compulsory)

  • Q5. Ather Energy, makers of one of the leading electric scooter manufacturer in the country, has recently upgraded its flagship electric scooter with the third generation iteration, dubbed the Ather Energy 450x Gen 3. The scooter offers a more powerful electric motor that can produce an equivalent of 8.7 bhp of power and an ARAI-certified riding range of 146 km. Besides, Ather’s already well-engineered scooter body is now even better-built thanks to an all aluminium frame. It also gets a new tread profile for the tyres, as well as a new tyre pressure monitoring system accessory. The digital dashboard has been upgraded with more RAM to make operations smoother and easier. All things considered, the Ather 450x Gen 3 is arguably the best electric scooter that you can buy in India. While its top-spec variant 450X with Pro pack is targeted toward the youth, and is priced @ Rs 1.28 lakh ex-showroom Delhi (including state government subsidies), it will take on the likes of the TVS iQube, Ola S1 and S1 Pro, Hero Vida V1, Bajaj Chetak, etc. Assuming that you are being retained by the company as a marketing communication consultant to assess and evaluate the prospects for the new variant and offer your future course of action specifically in the current competitive scenario in this category. Questions: (i) Prepare a precise integrated marketing communication programme for the new scooter. (ii) Propose a suitable advertising campaign for print medium by clearly specifying the creative as well as strategic considerations for the brand. (iii) How would you measure the advertising effectiveness of the above campaign? Discuss. [40 marks]

Solved Answers - MMPM-07 Question Paper

Easy model answers in plain English for every question of the June 2023 paper, grounded in the 14 units of the StudyHub study notes.

Q1(a). Explain: Integrated Marketing Communication, Advertising Campaign, Sales Promotion, Legal Issues. Q1(b). Steps in the IMC process with a hypothetical example.

Answer: IMC is the coordinated “one voice” use of all promotional tools; an advertising campaign is a themed series of ads across media; sales promotion is short-term incentives; legal issues are the laws and codes that keep ads truthful - and the IMC process runs from research through objectives, creative, budget, media and execution to evaluation.

(i) Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC): the coordinated planning and execution of all promotional tools - advertising, sales promotion, public relations, personal selling and direct marketing - so that every channel delivers a single, consistent message. It is not about using many channels; it is about every channel telling the same story, a “one voice” approach formalised by the 4As in the early 1990s and popularised by Schultz and Kotler.

(ii) Advertising Campaign: a series of coordinated, related advertisements built around a single theme or idea and placed across media over a period of time - for example, Amul’s topical campaign that comments on current events through a fresh ad each week.

(iii) Sales Promotion: short-term incentives such as discounts, coupons, contests, free samples and rebates designed to stimulate immediate purchase or trial, usually supporting advertising.

(iv) Legal Issues: laws and codes governing marketing communication - prohibition of misleading and deceptive ads, the ASCI code, the Consumer Protection Act 2019, product-specific rules such as FSSAI norms for food claims, and trademark and copyright protection.

(b) IMC process steps: 1) situational analysis and research, 2) define the target audience, 3) set communication objectives, 4) develop the message and creative, 5) fix the budget, 6) select media and tools, 7) execute, and 8) evaluate and feed back. Hypothetical example: launching a new energy drink for college students - research shows students trust social media and campus events; objective is 60% awareness in three months; the message stresses natural ingredients; media chosen are Instagram, YouTube and campus activations; evaluation uses awareness surveys and sales data.

Key points to remember: IMC = one voice across all tools; campaign = themed series of ads; sales promotion = short-term incentives; legal issues = truth in advertising (ASCI, CPA 2019).

Takeaway: Define each term with a tag (IMC = one voice, campaign = themed series, sales promotion = short-term incentives, legal = ASCI and CPA 2019) and reproduce the IMC process steps for Q1(b).

Q2(a). What is Strategy? Strategic decision-making process in promotion mix with a diagram. Q2(b). Publicity vs public relations; types of public relations.

Answer: Strategy is a long-term plan that decides how the promotion mix - advertising, sales promotion, PR, personal selling and direct marketing - is blended for the target market, while publicity is uncontrolled unpaid news and PR is planned relationship management.

(a) Strategy is a long-term plan of action designed to achieve organisational objectives by deploying resources effectively. In promotion, strategy decides how the promotion mix - advertising, sales promotion, PR, personal selling and direct marketing - will be blended to reach the target market.

The strategic decision-making process in the promotion mix runs in steps: 1) situational and market analysis, 2) setting promotion objectives, 3) determining the budget, 4) developing the message and creative strategy, 5) selecting the promotion mix elements, 6) choosing media, 7) implementation, and 8) evaluation with feedback into the next plan. The StudyHub notes show this as a flow diagram; draw it in the exam to earn marks. A rule of thumb: B2B markets rely more on personal selling, while B2C markets rely more on advertising and sales promotion.

(b) Publicity is unpaid, non-personal communication in news form; it is not controlled by the organisation and enjoys third-party credibility. Public relations is a deliberate, planned, sustained effort to build mutual understanding between an organisation and its publics. Types of PR: media relations, employee relations, community relations, investor relations, government relations, customer relations, and crisis management.

Key points to remember: Strategy = long-term resource deployment; promotion-mix decisions flow from analysis to evaluation; publicity = uncontrolled, credible news; PR = planned relationship management.

Takeaway: Draw the promotion-mix decision flow (analysis to evaluation) and end with the publicity-versus-PR contrast plus the PR types.

Q3(a). Consumer movement: features and implications for IMC. Q3(b). Steps in planning a sales promotion programme.

Answer: The consumer movement protects consumers through SICCHR rights, consumer courts and laws like the CPA 2019, forcing IMC to make truthful claims - while sales promotion planning runs from objectives and technique selection through budget, timing, integration, communication and evaluation.

(a) Consumer movement is the organised effort by consumers, associations and governments to protect consumers’ rights and secure ethical business practices. Its main features are the consumer rights themselves - the right to safety, the right to be informed, the right to choose, the right to be heard, the right to redress and the right to consumer education - supported by consumer organisations, consumer courts and legislation such as the Consumer Protection Act 2019 and the ASCI code in India.

Implications for IMC: advertisers must make truthful, substantiated claims; misleading ads, puffery and exaggerated promises are risky; pricing and offers must be transparent; and because informed consumers reward honest brands, credibility becomes a competitive advantage. SICCHR rights directly constrain the messages, claims and incentives an IMC plan can use.

(b) Planning a sales promotion programme: 1) set clear objectives (trial, repeat purchase, stock off-loading), 2) select the promotion technique (coupons, rebates, contests, premiums, samples), 3) identify the target audience, 4) decide the budget, 5) fix timing and duration, 6) integrate the promotion with advertising and PR, 7) communicate the offer through packaging and point-of-purchase material, 8) implement with trade support, and 9) evaluate results using redemption rates and sales before/after comparison.

Key points to remember: Consumer movement = rights + CPA 2019; it forces honest advertising. Sales promotion planning = objectives → technique → budget → integrate → evaluate.

Takeaway: Consumer movement = the rights that constrain advertising claims (SICCHR plus CPA 2019); sales promotion planning = objectives to evaluation, always integrated with advertising and PR.

Q4. Short notes: Disadvantages of Personal Selling, Humor as an Advertising Appeal, Rebates, Owned Media, Marketing Collateral and IMC.

Answer: Personal selling is costly per contact and hard to control; humor grabs attention but wears out and can overshadow the message; rebates are post-purchase discounts that protect the price image; owned media are free, controlled channels; and collateral carries one consistent message to every touchpoint.

(a) Disadvantages of Personal Selling: high cost per contact compared with advertising; limited reach; heavy dependence on the salesperson’s skill; messages may become inconsistent across salespeople; difficult to supervise and control; and it is time-consuming to train and develop the sales force.

(b) Humor as an Advertising Appeal: humor grabs attention, improves recall, creates a positive mood and spreads virally - brands like Fevicol and Zomato use it well. Risks: humor can overshadow the product message, offend some audiences, wear out quickly on repeat exposure, and suit low-involvement products better than serious ones like insurance.

(c) Rebates and its characteristics: a rebate is a price reduction given after purchase, usually by mail-in proof of purchase. Characteristics: reward comes later, so it stimulates trial without instantly damaging the price image; redemption is often low; it builds purchase records; and it suits higher-value products.

(d) Owned media and its benefits: channels the company controls - website, blog, app, and social media pages. Benefits: free after setup, complete control over content, a long-term asset, direct engagement with customers, and it strengthens the impact of paid and earned media.

(e) Marketing Collateral and IMC: collateral is the supporting material of a campaign - brochures, catalogues, leaflets, presentations, sales kits and giveaways. Well-designed collateral reinforces the same message, logo and look at every touchpoint, so the IMC principle of consistency is carried right up to the customer’s hands.

Key points to remember: personal selling = costly but personal; humor = attention but wears out; rebates = post-purchase discount, protects price image; owned media = controlled, free channels; collateral = consistent IMC at every touchpoint.

Takeaway: Give each note a definition, an advantage, a limitation and an Indian example - Fevicol for humor, a brand website for owned media, brochures for collateral.

Q5. Case: Ather Energy 450x Gen 3 - (i) IMC programme (ii) print advertising campaign (iii) measuring advertising effectiveness.

Answer: For the Ather 450x Gen 3, an IMC programme targets urban youth with a premium-performance positioning delivered through digital media, PR, influencers, test rides and exchange offers; a print campaign pairs strategic media scheduling with a benefit-led creative; and effectiveness is measured with DAGMAR goals, recall tests, response tracking and sales comparison.

(i) IMC programme: Objective - build awareness and preference among urban youth aged 25-35 for the 450x Gen 3 and position it as India’s premium, tech-forward electric scooter. Message - performance (8.7 bhp motor, 146 km ARAI range), premium build and smart features. Mix - digital and social media campaigns, PR and influencer reviews, test-ride events at showrooms, sales promotion such as exchange offers and EMI schemes, dealer and sales-force support, and an app-driven engagement layer. Budget is allocated across these tools, and success is measured by awareness levels, test rides and conversion.

(ii) Print campaign: Strategic considerations - choose business dailies and auto magazines reaching young professionals, schedule around festive season, and differentiate from TVS iQube, Ola S1 and Bajaj Chetak with the premium-performance positioning. Creative considerations - a strong visual of the scooter, a short benefit-led headline (for example on power or range), Ather’s brand colours, a clear call to action to visit the showroom or website, and consistent branding across all print executions.

(iii) Measuring advertising effectiveness: set communication goals using the DAGMAR approach and check them with pre- and post-campaign awareness surveys; use recall and recognition tests on the print ads; track response through coupon codes or QR codes in the ad; monitor enquiries and test-ride bookings; and compare sales in campaign and non-campaign markets to isolate the campaign’s effect.

Key points to remember: IMC programme = objectives, audience, message, mix, budget, evaluation. Print campaign = strategic (media, timing) + creative (visual, headline, CTA). Effectiveness = DAGMAR goals, recall tests, response tracking, sales comparison.

Takeaway: Structure the case answer as three parts - IMC programme, print campaign (strategic + creative) and effectiveness measurement (DAGMAR, recall, response, sales) - always tied to the Ather case facts.
