Unit 1: HRD — An Overview

📖 Definition

HRD = systematic development of employees' knowledge, skills, abilities, and attitudes to improve individual and organisational performance. NOT just training — it's a holistic ecosystem covering appraisal, feedback, counselling, career planning, and rewards. Human capital = the most valuable and inimitable asset.

📖 Prof. T.V. Rao's Definition

"HRD is a set of systematic and planned activities designed by an organisation to provide its members with the necessary learning opportunities to improve their performance and enable the organisation to achieve its goals." — Prof. T.V. Rao (Father of HRD in India)

Kirkpatrick's 4 Levels of Training Evaluation:

LevelQuestionMethodTiming
1. ReactionDid they enjoy it?Feedback formsImmediately after
2. LearningDid they learn?Tests, demonstrationsEnd of training
3. BehaviourAre they using it?Observation, 360°3-6 months later
4. ResultsDid performance improve?KPIs, productivity data6-12 months later
📖 Kirkpatrick's Evaluation Pyramid

The evaluation builds upward — each level provides stronger evidence of training effectiveness:

Level 4 — Results: Did performance improve? (KPIs, productivity data, 6-12 months)
Level 3 — Behaviour: Are they using it? (Observation, 360-degree, 3-6 months)
Level 2 — Learning: Did they learn? (Tests, demonstrations, end of training)
Level 1 — Reaction: Did they enjoy it? (Feedback forms, immediately after)

Most organisations only measure Level 1 (happy sheets). Progressive firms measure up to Level 4.

💡 Real-World Example

Tatas use a modified Kirkpatrick model — they add Level 5 (ROI) to calculate the return on training investment. For example, after a leadership programme, they calculate (Benefits - Cost) / Cost x 100 to get training ROI percentage.

💡 Training Method Examples

On-the-Job at Indian Companies: Infosys uses job rotation across banking, healthcare, and retail verticals. Tata Steel runs 4-year apprenticeship programmes. Maruti Suzuki pairs new hires with experienced mentors through shadowing.

Off-the-Job at Indian Companies: L&T uses case study method at its management development centre. Infosys Global Education Centre in Mysuru trains 14,000 employees simultaneously. Role-playing is standard in sales training at FMCG companies like HUL and P&G.

🧠 Memory Aid

ADDIE = Analysis, Design, Develop, Implement, Evaluate
RLBR = Reaction, Learning, Behaviour, Results

Remember the pyramid: "Rats Like Big Rewards" — Reaction (base) → Learning → Behaviour → Results (top)

🎯 Exam Tip

Kirkpatrick's model is one of the most frequently tested frameworks. Always draw the 4-level pyramid and give examples for each level. Mention that Tatas add Level 5 (ROI) — this shows deeper knowledge.

⭐ Summary
  • Two training categories: on-the-job vs off-the-job
  • ADDIE: 5-step training design framework
  • Kirkpatrick: Reaction → Learning → Behaviour → Results
  • Tatas add Level 5 (ROI) to Kirkpatrick's model
  • Most firms only measure Level 1 — progressive ones go to Level 4

📖 Individual Human Capital Formula

KSAA = Knowledge + Skills + Abilities + Attitudes

Knowledge = what you know (facts, theories, procedures)
Skills = what you can do (practical application, learned through practice)
Abilities = what you're capable of (innate capacity, aptitude)
Attitudes = how you approach work (mindset, values, commitment)

Human capital is rare, inimitable, and portable — it's the most valuable asset an organisation possesses.

💡 Real-World Example

Google's "20% time" policy (now defunct but influential) allowed employees to spend 20% of work time on personal projects — a radical HRD approach that produced Gmail and Google News. Indian IT companies like Infosys and Wipro have similar innovation time programmes.

💡 Indian Example

Quality Circles at Indian Companies: Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited (BHEL) introduced quality circles in the 1980s — small groups of 5-12 workers meeting regularly to identify and solve work-related problems. This participative approach improved productivity by 15-20% and reduced defects significantly. Today, companies like Tata Steel and Maruti Suzuki continue this practice.

Think: "Know-Do-Capable-Approach" — the four pillars of human capital

🎯 Exam Tip

Connect KSAA to competency mapping (Unit 6) — they're conceptually linked. Also mention that human capital is the only resource that appreciates with use (unlike machines which depreciate).

⭐ Summary
  • KSAA = Knowledge + Skills + Abilities + Attitudes
  • Evolution: Traditional → Modern → Contemporary (Employee Experience)
  • HRD makes employees more capable, confident, and committed
  • Human capital appreciates with use — unlike physical assets
  • Quality circles and job enrichment are key HRD tools for employees

📖 HRD Climate Survey

Measures employee perceptions across all 5 elements using Likert-scale questionnaires. Results identify strengths and gaps in HRD delivery.

Impact chain: Positive climate → Higher motivation → Better performance → Lower attrition

Key dimensions measured:
- Availability and accessibility of HRD mechanisms
- Manager support for learning and development
- Quality and frequency of performance feedback
- Fairness of rewards and recognition
- Effectiveness of OD interventions

💡 Real-World Example

Google's HRD climate surveys consistently show high scores on "learning opportunities" and "manager support" — directly correlating with their #1 employer brand status. Indian companies like Infosys and TCS also conduct annual climate surveys to track employee engagement and HRD effectiveness.

💡 Indian Example

HRD Climate at L&T: Larsen & Toubro's HRD department, established in 1975 under the guidance of Prof. T.V. Rao and Udai Pareek, pioneered HRD climate research in India. They developed the HRD Climate Survey instrument that measures 6 dimensions: general HRD mechanisms, training climate, OD interventions, rewards, feedback quality, and career development opportunities. This instrument is now used by hundreds of Indian organisations.

🧠 Memory Aid

G-T-O-R-F = General mechanisms, Training climate, OD interventions, Rewards, Feedback

Climate vs Culture: "Climate is what you FEEL, Culture is what you DO"
Climate = observable, measurable, short-term, changeable ("How it feels here")
Culture = deep, invisible, long-term, stable ("How things are done here")

🎯 Exam Tip

Distinguish between climate (surveys can measure) and culture (deep values). This is a common exam trap. Always mention the climate → motivation → performance → attrition chain to show systemic thinking.

⭐ Summary
  • HRD climate = collective employee perception
  • 5 elements: G-T-O-R-F
  • Climate (observable) vs Culture (deep values)

Unit 9: Counselling, Coaching & Mentoring

📖 Three Helping Relationships

Three distinct approaches to employee development, each with different focus, timing, and methods.

Types of Counselling:

  • Directive: Counsellor tells employee what to do
  • Non-directive: Employee finds own solutions (counsellor listens)
  • Participative: Collaborative problem-solving

GROW Coaching Model:

StepQuestionPurpose
G — GoalWhat do you want to achieve?Define the destination
R — RealityWhere are you now?Assess current situation
O — OptionsWhat could you do?Explore possibilities
W — WillWhat will you do?Commit to action

Mentoring Details:

  • Formal: Organisationally assigned mentor-mentee pairs with structured timelines and goals
  • Informal: Natural development relationships that emerge organically
  • Reverse Mentoring: Junior teaches senior (e.g., digital skills, social media, new technology trends)
📖 Benefits of Mentoring

For the mentee: Knowledge transfer, career advancement, psychosocial support, networking, confidence building
For the mentor: Leadership development, fresh perspectives, personal satisfaction, legacy building
For the organisation: Talent retention, knowledge preservation, leadership pipeline, cultural integration

💡 Real-World Example

Wipro's formal mentoring programme pairs senior leaders with high-potential employees for 18-month development journeys, resulting in 40% faster promotions for mentees. They also practice reverse mentoring where juniors teach seniors digital skills. TCS has a similar programme called "Mentoring Circles" where groups of 8-10 mentees share a senior mentor.

💡 GROW Coaching Conversation Example

Scenario: A manager coaching a team member who wants to improve presentation skills

G (Goal): "What do you want to achieve?" → "I want to deliver confident client presentations"
R (Reality): "Where are you now?" → "I get nervous, speak too fast, avoid eye contact"
O (Options): "What could you do?" → "Practice with colleagues, join Toastmasters, record myself"
W (Will): "What will you do?" → "I'll practice twice this week and present at next team meeting"

🧠 Memory Aid

DNP = Directive, Non-directive, Participative (counselling types)
GROW = Goal, Reality, Options, Will

Counselling vs Coaching vs Mentoring:
Counselling = "Fix the problem" (past/present, therapeutic)
Coaching = "Improve the performance" (present, goal-driven)
Mentoring = "Grow the career" (future, development-driven)

🎯 Exam Tip

The GROW model is exam gold. Always draw the 4-step framework and give a coaching conversation example. Also mention reverse mentoring as a contemporary trend — examiners love it.

⭐ Summary
  • Counselling (problems) vs Coaching (performance) vs Mentoring (career)
  • 3 counselling types: Directive, Non-directive, Participative
  • GROW: Goal → Reality → Options → Will
  • Formal vs Informal vs Reverse mentoring
  • Mentoring benefits: mentee growth, mentor development, organisational retention

Unit 10: HRD in Industrial Relations

📖 Overview

HRD improves industrial relations by building trust, communication, and shared goals. When employees feel developed and valued, they're less likely to engage in conflict. HRD contributes to better IR through communication systems, grievance handling, participative management, worker education, and QWL programmes.

▶
Counselling Mentoring
▶
Coaching at Work

HRD Contributions to Better IR:

  1. Communication systems (transparent information flow)
  2. Grievance handling (fair resolution)
  3. Participative management (employee voice in decisions)
  4. Worker education (skilled, confident workforce)
  5. QWL programmes (Quality of Work Life)

QWL Programmes:

  • Flexible working hours — flextime, compressed work weeks
  • Job enrichment and enlargement — adding meaning and variety to work
  • Health and safety initiatives — on-site medical, safety training, ergonomic workstations
  • Employee assistance programmes (EAPs) — counselling, stress management, financial advice
  • Work-life balance policies — parental leave, childcare support, sabbaticals
💡 Real-World Example

Maruti Suzuki's QWL programme includes on-site medical facilities, recreational centres, and continuous skill upgradation — contributing to relatively stable IR despite being in the auto sector. Tata Steel's Jamshedpur township provides housing, schools, hospitals, and sports facilities — a comprehensive QWL ecosystem.

💡 Indian Example

Participative Management at Indian PSUs: Bharat Petroleum Corporation Limited (BPCL) implemented Joint Management Councils (JMCs) where worker representatives participate in operational decisions. This participative approach reduced grievances by 40% and improved productivity. Similar practices exist at SAIL and NTPC through Works Committees and Safety Committees.

🧠 Memory Aid

C-G-P-W-Q = Communication, Grievance handling, Participative management, Worker education, QWL

🎯 Exam Tip

"How does HRD contribute to better industrial relations?" appears frequently. Use the 5 points above.

⭐ Summary
  • HRD builds trust and communication for better IR
  • 5 contributions: Communication, Grievance, Participative, Worker education, QWL

⚡ Quick Revision Sheet

One-Liner Definitions:

  • HRD = systematic employee development for organisational goals
  • VUCA = Volatile, Unprecedented, Complex, Ambiguous
  • KSAA = Knowledge + Skills + Abilities + Attitudes
  • Competency = underlying characteristic leading to effective performance
  • HRD Climate = collective employee perception of HRD practices
  • Counselling = helping employees deal with personal/work problems
  • Coaching = performance improvement through guided practice
  • Mentoring = senior guides junior's career development
  • QWL = Quality of Work Life programmes
  • 360-degree = multi-source feedback from all directions
  • ADDIE = Analyse, Design, Develop, Implement, Evaluate (training design)
  • Kirkpatrick = 4-level training evaluation (Reaction, Learning, Behaviour, Results)
  • GROW = Goal, Reality, Options, Will (coaching framework)
  • Ulrich = 4 HR roles (Strategic Partner, Change Agent, Admin Expert, Employee Champion)
  • HRD Matrix = cross-reference of individual needs with organisational goals

🎯 Exam Strategy

For 10-mark questions: Write 2-3 pages. Include definition, framework, diagram, 2-3 Indian examples, and a conclusion.

Time allocation: Spend 2 minutes planning, 12 minutes writing, 1 minute reviewing. Always draw diagrams — they earn easy marks.

Common mistakes to avoid:
- Not giving Indian examples (lose 2-3 marks)
- Confusing climate with culture (common trap)
- Mixing up Kirkpatrick levels (draw the pyramid!)
- Not connecting to VUCA context (shows limited thinking)
