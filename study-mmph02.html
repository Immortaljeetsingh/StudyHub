Unit 1: HRD — An Overview

📖 Definition

HRD = systematic development of employees' knowledge, skills, abilities, and attitudes to improve individual and organisational performance. NOT just training — it's a holistic ecosystem covering appraisal, feedback, counselling, career planning, and rewards. Human capital = the most valuable and inimitable asset.

PeriodMovementKey FigureFocus
1920s-30sHuman RelationsElton Mayo (Hawthorne)Human element matters
1950s-60sHuman Behaviour at WorkMcGregor, MaslowMotivation, leadership
1970s-80sSeparate HRD DeptsRao & AbrahamDedicated HRD function
2000s+Digital HRDAI, AnalyticsData-driven development
#MechanismWhat It Does
1Performance AppraisalEvaluates current job performance
2Potential AppraisalAssesses future capability
3TrainingBuilds specific job skills
4Management DevelopmentGrows managerial capabilities
5Organisation DevelopmentImproves organisational effectiveness
6FeedbackProvides performance information
7CounsellingAddresses personal/work issues
8RewardsMotivates through recognition
9Employee WelfareEnsures well-being
💡 Real-World Example

An IT company in Bengaluru with 3000+ employees found 43% wanted WFH, 26% hybrid, 31% full-time office — requiring completely different HRD interventions for each group. Post-COVID reality: Work From Home, hybrid models, "third place" workstations have fundamentally changed HRD delivery.

🧠 Memory Aid

PPTM-OD-FCRW — Performance appraisal, Potential appraisal, Training, Management dev, OD, Feedback, Counselling, Rewards, Welfare (all 9 HRD mechanisms)

⭐ Summary
  • HRD integrates training, OD, and career development for effectiveness
  • L&T pioneered HRD practices in India (1975)
  • 9 HRD mechanisms from appraisal to welfare
  • VUCA world demands agile HRD responses

Unit 2: HRD Systems

📖 Definition

HRD System = integrated set of mechanisms working together for systematic people development. Not isolated programmes — they must connect to organisational strategy.

5 Sub-systems:

  • Career Planning System
  • Performance Management System
  • Training System
  • Organisation Development System
  • Quality of Work Life (QWL) System
📖 HRD Matrix

Cross-references individual development needs with organisational requirements. Rows = Individual competencies needed. Columns = Organisational goals. Cells = Specific HRD interventions.

Systematic Approach:

StepQuestionActivity
Need AssessmentWhat?Identify skill gaps
DesignHow?Plan the intervention
ImplementationDo it!Execute the programme
EvaluationDid it work?Measure outcomes
💡 Real-World Example

Infosys uses a competency-linked HRD matrix where every employee's development plan directly maps to business unit objectives.

🧠 Memory Aid

CPTQ-O — Career planning, Performance mgmt, Training, QWL, OD (5 HRD sub-systems)

⭐ Summary
  • 5 HRD sub-systems must work together
  • HRD Matrix links individual needs to organisational goals
  • Systematic approach: Assess → Design → Implement → Evaluate

Unit 3: HRD Processes & Methods

📖 Overview

Training methods fall into two categories: On-the-Job (learning by doing in real work environment) and Off-the-Job (classroom-based, simulated environment).

On-the-JobOff-the-Job
Job RotationLectures
ApprenticeshipCase Studies
Coaching/ShadowingRole Playing
UnderstudySimulation Games
Job EnrichmentSensitivity Training (T-groups)
In-basket exercisesManagement Games

ADDIE Model (Training Design Framework):

StepActivityKey Question
A — AnalyseNeeds assessmentWhat training is needed?
D — DesignObjectives, content, methodsHow to deliver?
D — DevelopCreate materialsBuild the content
I — ImplementDeliver the programmeRun the training
E — EvaluateMeasure effectivenessDid it work? (Kirkpatrick!)

Kirkpatrick's 4 Levels of Training Evaluation:

LevelQuestionMethodTiming
1. ReactionDid they enjoy it?Feedback formsImmediately after
2. LearningDid they learn?Tests, demonstrationsEnd of training
3. BehaviourAre they using it?Observation, 360°3-6 months later
4. ResultsDid performance improve?KPIs, productivity data6-12 months later
💡 Real-World Example

Tatas use a modified Kirkpatrick model — they add Level 5 (ROI) to calculate the return on training investment.

🧠 Memory Aid

ADDIE = Analysis, Design, Develop, Implement, Evaluate
RLBR = Reaction, Learning, Behaviour, Results

🎯 Exam Tip

Kirkpatrick's model is one of the most frequently tested frameworks. Always draw the 4-level pyramid and give examples for each level.

⭐ Summary
  • Two training categories: on-the-job vs off-the-job
  • ADDIE: 5-step training design framework
  • Kirkpatrick: Reaction → Learning → Behaviour → Results

Unit 4: HRD for Employees

📖 Definition

KSAA = Knowledge + Skills + Abilities + Attitudes — the components of individual human capital. HRD for employees focuses on making them more capable, confident, and committed.

HRD Department Functions for Employees:

  • Career planning and development
  • Performance management support
  • Training and development programmes
  • Counselling and mentoring
  • Quality circles (small groups solving work problems)
  • Job enrichment (adding responsibility/meaning)
PhaseFocus
TraditionalTraining only
ModernHolistic development (KSAA)
ContemporaryEmployee experience (EX)
💡 Real-World Example

Google's "20% time" policy (now defunct but influential) allowed employees to spend 20% of work time on personal projects — a radical HRD approach that produced Gmail and Google News.

🧠 Memory Aid

KSAA — Knowledge (what you know), Skills (what you can do), Abilities (what you're capable of), Attitudes (how you approach work)

🎯 Exam Tip

Connect KSAA to competency mapping (Unit 6) — they're conceptually linked.

⭐ Summary
  • KSAA = Knowledge + Skills + Abilities + Attitudes
  • Evolution: Traditional → Modern → Contemporary (Employee Experience)
  • HRD makes employees more capable, confident, and committed

Unit 6: Competency Mapping

📖 Definition

Competency: An underlying characteristic of a person that leads to effective and/or superior performance in a job — Boyatzis (1982)

TypeMeaningExample
ThresholdMinimum required to do the jobTyping speed for data entry
DifferentiatingDistinguishes superior from averageLeadership initiative

Competency Framework — 4 Categories:

CategoryExamples
TechnicalJob-specific knowledge & skills
BehaviouralCommunication, Teamwork, Ethics
ManagerialPlanning, Organising, Controlling
LeadershipVision, Inspiration, Change

Competency Mapping Steps:

  1. Identify roles and responsibilities
  2. Identify competencies required for each role
  3. Assess current competency levels (assessments, 360°)
  4. Identify gaps (required vs current)
  5. Develop action plan (training, coaching, job rotation)
💡 Real-World Example

L&T uses a competency dictionary with 52 competencies mapped to every role — from CEO to shop floor worker.

🧠 Memory Aid

TD = Threshold (minimum) vs Differentiating (superior)
TBML = Technical, Behavioural, Managerial, Leadership

🎯 Exam Tip

Competency mapping questions often ask you to "explain the steps" — memorise the 5-step process and give an industry example.

⭐ Summary
  • Threshold vs Differentiating competencies
  • 4-category framework: Technical, Behavioural, Managerial, Leadership
  • 5-step mapping process from identification to action plan

Unit 8: HRD Culture & Climate

📖 Definition

HRD Climate: The employees' collective perception of HRD practices, policies, and culture within the organisation

5 Elements of HRD Climate:

  1. General HRD mechanisms available (training, counselling, etc.)
  2. Training climate (learning culture, manager support)
  3. OD interventions (change programmes, team building)
  4. Rewards and recognition systems
  5. Performance feedback quality and frequency
ClimateCulture
Observable, measurableDeep, invisible
Short-term, changeableLong-term, stable
"How it feels here""How things are done here"
💡 Real-World Example

Google's HRD climate surveys consistently show high scores on "learning opportunities" and "manager support" — directly correlating with their #1 employer brand status.

🧠 Memory Aid

G-T-O-R-F = General mechanisms, Training climate, OD interventions, Rewards, Feedback

🎯 Exam Tip

Distinguish between climate (surveys can measure) and culture (deep values). This is a common exam trap.

⭐ Summary
  • HRD climate = collective employee perception
  • 5 elements: G-T-O-R-F
  • Climate (observable) vs Culture (deep values)

Unit 9: Counselling, Coaching & Mentoring

📖 Three Helping Relationships

Three distinct approaches to employee development, each with different focus, timing, and methods.

CounsellingCoachingMentoring
Dealing with problemsImproving performanceCareer development
Past/present focusedPresent focusedFuture focused
TherapeuticPerformance-drivenDevelopment-driven
Trained counsellorLine manager/coachSenior colleague

Types of Counselling:

  • Directive: Counsellor tells employee what to do
  • Non-directive: Employee finds own solutions (counsellor listens)
  • Participative: Collaborative problem-solving

GROW Coaching Model:

StepQuestionPurpose
G — GoalWhat do you want to achieve?Define the destination
R — RealityWhere are you now?Assess current situation
O — OptionsWhat could you do?Explore possibilities
W — WillWhat will you do?Commit to action
💡 Real-World Example

Wipro's formal mentoring programme pairs senior leaders with high-potential employees for 18-month development journeys, resulting in 40% faster promotions for mentees. They also practice reverse mentoring where juniors teach seniors digital skills.

🧠 Memory Aid

DNP = Directive, Non-directive, Participative (counselling types)
GROW = Goal, Reality, Options, Will

🎯 Exam Tip

The GROW model is exam gold. Always draw the 4-step framework and give a coaching conversation example.

⭐ Summary
  • Counselling (problems) vs Coaching (performance) vs Mentoring (career)
  • 3 counselling types: Directive, Non-directive, Participative
  • GROW: Goal → Reality → Options → Will

Unit 10: HRD in Industrial Relations

📖 Overview

HRD improves industrial relations by building trust, communication, and shared goals. When employees feel developed and valued, they're less likely to engage in conflict.

HRD Contributions to Better IR:

  1. Communication systems (transparent information flow)
  2. Grievance handling (fair resolution)
  3. Participative management (employee voice in decisions)
  4. Worker education (skilled, confident workforce)
  5. QWL programmes (Quality of Work Life)

QWL Programmes:

  • Flexible working hours
  • Job enrichment and enlargement
  • Health and safety initiatives
  • Employee assistance programmes
  • Work-life balance policies
💡 Real-World Example

Maruti Suzuki's QWL programme includes on-site medical facilities, recreational centres, and continuous skill upgradation — contributing to relatively stable IR despite being in the auto sector.

🧠 Memory Aid

C-G-P-W-Q = Communication, Grievance handling, Participative management, Worker education, QWL

🎯 Exam Tip

"How does HRD contribute to better industrial relations?" appears frequently. Use the 5 points above.

⭐ Summary
  • HRD builds trust and communication for better IR
  • 5 contributions: Communication, Grievance, Participative, Worker education, QWL
